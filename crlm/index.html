Clinical decisions

Liver-directed therapy

Open the liver-directed therapy matrix across all five tumor types →

Intent is assigned before a device is chosen. Curative intent means an attempt to eradicate all known cancer; it does not establish a cure rate. Local control, complete radiographic response, complete pathologic necrosis in a treated lesion, and overall survival are four different outcomes.

Choose treatment by its goal and suitability

Do not use a universal ablation → SBRT → Y-90 → TACE → HAI → histotripsy sequence. An upfront alternative chosen because of anatomy or organ reserve is not a post-progression second line. Bridge, downstaging and conversion preserve a later resection or transplant opportunity; conversion treatment is not itself the curative operation.

The tiers below are a clinical synthesis of treatment intent. They are not the roadmap's A–D evidence grading system, and the two should not be read as equivalent.

Scope and interpretation

Later-line efficacy does not define persistent unresectability. HPB MDT must document why complete clearance of every original disease site cannot be achieved with a credible resection/ablation strategy, liver reserve and staged options. Treated/nonviable lesions alone are not proof of active unresectable disease. Preserve all biology, extrahepatic and allocation gates.

Source history and review limitations

Status. Draft for multidisciplinary and institutional governance review. Not active clinical policy. It does not replace clinician judgement, current drug labelling, OPTN policy, or patient-specific informed consent. Every criterion drawn from OPTN policy, drug labelling or device authorisation must be re-verified against the current source at implementation and at each scheduled review. Clinical transplant candidacy and allocation eligibility are separate judgements.
