Mehta 2019: AFP response

Application. Supports response-based biological review and the existing >1000-to-<500 allocation framework. Does not require AFP <100 for every within-Milan candidate or prove a safe AFP-only exception.

Mehta 2019: AFP response · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Retrospective UNOS recipient cohort, 2005–2015; 407 patients with AFP >1000 at least once and final AFP within 90 days of LT. Living-donor and multiorgan recipients excluded; 93.6% initially within Milan.

Five-year post-LT OS: 48.8% with final AFP >1000, 67.0% at 101–499, and 88.4% at ≤100. Corresponding Kaplan–Meier recurrence estimates: 35.0%, 13.3%, and 7.2%. The 500–1000 group had only 17 patients. Differences between ≤100 and 101–499 were not statistically significant.

Recipient-only, selected historical cohort; treatment and waiting time differed substantially between groups. Non-HCC death was censored for recurrence. No proof that forcing AFP lower causes benefit, no AFP-only/imaging-complete-response subgroup, and no LDLT validation.

Main PDF pp 3–9; Tables 1–3 and Figures 1–3.

Goldman 2022: final AFP ≥500

Application. Supports discussing a conservative clinical hold at confirmed AFP ≥500, even without a prior >1000 result. This is an OPEN local proposal, not a universal OPTN contraindication or a proven transplant-benefit cutoff.

Goldman 2022: final AFP ≥500 · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

UNOS 2005–2015; 1766 LT recipients with initial AFP 100–999. Only 879 had serial values; final AFP ≥500 subgroup n=109. LDLT excluded.

Five-year OS at final AFP <100 / 100–499 / ≥500: 72.7% / 70.4% / 56.1%; recurrence: 13.1% / 18.7% / 24.2%. Final AFP ≥500: adjusted mortality HR 1.50 (95% CI 1.07–2.10), recurrence HR 1.88 (1.13–3.14).

Historical recipient-only study, not a comparison of transplant versus no transplant. Many had only one AFP. In the ≥500 subgroup, 40.4% were ≥1000; small subgroups cannot establish equivalence. Recurrence estimates censor competing death.

Main PDF pp 4–9; Tables 1–3 and Figures 1–3.

Giard 2018: AFP slope

Application. Use slope as an alert to reassess biology, imaging and inflammation, not a stand-alone absolute stop or permanent delisting rule.

Giard 2018: AFP slope · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Single-center 336 transplanted T2/Milan patients, 2003–2013; 98.2% received LRT. Median five AFP measurements over 9.8 months. Slope fitted to all values; >7.5 subgroup n=23.

AFP slope >7.5 ng/mL/month: adjusted recurrence HR 3.0 (1.1–8.1), microvascular invasion OR 6.8 (1.6–28.7). Five-year recurrence-free probability 69.8% versus 87.4%; survival without recurrence 59.5% versus 75.3%. These are different endpoints, neither pair should be labeled overall survival.

Small, data-selected cutoff group; no prospective hold/release validation. The long-term fitted slope cannot be inferred reliably from two closely spaced noisy results. Death was censored for recurrence-free probability.

Main PDF pp 2–6; Tables 1–4 and Figure 1.

Halazun 2021: dynamic AFP/NYCA

Application. Use dynamic AFP as complementary risk information. The NYCA definition of response does not supersede the stricter OPTN requirement to fall below 500 after AFP >1000.

Halazun 2021: dynamic AFP/NYCA · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

External retrospective validation in 2236 LT recipients at eight centers, 2001–2013; 545 beyond Milan. Composite score combines tumor size, count and AFP response. Deaths within 90 postoperative days excluded.

Five-year competing-risk cumulative recurrence: 9.5%, 20.5%, 40.5% for low/intermediate/high NYCA categories. Discrimination C statistic 0.66. These are composite-score categories, not isolated AFP ranges.

Recipient selection, exclusion of early deaths, no waitlist dropout or transplant-benefit comparison. Maximum-to-final AFP can miss a rebound below the original peak. Extreme-AFP response subgroup was tiny.

Main PDF pp 1–8; Table 1, Figures 1–3. Trailing commentary is not a separate reviewed study.

Moriyama 2026: markers before imaging recurrence

Application. Supports prompt repeat markers and high-quality/alternate imaging for an unexplained rise. Does not prove occult recurrence in every individual, justify empiric systemic/LRT treatment without a target, or validate an AFP-only transplant cutoff.

Moriyama 2026: markers before imaging recurrence · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Retrospective post-RFA, nontransplant cohort. Among 1620 initially treated patients, 907 episodes in 509 patients had elevated AFP, AFP-L3 and/or DCP without imaging evidence. Episodes are not independent patients and this is not AFP alone.

Recurrence was subsequently identified in 886 episodes; median detection interval 89 days. Local curative treatment 83.5% versus 78.8% and post-recurrence OS 3.55 versus 3.31 years for detection ≤180 versus >180 days; differences were not statistically significant.

RFA-specific, repeated episodes and competing losses; no LT cohort or tested optimal recall interval. Routine imaging is described as every four months in methods versus quarterly in abstract. Case-level AFP discrepancy between Figure 3 and Table 4 is not used.

All nine main-PDF pages; methods, Tables 2–4, Figures 1–3. External Tables S1/S2 not supplied.

SHARP: sorafenib

Application. Retain sorafenib as a guideline-supported TKI option when appropriate; not routine LT bridging or a way to treat an isolated AFP rise without a justified cancer indication.

SHARP: sorafenib · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Randomized double-blind phase III, 602 advanced HCC patients without prior systemic therapy; 97% Child-Pugh A, ECOG 0–2. Sorafenib 400 mg twice daily versus placebo.

Median OS 10.7 versus 7.9 months; HR 0.69 (0.55–0.87). Radiologic time to progression 5.5 versus 2.8 months, HR 0.58; this is not PFS. ORR 2% versus 1%, no complete responses.

Historical placebo comparison, not a comparison with current ICI combinations. Grade 3 diarrhea and hand–foot reaction each 8%; dose interruptions/reductions frequent. Little evidence for decompensated cirrhosis.

NEJM main PDF pp 1–13, especially efficacy and safety Tables 2–3.

ASCO BTC living guideline 2026.1.0

Application. Add biomarker-led branches: IDH1 ivosidenib; FGFR2 fusion/rearrangement pemigatinib/futibatinib; BRAF V600E dabrafenib/trametinib; HER2 IHC3+ zanidatamab/T-DXd; qualifying RET/NTRK/NRG1 options. Confirm current label and line. Negative plasma does not exclude a fusion; pursue tissue/RNA when feasible. Pre-LT ICI requires separate safety review.

ASCO BTC living guideline 2026.1.0 · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Full 56-page accepted, unedited living-guideline manuscript (October 2026). Advanced BTC systemic care, not pCCA transplant-neoadjuvant therapy or a transplant eligibility guideline.

Broad tissue DNA/RNA profiling, MMR and HER2 testing at diagnosis. For eligible MSS/pMMR disease: GemCis plus durvalumab or pembrolizumab (high-quality/strong); GemCis without ICI if unsuitable (conditional). After progression without an actionable target: FOLFOX plus active symptom control or active symptom control alone (conditional).

For oxaliplatin-ineligible patients, irinotecan/nal-IRI-based options have low-quality conditional support, not equal default status. Rare targets often rely on small basket cohorts. Accepted-manuscript tables/narrative contain inconsistent estimates; primary trial results govern this update.

All 56 pages; recommendations Table 1 pp 40–42; Figure 1 p 34 visually inspected. Separate data supplement not reviewed.

NALIRICC: negative BTC randomized trial

Application. Show NALIRICC alongside NIFTY. Do not call nal-IRI/5-FU an unqualified preferred second-line BTC regimen. Consider only a qualified alternative after target review, especially if oxaliplatin is unsuitable, with explicit benefit/toxicity discussion.

NALIRICC: negative BTC randomized trial · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

German randomized phase II, 100 patients (49 versus 51), ECOG 0–1, after gemcitabine-based therapy; 64% iCCA. nal-IRI plus 5-FU/LV versus 5-FU/LV.

PFS 2.6 versus 2.3 months, HR 0.87 (0.56–1.35); OS 6.9 versus 8.2 months, HR 1.08 (0.68–1.72). ORR 14% versus 4% did not translate into PFS/OS benefit. Grade ≥3 events 71% versus 50%; treatment-related serious events 33% versus 2%.

Small phase II trial; subsequent therapy differed (more control patients received later therapy). This does not erase the negative primary PFS result. Contrasts with positive NIFTY; population and treatment differences do not resolve the inconsistency.

Main PDF pp 1–11; Figure 2 and Tables 1–3.

MOUNTAINEER final: HER2-positive CRC

Application. Use local validated HER2 IHC3+, IHC2+/ISH+, or qualifying tissue amplification with RAS wild type and prior-treatment review. A negative plasma result may require tissue testing. Response can prompt HPB reassessment but does not establish persistent unresectability or transplant benefit.

MOUNTAINEER final: HER2-positive CRC · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Phase II open-label, noncomparative combination cohorts: 84 efficacy patients, 86 safety. HER2-positive/RAS-wild-type refractory CRC after fluoropyrimidine, oxaliplatin, irinotecan and anti-VEGF; no prior HER2 therapy. LVEF ≥50%.

Tucatinib plus trastuzumab: confirmed ORR 39.3% (28.8–50.5), median response duration 15.2 months, PFS 8.1 months, OS 23.9 months. Grade 3 and 4 events 33.7% and 7.0%; 5.8% discontinued for adverse events.

No randomized combination comparator or proven best sequence after other HER2 drugs. Do not rank its median OS against unrelated later-line trials. Exploratory HER2 copy-number thresholds are not mandatory selection cutoffs.

All 12 main-PDF pages; methods, efficacy Table 2, safety Table 4 and biomarker figures.

SUNLIGHT: FTD/TPI plus bevacizumab

Application. Offer a later-line disease-control option when marrow reserve and anti-VEGF safety permit. Record bleeding, wound-healing and operative timing; do not translate disease control automatically into LT eligibility.

SUNLIGHT: FTD/TPI plus bevacizumab · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Randomized phase III, 492 refractory mCRC patients, ECOG 0–1; 92% had two prior treatment regimens. FTD/TPI plus bevacizumab versus FTD/TPI alone.

OS 10.8 versus 7.5 months, HR 0.61 (0.49–0.77); PFS 5.6 versus 2.4 months, HR 0.44. ORR 6.1% versus 1.2%. Grade ≥3 events 72.4% versus 69.5%; neutropenia 43.1% versus 32.1%.

Open-label; no head-to-head comparison with fruquintinib/regorafenib. Low response rate and an advanced refractory population: not a preferred curative conversion regimen or LT trial.

All 11 main-PDF pages; Figures 1–2 and Tables 1–2.

FRESCO-2: fruquintinib

Application. Retain a later-line option with BP, proteinuria, hand–foot, bleeding/perforation and operative safety assessment. Not an evidence-based curative conversion strategy or new transplant-selection route.

FRESCO-2: fruquintinib · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Double-blind phase III, 691 refractory mCRC patients (461 versus 230); median four prior lines, prior FTD/TPI and/or regorafenib required; ECOG 0–1.

Fruquintinib versus placebo plus supportive care: OS 7.4 versus 4.8 months, HR 0.66 (0.55–0.80); PFS 3.7 versus 1.8, HR 0.32. Seven partial responses among 461; no complete response. Grade ≥3 events 63% versus 50%.

Very late-line population, no direct comparison with SUNLIGHT. Grade ≥3 hypertension 14%, asthenia 8%, hand–foot syndrome 6%; one treatment-related intestinal-perforation death.

All 13 main-PDF pages; Figures 2–3 and Tables 1–3.

CASSANDRA: preoperative PAXG

Application. Discuss PAXG as an evidence-supported preoperative option for selected patients, with local MDT/formulary adoption still OPEN. Not a blanket replacement for mFOLFIRINOX, not a metastatic comparison, and not a PDAC transplant indication.

CASSANDRA: preoperative PAXG · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Italian phase III, 260 eligible randomized patients, age 18–75, KPS ≥70, resectable/borderline PDAC (including biological borderline CA19-9 ≥500). PAXG versus mFOLFIRINOX; four preoperative months. Second timing randomization not reported in this analysis.

Median event-free survival 16.0 versus 10.2 months, HR 0.63 (0.47–0.84). EFS included confirmed CA19-9 rises and unresectability, not just progression/death. Resection 75% versus 67% (not significant); R0 rates similar. Grade ≥3 toxicity 66% versus 61%. OS immature; no established OS superiority.

Open-label, high-volume Italian centers, all White population, age cap. Converted phase II to III after 86% enrollment; no independent monitoring committee. Thirty-three EFS events were marker-only. Do not relabel EFS as OS or PFS.

All 12 main-PDF pages; endpoint definition, Figure 3 and Tables 1–3.

NAPOLI-1: nal-IRI after gemcitabine

Application. Use the combination after gemcitabine when fit; avoid treating nal-IRI as interchangeable with conventional irinotecan. Dose conventions differ: 70 mg/m² free base equals 80 mg/m² salt in the publication. Pharmacy must verify the actual product/order set.

NAPOLI-1: nal-IRI after gemcitabine · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Global randomized phase III, amended three-arm design. Primary combination comparison: 117 versus 119 concurrent controls. Metastatic PDAC after gemcitabine; KPS ≥70, normal bilirubin, albumin ≥30 g/L.

nal-IRI plus 5-FU/LV: OS 6.1 versus 4.2 months, HR 0.67 (0.49–0.92); PFS 3.1 versus 1.5, HR 0.56. ORR 16% versus 1%. nal-IRI monotherapy did not improve OS (HR 0.99). Grade 3–4 neutropenia 27%, diarrhea 13%.

Small prior-irinotecan subgroup, different 5-FU schedules, selected organ function. No validated immediate reuse after FOLFIRINOX/NALIRIFOX. One combination treatment-related septic-shock death.

All 13 main-PDF pages; Figures 1–3 and Tables 1–3.

Kobayashi 2018: combined HCC-CCA

Application. Supports cautious platinum-based treatment discussion and expert pathology/molecular review, not a definitive superiority claim or transplant-selection benefit. Adding ICI is extrapolation and requires LT-intent safety review.

Kobayashi 2018: combined HCC-CCA · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Retrospective 36 patients at 15 Japanese centers, 2002–2015, pathologically diagnosed unresectable/recurrent combined HCC-CCA. GemCis n=12, fluoropyrimidine/cisplatin n=11, sorafenib n=5, other n=8.

Overall OS 8.9 months, PFS 2.8 months, ORR 5.6%. Pooled platinum regimens had longer observed OS than sorafenib; adjusted sorafenib-versus-platinum mortality HR 15.83 (2.25–111.43). All seven sorafenib exposures including later line had progression.

Tiny nonrandomized groups, major confounding and very wide CI. Abstract and body reverse individual GemCis/5-FU-cisplatin median ordering; do not present those medians as settled. Old pathology classification; no modern ICI comparison.

All nine main-PDF pages; Table 2 and Figure 3 p 5 visually inspected; Table 3 p 6.
