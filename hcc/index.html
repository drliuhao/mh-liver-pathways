What we do, and how good the evidence is

The adult HCC roadmap as a working console. Pick a stage and the pathway answers with the standard, the reasonable alternative, what to avoid, and the grade behind each — every claim tied to the paper it came from. The liver-directed evidence matrix covers all four tumours.

Recommendation label — what we advise

StandardDefault when eligibility is met; departure needs a recorded reason.
PreferredFavoured only for the stated phenotype, not a general default.
ReasonableAcceptable after documented MDT review; benefit is context dependent.
Trial onlyProtocol or mandatory outcomes registry; effectiveness evidence is immature.
AvoidExpected harm, futility, or absent benefit generally outweighs benefit.

Evidence grade — how strong the basis is

AGuideline supported by randomised trials or strong consistent comparative evidence.
BOne randomised trial, prospective comparative evidence, or strong prospective cohorts.
CSingle-arm prospective, retrospective comparative, or inconsistent evidence.
DExpert consensus or local operational requirement, no comparative outcome data.
A strong label does not imply a high grade. Several operating requirements are deliberately Standard at grade D because they are local policy choices.

The decision tree

Three questions in order. Every yes settles the stage; every no moves to the next question. Performance status and spread are therefore decided before tumour burden is ever asked. Choose any answer or any stage to see the full recommendation.

HCC confirmed — LR-5 on multiphase CT or MRI in an at-risk liver; biopsy when imaging is indeterminate, decided at MDT. Stage with liver imaging and chest CT · ECOG · Child-Pugh and bilirubin · portal hypertension assessment · AFP. Nonresectability and transplant candidacy are assessed in parallel with the rest of staging, not after it.
Reference
Child-Pugh

Five parameters, 1–3 points each. Class A 5–6 · Class B 7–9 · Class C 10–15.

Class A5 points Preserved function. This does not establish portal pressure — see the CSPH gate.

In primary biliary cholangitis and primary sclerosing cholangitis the bilirubin bands are <4, 4–10 and >10 mg/dL. The roadmap also records ALBI and MELD 3.0; Child-Pugh alone does not drive allocation.

LI-RADS, in full

CT/MRI LI-RADS v2018 diagnostic algorithm and the v2024 treatment-response algorithms. Applies only in the at-risk population — cirrhosis of any cause, chronic hepatitis B, or current or prior HCC. OPTN class 5 criteria for listing are a separate system.

The four major features

Nonrim APHEUnequivocal enhancement in the arterial phase, in whole or in part, that is not rim-shaped.
Nonperipheral washoutVisually assessed reduction in enhancement relative to liver, in whole or in part, from an earlier to a later phase — not peripheral.
Enhancing capsuleSmooth, uniform, sharp border around most or all of the observation, unequivocally thicker or more conspicuous than around background nodules.
Threshold growth≥50% increase in size in ≤6 months.

Size is the fifth criterion and sets the row of the table; the three features other than APHE are what “additional major features” counts.

Applying the nonradiation algorithm after radioembolisation or SBRT is a recurring and consequential error in this pathway — it turns expected delayed necrosis into a false call of residual tumour.

Stage migration is permitted when anatomy, biology, liver function, transplant strategy, comorbidity, access or patient goals make the stage default unsuitable — the reason must be recorded. It is not a licence to substitute a lower-evidence modality for a proven curative option.

Liver-directed therapy

Intent is assigned before a device is chosen. Curative intent means an attempt to eradicate all known cancer; it does not establish a cure rate. Local control, complete radiographic response, complete pathologic necrosis in a treated lesion, and overall survival are four different outcomes.

There is no ladder

Do not use a universal ablation → SBRT → Y-90 → TACE → HAI → histotripsy sequence. An upfront alternative chosen because of anatomy or organ reserve is not a post-progression second line. Bridge and downstaging preserve a later transplant or resection opportunity; conversion treatment is not itself the curative operation.

Compare resection, local ablation and transplant where appropriate at multidisciplinary review, and record the intended outcome, why alternatives fit or do not fit, the systemic-treatment plan, the reassessment date and the next action. After progression or toxicity, restage and reconsider complete treatment rather than automatically repeating the same procedure.

Operating model, quality and approvals

Sections 9, 10 and 12 of the roadmap, plus the ratification list. These are stakeholder-derived operating requirements — evidence grade D by construction, local policy choices with no comparative outcome evidence. They are labelled Standard because the programme chose them, not because trials support them, and should be audited for adherence rather than effectiveness until the registry can support an effectiveness question.
