Resection recommendation, PDF p.18 · Source

OPTN 9.5.I

An indication that the candidate is not eligible for resection

OPTN 9.5.I.i; printed p.199 (18 November 2026 PDF) · Source

Proposed local approach

Use liver reserve, portal-hypertension severity, tumor location, operative extent and future remnant to compare resection with transplantation. Selected minor or minimally invasive resection may be appropriate after HPB and hepatology assessment; agree the local selection and risk-assessment criteria. Obtain invasive hemodynamics only when the result could change the operation.

Why this matters

Guideline-supported selection is not a choice between ignoring portal hypertension and forbidding every resection. Allocation documentation does not substitute for a surgical risk-benefit decision.

How should clinical selection and exception review differ for excess tumor burden, previously treated rupture and portal-branch invasion?

Pending multidisciplinary approval. Open this question in the pathway →

OPTN 9.5.I

not automatically eligible for a standardized MELD or PELD exception

OPTN 9.5.I.iii; printed p.200 (18 November 2026 PDF) · Source

NLRB · HCC

Guidance permits selected review after downstaging beyond standardized criteria. For previously treated rupture or primary portal-vein branch invasion, it describes a prolonged stable interval of at least 12 months before some cases may be considered.

· Source

Proposed local approach

Continue individual transplant-program review when standard criteria are not met; seek separate NLRB review if HCC MELD-exception points are requested. Record pretreatment burden, AFP, prior rupture or invasion, response and observation. For previously treated rupture, agree explicit clinical selection criteria: confirmation of rupture, peritoneal and distant staging, durable response, liver reserve, tumor biology, combined-risk exclusions and review ownership. No treated-rupture cohort is activated by this discussion.

Why this matters

NLRB guidance allows selected exception consideration after prolonged stability, including at least 12 months for previously treated rupture or primary portal-vein branch invasion. That interval is not a complete clinical transplant protocol or automatic eligibility. The initial portal-invasion pilot continues to exclude prior rupture; previously proven extrahepatic or nodal disease remains a separate HCC-B5 decision.

Complete pathologic necrosis 30/45 (67%); >400 Gy: 11/11 (100%). Selected surgical specimens after segmentectomy; no comparison of modalities or waitlist dropout.

HeiLivCa

50 waitlisted patients. TACE + sorafenib vs TACE + placebo. Progression occurred in 7 patients per arm. Time to progression HR 1.11 (95% CI 0.39–3.16). Severe treatment-related events occurred in 50% vs 16%. Small blinded trial. No demonstrated gain in time to transplant or tumor control.

Hoffmann et al. BMC Cancer. 2015. PMID 25957784. DOI 10.1186/s12885-015-1373-z. · Source

Kulik

Y90 plus sorafenib vs Y90 alone. Of 23 randomized, 20 received treatment (10 per arm). 8/10 vs 9/10 received a graft. Time to transplant did not differ significantly (P=0.35). Biliary complications within 30 days occurred in 4/8 vs 0/9 recipients (P=0.029). All 10 sorafenib patients required a permanent dose reduction. The pilot was too small to establish an effect on transplant receipt or survival.

Kulik et al. J Hepatol. 2014. PMID 24681342. DOI 10.1016/j.jhep.2014.03.023. · Source

PLENTY

22 beyond Milan. Pembrolizumab + lenvatinib vs locoregional treatment such as TACE. 10/11 per arm received a transplant. One-year conventional post-transplant recurrence-free survival was 70% vs 30%. Single-center pilot with frequent portal-vein tumor thrombus. The experimental regimen omitted concurrent LRT. An overall or waitlist survival benefit remains unproven.

Lv et al. Int J Surg. 2024. PMID 38995162. DOI 10.1097/js9.0000000000001813. · Source

Minoux

62 listed patients given sorafenib. No feasible LRT vs progression after LRT. Transplant receipt was 18/31 vs 8/31. Across both groups 36/62 dropped out. The two indications carry different prognoses. All received sorafenib and there was no LRT-only control.

Minoux et al. Transpl Int. 2022. PMID 36438781. DOI 10.3389/ti.2022.10569. · Source

VITALITY

117 ICI-treated patients assessed for transplant. 94% also received LRT. 65/86 who exceeded Milan downstaged. Overall 43/117 received a graft. Three-year dropout was 28% within vs 48% beyond Milan. The dropout comparison is by tumor burden. There was no LRT-only arm.

Tabrizian et al. J Hepatol. 2025. PMID 39255928. DOI 10.1016/j.jhep.2024.09.003. · Source

ImmunoXXL

61 potentially eligible patients started atezolizumab–bevacizumab. 16/61 received a graft. Of these, 15 had prior LRT and 4 experienced rejection. Two-year recipient overall survival was 94% and recurrence-free survival 90%. Single prospective cohort without a concurrent comparator. The survival estimates start at transplant.

Bhoori et al. J Hepatol. 2026. PMID 41763363. DOI 10.1016/j.jhep.2026.02.019. · Source

Abdelrahim

Houston Methodist. TACE + sorafenib vs TACE alone in a beyond-Milan subgroup of 12 vs 24 recipients. Mean explant necrosis was 69.6% vs 43.8% (P=.03). Five-year disease-free survival was 100% vs 67.2% (P=.07). Milan grouping incorporated explant findings. Recipient selection prevents estimation of waitlist dropout.

Abdelrahim et al. Cancers (Basel). 2022. PMID 35158918. DOI 10.3390/cancers14030650. · Source

Xu

TACE + a tyrosine kinase inhibitor vs TACE alone. Matched transplant recipients, 60 per arm. Objective imaging response was 33.3% vs 16.7% (P=.035). Recipient-only comparison. Imaging response cannot establish transplant access or survival from listing.

Xu et al. World J Surg. 2026. PMID 42421575. DOI 10.1002/wjs.70454. · Source

Arvind

38 retrospective studies of bridging LRT vs no bridging in HCC, focused on patients within Milan criteria (39,819 patients overall). Post-transplant recurrence-free survival HR 0.91 (95% CI 0.77–1.08) and overall survival HR 1.09 (95% CI 0.95–1.24). Five studies reported waitlist dropout (287 bridged, 166 unbridged) and found no significant difference. Selection and differences in waiting time limit inference.

Arvind et al. Liver Transpl. 2024. PMID 38466889. DOI 10.1097/lvt.0000000000000357. · Source

Chuncharunee

Seven observational studies of LRT bridging. For waits of at least six months, bridging was associated with lower post-transplant mortality (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.60–0.96). Observational subgroup evaluating local bridging. Systemic therapy was not the comparator.

Chuncharunee et al. JGH Open. 2024. PMID 38978769. DOI 10.1002/jgh3.13111. · Source

EASL–AASLD–ILCA

Consensus on HCC clinical trial design. Assess transplant receipt, progression or death before transplant, and survival from entry for all enrolled candidates. Recipient recurrence and pathologic response address separate outcomes.

Nat Rev Clin Oncol. 2026;23(8):619–647. Transplant trial design and intention-to-treat endpoints, pp. 634–635. · Source

Nanneboyina · ASCO 2025

Conference abstract. Among transplant recipients, systemic treatment + LRT (15) vs LRT alone (89) showed no significant difference in time to recurrence (P=.41). Dropout was unmeasured.

J Clin Oncol 2025, ASCO abstract 4135. · Source

Proposed local approach

Record the treatment goal, anatomical and liver-function considerations, and response-imaging date. Ask transplant radiology to resolve uncertain residual tumor before changing treatment. The four-week guidance applies to an individual NLRB request for a patient initially beyond standard downstaging entry criteria.

Why this matters

Choice and sequencing require a clinical decision; the timing guidance is not a modality algorithm. Use locoregional therapy when a patient within transplant criteria needs bridging during a prolonged wait and liver reserve and anatomy permit treatment. Adding systemic therapy has no demonstrated benefit for dropout, receipt of a transplant, or survival from listing.

Ong

208 matched recipients per arm. ICI-based multimodal therapy vs heterogeneous non-ICI therapy beyond Milan. Post-transplant overall survival HR 0.63 (95% CI 0.43–0.92) and recurrence-free survival HR 0.59. Ninety-day rejection was 16.3% vs 8.2%. Observational comparison restricted to recipients. Regimens differed within both groups.

JAMA Oncol. Published online October 1, 2026. Publisher PDF pp. E3–E7, patient flow, rejection and survival. · Source

Aceituno

48 ICI-exposed vs 144 matched non-ICI recipients. 142/144 controls had LRT. Rejection occurred in 18.8% vs 19.4% (P=.916). Eight exposed recipients received ATG induction vs none of the controls. Similar observed proportions do not establish equivalent risk or a preferred induction strategy.

Aceituno et al. Aliment Pharmacol Ther. 2026. PMID 41640254. DOI 10.1111/apt.70528. · Source

Rodrigues Ribeiro

14 reports with 412 reported recipients after systemic therapy. Up to 26 recipients potentially overlapped. Pooled three-year post-transplant overall survival was 76% and acute rejection 21%. Mixed regimens and possible overlap. These data cannot establish a comparative effect on dropout.

Rodrigues Ribeiro et al. J Gastrointest Cancer. 2026. PMID 41689676. DOI 10.1007/s12029-026-01399-w. · Source

Proposed local approach

Explicitly discuss the AASLD 3-month recommendation, agent/last dose, rejection risk and urgency. Any departure needs a documented multidisciplinary rationale; no washout guarantees safety.

Why this matters

Eligibility for HCC MELD-exception points and the risk of rejection after checkpoint-inhibitor exposure require separate assessments. Before the first checkpoint dose, agree the last-dose interval, management of an early deceased-donor offer and the plan for active immune toxicity. For atezolizumab–bevacizumab, record both last doses. A living-donor operation can be scheduled. AASLD advises at least three months after ICI. The proposed local elective interval is 90 days. Rejection can occur after longer intervals.

Proposed responsible team: Transplant oncology · pharmacy · transplant surgery
