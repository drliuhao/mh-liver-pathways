CRLM G1 — detailed web subquestion

Does a nonviable-appearing or disappearing liver lesion count as persistent disease?

Status: discussion required — not yet approved. Return to interactive pathway

Expert consensus

IHPBA · CRLM

“a complete response”

Short verbatim excerpt · Statement 24 includes complete response among favorable biology patterns

IHPBA · CRLM · full text
NLRB guidance

NLRB · CRLM

Guidance requires unresectability/recurrence context and durable control; it does not specify a universal minimum viable or RECIST-measurable lesion count.

Source summary / scope · December 2025 · pp.9–10, including timing/progression footnotes

NLRB · CRLM · full text
Proposed local approach

Map original and current sites, distinguish scar from suspected residual tumor, and assess whether all disease can be treated by resection or ablation. A residual scar or a negative PET alone should not determine transplant selection.

Rationale

Response, residual viability and technical unresectability answer different questions. This remains case-level judgment, not a new numerical eligibility rule.

NLRB · CRLM · full text
Expert consensus

IHPBA · CRLM

Statement 25 distinguishes rising versus falling CEA >80; this is not the same as the NLRB exclusion.

Source summary / scope · 2021 · resectability / selection pp.3–5; response assessment pp.7–8 (PDF pages)

IHPBA · CRLM · full text
Proposed local approach

Do not automatically qualify exact 80 or override an exclusion with a falling trend. Record clinical RECIST assessment separately from the NLRB guidance threshold and seek review for boundary cases.

Rationale

Where guidance and consensus differ, record which criterion applies to the MELD-exception request and which informs treatment.
