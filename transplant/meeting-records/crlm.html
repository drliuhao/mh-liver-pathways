Does a nonviable-appearing or disappearing liver lesion count as persistent disease?

Pending multidisciplinary approval. Open this question in the pathway →

IHPBA · CRLM

a complete response

Statement 24 includes complete response among favorable biology patterns · Source

NLRB · CRLM

Guidance requires unresectability/recurrence context and durable control; it does not specify a universal minimum viable or RECIST-measurable lesion count.

· Source

Proposed local approach

Map original and current sites, distinguish scar from suspected residual tumor, and assess whether all disease can be treated by resection or ablation. A residual scar or a negative PET alone should not determine transplant selection.

Why this matters

Response, residual viability and technical unresectability answer different questions. This remains case-level judgment, not a new numerical eligibility rule.

Proposed responsible team: HPB radiology · HPB/transplant surgery

NLRB · CRLM

The progression footnote uses >10% diameter increase or any new lesion, while calling it RECIST; usual RECIST 1.1 progression is not this threshold.

· Source

IHPBA · CRLM

Statement 25 distinguishes rising versus falling CEA >80; this is not the same as the NLRB exclusion.

· Source

Applicable guidance

Apply the request-specific CEA criterion: initial exclusion >80 µg/L; extensions <80 µg/L. Record the NLRB progression criterion (>10% diameter increase or new lesions) separately from the clinical RECIST 1.1 assessment.

Why this matters

Where guidance and consensus differ, record which criterion applies to the MELD-exception request and which informs treatment.

Proposed local approach

Use ctDNA as additional prognostic/research information only. Coordinate the PET interval safely; seek interpretation rather than silently omit a specified test.

Why this matters

An investigational biomarker is not a validated replacement for the staging pathway.
