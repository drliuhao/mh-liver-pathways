Mehta 2019: AFP response

Application. Supports response-based biological review and the existing >1000-to-<500 allocation framework. Does not require AFP <100 for every within-Milan candidate or prove a safe AFP-only exception.

Mehta 2019: AFP response · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Retrospective UNOS recipient cohort, 2005–2015; 407 patients with AFP >1000 at least once and final AFP within 90 days of LT. Living-donor and multiorgan recipients excluded; 93.6% initially within Milan.

Five-year post-LT OS: 48.8% with final AFP >1000, 67.0% at 101–499, and 88.4% at ≤100. Corresponding Kaplan–Meier recurrence estimates: 35.0%, 13.3%, and 7.2%. The 500–1000 group had only 17 patients. Differences between ≤100 and 101–499 were not statistically significant.

Recipient-only, selected historical cohort; treatment and waiting time differed substantially between groups. Non-HCC death was censored for recurrence. No proof that forcing AFP lower causes benefit, no AFP-only/imaging-complete-response subgroup, and no LDLT validation.

Main PDF pp 3–9; Tables 1–3 and Figures 1–3.

Goldman 2022: final AFP ≥500

Application. Supports discussing a conservative clinical hold at confirmed AFP ≥500, even without a prior >1000 result. This is an OPEN local proposal, not a universal OPTN contraindication or a proven transplant-benefit cutoff.

Goldman 2022: final AFP ≥500 · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

UNOS 2005–2015; 1766 LT recipients with initial AFP 100–999. Only 879 had serial values; final AFP ≥500 subgroup n=109. LDLT excluded.

Five-year OS at final AFP <100 / 100–499 / ≥500: 72.7% / 70.4% / 56.1%; recurrence: 13.1% / 18.7% / 24.2%. Final AFP ≥500: adjusted mortality HR 1.50 (95% CI 1.07–2.10), recurrence HR 1.88 (1.13–3.14).

Historical recipient-only study, not a comparison of transplant versus no transplant. Many had only one AFP. In the ≥500 subgroup, 40.4% were ≥1000; small subgroups cannot establish equivalence. Recurrence estimates censor competing death.

Main PDF pp 4–9; Tables 1–3 and Figures 1–3.

Giard 2018: AFP slope

Application. Use slope as an alert to reassess biology, imaging and inflammation, not a stand-alone absolute stop or permanent delisting rule.

Giard 2018: AFP slope · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Single-center 336 transplanted T2/Milan patients, 2003–2013; 98.2% received LRT. Median five AFP measurements over 9.8 months. Slope fitted to all values; >7.5 subgroup n=23.

AFP slope >7.5 ng/mL/month: adjusted recurrence HR 3.0 (1.1–8.1), microvascular invasion OR 6.8 (1.6–28.7). Five-year recurrence-free probability 69.8% versus 87.4%; survival without recurrence 59.5% versus 75.3%. These are different endpoints, neither pair should be labeled overall survival.

Small, data-selected cutoff group; no prospective hold/release validation. The long-term fitted slope cannot be inferred reliably from two closely spaced noisy results. Death was censored for recurrence-free probability.

Main PDF pp 2–6; Tables 1–4 and Figure 1.

Halazun 2021: dynamic AFP/NYCA

Application. Use dynamic AFP as complementary risk information. The NYCA definition of response does not supersede the stricter OPTN requirement to fall below 500 after AFP >1000.

Halazun 2021: dynamic AFP/NYCA · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

External retrospective validation in 2236 LT recipients at eight centers, 2001–2013; 545 beyond Milan. Composite score combines tumor size, count and AFP response. Deaths within 90 postoperative days excluded.

Five-year competing-risk cumulative recurrence: 9.5%, 20.5%, 40.5% for low/intermediate/high NYCA categories. Discrimination C statistic 0.66. These are composite-score categories, not isolated AFP ranges.

Recipient selection, exclusion of early deaths, no waitlist dropout or transplant-benefit comparison. Maximum-to-final AFP can miss a rebound below the original peak. Extreme-AFP response subgroup was tiny.

Main PDF pp 1–8; Table 1, Figures 1–3. Trailing commentary is not a separate reviewed study.

Moriyama 2026: markers before imaging recurrence

Application. Supports prompt repeat markers and high-quality/alternate imaging for an unexplained rise. Does not prove occult recurrence in every individual, justify empiric systemic/LRT treatment without a target, or validate an AFP-only transplant cutoff.

Moriyama 2026: markers before imaging recurrence · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Retrospective post-RFA, nontransplant cohort. Among 1620 initially treated patients, 907 episodes in 509 patients had elevated AFP, AFP-L3 and/or DCP without imaging evidence. Episodes are not independent patients and this is not AFP alone.

Recurrence was subsequently identified in 886 episodes; median detection interval 89 days. Local curative treatment 83.5% versus 78.8% and post-recurrence OS 3.55 versus 3.31 years for detection ≤180 versus >180 days; differences were not statistically significant.

RFA-specific, repeated episodes and competing losses; no LT cohort or tested optimal recall interval. Routine imaging is described as every four months in methods versus quarterly in abstract. Case-level AFP discrepancy between Figure 3 and Table 4 is not used.

All nine main-PDF pages; methods, Tables 2–4, Figures 1–3. External Tables S1/S2 not supplied.
