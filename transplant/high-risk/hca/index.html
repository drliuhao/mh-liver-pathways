Which NLRB recommendation applies?

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Document at least one actual guidance route: (1) adenoma with GSD or Abernethy; OR (2) unresectable β-catenin-mutated adenoma; OR (3) unresectable adenoma with adenomatosis >10 lesions (at least 11); OR (4) unresectable AND unresponsive to nonoperative therapy AND progressive or complicated disease.
  • Do not require all four routes, and do not impose cirrhosis as a national prerequisite. Conversely, meeting one guidance route does not itself justify surgery.
  • Require specialist pathology/radiology and an alternatives review before requesting an exception; proven malignant transformation must be evaluated under the appropriate cancer pathway.
Tests and information to review

Subtype/β-catenin result where needed; lesion count and distribution; GSD/shunt status; resectability; prior nonoperative treatment and complication record. Record the exact lesion count and, when available, the CTNNB1 exon/activation phenotype; exactly ten is not >10.

Allocation and donor route

NLRB wording concerns exception review and remains nonbinding. Submission, eligibility and approval are distinct.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

Allocation-route eligibility and clinical necessity must be recorded separately. Lesion number, size and molecular subtype identify different questions. The NLRB four-route OR structure is more permissive than older clinical LT guidance; clinical risk still needs confirmation and alternatives review.

Evidence against expansion

Adenomatosis is ≥10 in ACG terminology but >10 in the cited NLRB route: exactly ten lesions cannot be silently rounded into >10. Molecular subtypes are not biologically interchangeable. Selected-registry survival does not quantify the benefit of replacing an otherwise functioning liver.

Certainty

High confidence in the meaning of the current guidance text; low certainty for phenotype-specific LT benefit; moderate biological evidence for subtype heterogeneity, not a validated LT threshold.

Basis for proposed thresholds

  • >10: literal allocation-guidance wording (at least 11); different from ACG’s diagnostic definition.
  • β-catenin: guidance category; genotype/phenotype should inform clinical risk, without silently rewriting the allocation category.
  • Need for LT: multidisciplinary clinical decision beyond exception eligibility.

NLRB oncology guidance

Design and population. Current NLRB hepatic-adenoma guidance.

Selection. Four alternative routes; route four contains conjunctive clinical requirements.

Results. GSD/Abernethy; OR unresectable β-catenin-mutated HCA; OR unresectable adenomatosis >10 lesions; OR unresectable, nonoperative-unresponsive and progressive/complicated HCA.

Limits. These are review routes, not proof of benefit or a mandatory transplant recommendation. Current text does not require all four conditions simultaneously or cirrhosis for all routes.

ACG 2024

Design and population. ACG 2024, HCA recommendations/key concepts 11–18.

Selection. Risk-stratified management and explicit alternative approaches to resection.

Results. Stable women with lesions ≥5 cm: risk-factor change and 6–12-month observation, resection if not <5 cm. Men/β-catenin lesions warrant earlier definitive review; LT may be considered in selected unresectable/complicated disease.

Limits. Six versus 6–12 months is a real timing nuance. ACG adenomatosis definition is ≥10, whereas current NLRB route says >10; definitions cannot silently replace each other.

European Association for the Study of the Liver 2016

Design and population. EASL 2016 benign liver tumor guideline.

Selection. Women after risk-factor modification versus men/β-catenin disease; multifocal disease addressed separately.

Results. Six-month observation in suitable women; ≥5 cm or growth prompts resection. LT is not routine for multiple adenomas; underlying liver disease can alter consideration.

Limits. Older clinical guidance is narrower than current NLRB alternative exception routes. A resection threshold is not an LT threshold.

Nault 2017

Design and population. Molecular study: 533 adenomas from 411 patients across 28 centers.

Selection. Tissue-based classification, predominantly women; not a prospective population natural-history sample.

Results. Symptomatic bleeding in 14% of patients; 3% developed HCC. Strong exon-3 CTNNB1 activation tracks malignant risk; exon-7/8 activation is biologically different; sonic-hedgehog subtype associates with bleeding.

Limits. Observed prevalence is not individual lifetime risk. Molecular risk supports definitive-treatment assessment, not automatic LT or equivalence of all β-catenin variants.

Ziogas 2022

Design and population. UNOS 1987–2020: 199 listed; 142 transplanted, including 118 listed for HCA and 24 incidental diagnoses.

Selection. Mixed adults/children; 32/142 children; 89.4% had no HCC; size/count unavailable.

Results. Crude waitlist mortality 9.0%. Post-LT 1/3/5-year OS 94.2%/89.7%/86.3%; 5-year CI 78.5–91.5%; median follow-up 62.9 months.

Limits. No contemporary non-LT comparator; registry indication misclassification. Separate systematic-review patients must not be pooled with UNOS or Chiche as independent evidence.

Is transplantation clinically justified?

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • For suitable stable women, reassess after six months of hormone/lifestyle optimization; consider continued observation toward twelve months if the lesion is regressing and delay is clinically safe. A lesion ≥5 cm or continued growth triggers resection/other definitive-treatment review, not automatic LT.
  • Do not impose that waiting period on hemorrhage, high-risk male/β-catenin disease or confirmed malignant transformation when earlier treatment is appropriate.
  • Proposed transplant requirements: two independent specialist reviews confirm that resection is not feasible and nonoperative options are unsuitable or have failed, plus a documented clinical-benefit basis: progressive/complicated disease, underlying liver-disease risk, or a high-risk molecular/malignant-transformation phenotype. Stable unresectable high-risk exon-3 disease is not automatically excluded. Pure diagnostic anxiety or an allocation route without a defined benefit is insufficient.
Tests and information to review

Subtype and malignancy review; sex/hormonal history; serial size; complications; embolization/resection feasibility; hepatic reserve; documented expected benefit and alternatives.

Allocation and donor route

An exception is an allocation mechanism, not proof that expected benefit exceeds donor/graft risk.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

LT is feasible in carefully selected adenoma patients, but the strongest comparative gap is benefit versus less invasive care. Overdiagnosed malignant transformation, real perioperative/retransplant burden and frequent regression after hormone withdrawal argue against transplantation for concern alone.

Evidence against expansion

Some patients have unresectable β-catenin lesions, underlying metabolic/vascular disease, recurrent hemorrhage or uncontrolled progression. For them, waiting or repeated local therapy may be harmful. A six-month reassessment is not a reason to postpone urgent definitive treatment or automatically transplant a stable lesion.

Certainty

Low certainty for selected LT prognosis; very low for comparative LT benefit. Nonrandomized alternative-treatment data support individualized sequencing, not a universal delay.

Basis for proposed thresholds

  • Six months: EASL reassessment point; ACG allows 6–12 months in selected women. Not a mandatory waiting period for men, hemorrhage or malignant transformation.
  • ≥5 cm/growth: trigger for definitive-treatment/resection assessment, not automatic LT.
  • Two independent reviews: local anti-overtreatment safeguard, not a trial-validated number.

Chiche 2016

Design and population. European retrospective adenomatosis LT series, n=49, 1986–2013.

Selection. LT for proven HCC 16, suspected HCC 15, progression 8, hemorrhage 5, symptoms 5.

Results. Only 1/15 suspected-HCC cases had HCC confirmed. Three deaths within 90 days; five retransplants. HCC recurrence in 3/17 with malignant explants, all fatal; 41/49 alive and well at final report.

Limits. Selected surgical series, not untreated transformation risk; overlaps European cases in later reviews. Low diagnostic yield warns against using suspicion alone.

Haring 2019

Design and population. Retrospective hormone-withdrawal cohort, n=78.

Selection. Stopped oral contraception; RECIST diameter response of largest HCA.

Results. At median 1.3 years after withdrawal, regression >30% 37.2%, complete regression 5.1%, stable 56.4%, progression 1.3%; no HCA-related complications reported.

Limits. Primary abstract/repository extraction; not a transplant comparison. Follow-up exceeds six months, so it does not prove that six months is a biological deadline.

van Rosmalen 2019

Design and population. Six-center retrospective embolization cohort, n=59; 23 bleeding and 36 nonbleeding.

Selection. Median lesion 76 mm; 57/59 women.

Results. Six major complications (10%); median diameter 48 mm at median 5.5-month imaging. One death at three months after emergency embolization with hemorrhagic shock; no clinically overt rebleeding of treated lesions.

Limits. No randomized comparator; embolization is neither harmless nor proven to eliminate malignant potential. Requires lesion-specific technical and pathology assessment.
