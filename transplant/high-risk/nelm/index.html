NET-B1High-risk protocol · Benefit review

Does transplantation offer enough additional benefit?

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Proposed requirements: favorable-biology unresectable liver disease with controlled primary, plus at least one trigger—refractory functioning syndrome, clinically meaningful liver-related functional compromise, or a prospectively documented credible threat to durable hepatic control despite reasonable alternatives. Do not require established irreversible failure before assessing benefit.
  • NET oncology and an independent transplant/HPB reviewer must agree that LT offers meaningful additional survival or symptom/function benefit over the patient’s accessible options, with B2 biological stability satisfied.
  • Asymptomatic stable low-burden disease with preserved hepatic function and reasonable durable options defaults to continued non-LT care/observation. Evaluate at least one appropriate treatment strategy; do not require two failures or exhaustion of every therapy. Do not use ENETS illustrative <30%/≥70% figures as a validated calculator.
Tests and information to review

Functional syndrome and treatment response; symptoms/liver reserve; growth trajectory; SSTR status and treatment alternatives; patient goals; independent benefit review.

Allocation and donor route

Favorable clinical prognosis does not establish urgency, exception entitlement or a reason to expose a healthy donor.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

Selected NET recipients can have excellent long-term survival, and comparative cohorts suggest potential benefit, especially over a decade. However, good post-LT survival is not synonymous with large incremental benefit: non-LT eligible patients can also live many years. The best Milan comparison adjusted delayed LT entry but remains confounded and predates most modern sequencing. The multicentre main-body matched OS difference is smaller than its abstract suggests. A credible benefit assessment must include both long-term hepatic threat and competing treatment/transplant harms.

Evidence against expansion

Kjaer's eligibility-based non-LT cohort had substantial long-term survival; CLARINET supports prolonged control for indolent Ki-67 <10% disease. CABINET and PRRT offer additional disease-control options, but none is a randomized comparator to LT. Conversely, requiring every systemic option to fail may forfeit the transplant window and is not tested. Maspero documents late multisite recurrence and non-cancer deaths despite excellent LT survival.

Certainty

Low certainty for a causal LT benefit magnitude and any individual benefit cutoff; moderate certainty that NET biology and modern alternatives vary greatly. High-quality systemic-trial efficacy is indirect for transplant selection. No validated calculator supports the ENETS illustrative 30%/70% survival targets.

Basis for proposed thresholds

No randomized study validates a minimum survival-gain percentage, two-line failure count, or ENETS <30% versus ≥70% prediction. Six-month observation screens biology but does not prove added benefit. Any local two-reviewer requirement and reassessment cadence are governance, not biological cutoffs.

Mazzaferro 2016 · Milan-NET

Design and population. Single-centre comparative Milan-NET study, 88 eligible patients: 42 LT and 46 non-LT, 1995–2010 era, nonrandomized.

Selection. Well-differentiated portal-drained GEP primary, primary/extrahepatic disease removed, limited hepatic burden, at least six months stability; age <60 was relative. LT recipients were substantially younger (median 40.5 versus 55.5), and post hoc grading differed, including four G3 in non-LT versus none in LT. Primary regional nodes had been positive in 34/42 LT recipients; this is not evidence supporting active uncleared extrahepatic disease at LT.

Results. Median follow-up 122 months. Five-/ten-year OS 97.2%/88.8% LT versus 50.9%/22.4% non-LT. Time origin was primary-tumour resection; authors used left-truncated Cox models to account for delayed LT entry. Propensity-adjusted restricted mean survival gain was 6.82 months at five years and 38.43 months at ten years. These are cohort estimates, not individual calculators.

Limits. Major selection/age/treatment-era differences, small numbers and residual confounding remain despite statistical handling of delayed treatment. Only 4/46 non-LT patients received PRRT, so this is not a modern sequential-care comparator. Patients overlap subsequent Milan series and some multicentre LT studies; do not sum them as independent evidence.

Eshmuminov 2023 · LT versus resection

Design and population. International retrospective comparison from 15 centres, 1988–2021: 455 patients (225 LT, 230 liver resection); median follow-up 97 months. Propensity matching produced 96 patients per group.

Selection. Surgically selected patients, matched on age, grade and primary location. Twenty-seven percent of LT cases had unknown grade; burden, era and treatment history remain imperfectly controlled. Resection and LT do not necessarily address anatomically interchangeable patients.

Results. Use main Results p.e1067/Table 4: matched five-year OS 75% LT versus 68.3% LR, HR 0.56 (95% CI 0.35–0.90); five-year PFS 64.2% versus 14.2%, HR 0.25 (0.16–0.39), measured from operation. Ninety-day deaths 13/225 LT (5.8%) versus 3/230 LR (1.3%). LT G1 versus G2 five-year OS 92.9% versus 70%; adjusted G2 HR 2.52 (1.15–5.52). Outside-Milan matched OS HR 0.64 (0.31–1.33), not statistically conclusive.

Limits. Material abstract/body discrepancy: abstract reports matched five-year OS 73% versus 52.8%, whereas main Results/Table 4 report 75% versus 68.3%. The latter is used with explicit discrepancy flag; clarification would be desirable. Matching does not remove unmeasured confounding, missing pathology, era or selection bias. The nonsignificant outside-Milan estimate does not prove no benefit, and ten G3 cases do not validate G3 LT. Overlaps centre cohorts including Milan.

Maspero 2022 · Milan long-term

Design and population. Retrospective Milan single-centre series, 1984–2019: 149 liver operations (53 LT, 96 LR), with 104 Milan-eligible cases analysed (48 LT, 56 LR).

Selection. Portal-drained G1/G2 primary previously resected, no extrahepatic disease, <50% hepatic involvement, at least six months stable disease, age <60 relative. LT and resection had different resectability/age indications. Minimum six-month follow-up inclusion needs care when interpreting early risk; this is not an all-referral cohort.

Results. Time zero liver operation; median follow-up 158 months LT and 126 LR. Five-year OS 95.5% versus 90%; ten-year OS 93% versus 75%. Ten-year DFS 52% versus 18%; recurrence 25/48 versus 42/56 and median recurrence time 78 versus 24 months. LT recurrence was more often multisite; LR recurrence predominantly liver. Nine LT deaths included three cancer and six non-cancer deaths, including new malignancy, sepsis/lymphoproliferative and cardiovascular causes.

Limits. Not randomized or anatomically exchangeable; prolonged selected follow-up and differing age can favor LT. Strong overlap with 2016 Milan-NET makes it long-term/alternative-comparator information, not independent confirmation. Late recurrence and immunosuppression-related competing causes matter even with excellent survival.

Kjaer 2023 · eligible non-LT panNET

Design and population. Single-centre retrospective non-LT panNET cohort, 2000–2021: 519 screened, 41 G1/G2 metastatic patients included; 16 met at least one historical LT framework (Milan 11, ENETS 8, UNOS 13, overlapping).

Selection. Resected primary, liver metastases, no extra-abdominal disease, age ≤75 and no G3. Definitions modified historical criteria, including Milan age <65 and two 'UNOS' patients with stable regional nodes, so not all meet today's NLRB requirements. Multimodal care included five liver resections, 15 ablations, six embolizations and later-line PRRT, not purely medical therapy.

Results. Time zero was date LT criteria fulfilled (for remaining patients, initial institutional visit after primary resection with liver metastases). Mean follow-up 5.4 years. Overall 41-patient median OS 9.3 years; five-year OS 64.7% (95% CI 48.2–81.2). Milan subset five-year OS 64.9% (32.2–97.6), ENETS 85.7% (59.8–100), historical UNOS 55.4% (26.0–84.8).

Limits. Small retrospective selected subgroups with very wide intervals; no internal LT comparator and treatment-era limitations. It challenges the assumption that eligible stage IV NET has uniformly poor non-LT survival; it cannot prove that non-LT is superior to LT. Avoid comparing its eligibility-based survival directly with post-LT curves as if randomized.

Chan 2025 · CABINET

Design and population. CABINET double-blind phase III randomized trial, 298 previously treated progressive well-differentiated NET patients: independent extrapancreatic cohort n=203 and pancreatic cohort n=95, 2:1 cabozantinib versus placebo.

Selection. Prior PRRT and/or targeted therapy; advanced progressive disease, not specifically liver-only Milan-eligible transplant candidates. Different disease strata and prior-treatment burden preclude direct comparison with LT series.

Results. Time zero randomization. Median PFS 8.4 versus 3.9 months in extrapancreatic NET (HR 0.38, 95% CI 0.25–0.59), 13.8 versus 4.4 months in panNET (HR 0.23, 0.12–0.42). Objective response 5% and 19% in respective cabozantinib cohorts versus 0% placebo. Grade ≥3 adverse events approximately 62–65% versus 23–27%. The primary report does not establish an LT-comparable OS benefit.

Limits. High-quality evidence for this systemic-treatment setting, indirect for transplant benefit. A treatment's availability does not mean every candidate must receive it before LT, and its toxicities/tempo must enter individualized comparison. PFS is not OS and placebo control does not represent an entire modern lifetime sequence.

Caplin 2014 · CLARINET

Design and population. CLARINET randomized double-blind trial, 204 nonfunctioning advanced enteropancreatic NET patients (101 lanreotide, 103 placebo).

Selection. SSTR-positive G1/G2 with Ki-67 <10%; 96% had stable disease over the prior 3–6 months; one-third had hepatic burden >25%. It represents indolent disease biology, not all progressive transplant candidates.

Results. From randomization, median PFS not reached with lanreotide versus 18 months placebo; HR 0.47 (95% CI 0.30–0.73). Twenty-four-month PFS 65.1% versus 33.0%. No significant OS or QoL difference in the primary report. Diarrhea 26% versus 9%.

Limits. Shows that stability and substantial survival without LT are plausible; does not estimate the added benefit of transplant or support treating all stable low-burden NET. G2 up to 20% and G3 are not represented by this <10% trial threshold.

Strosberg 2021 · NETTER-1 final OS

Design and population. NETTER-1 phase III randomized trial final OS follow-up: 360 assessed, 231 randomized (117 Lu-177-Dotatate, 114 high-dose octreotide), 223 treated in safety population.

Selection. Progressive, well-differentiated G1/G2 SSTR-positive midgut NET on somatostatin analogue; not a liver-only transplant comparison. Corrected publisher version dated January 2022 reviewed.

Results. Time zero randomization; median follow-up about 76 months. Median OS 48.0 versus 36.3 months, HR 0.84 (95% CI 0.60–1.17), P=0.30; primary final OS endpoint not met. Forty-one of 114 controls (36%) crossed to PRRT. Two of 111 Lu-treated safety patients developed myelodysplastic syndrome, one fatal. Previously established PFS benefit and long-term tolerability remain relevant despite nonsignificant OS.

Limits. Crossover, additional therapies, attrition and distinct population prevent using median OS as a transplant counterfactual. Do not equate nonsignificant OS with no clinical activity, or assume PRRT has no long-term marrow risk.

ENETS 2026

Design and population. ENETS 2026 position statement explicitly addressing controversies; expert, hypothesis-generating framework rather than original comparative outcome study.

Selection. Reorients toward incremental benefit and proposes substantial liver burden, young/fit patients and selected controllable extrahepatic disease; broadens beyond traditional Milan in some respects.

Results. No original outcome denominator. Proposed liver-burden bands around 25–70%, age <60 as relative and six-month stability are pragmatic concepts. Illustrative non-LT five-year survival <30% versus post-LT ≥70% are not a calibrated individual benefit model.

Limits. Do not turn an expert future-oriented proposal into validated numerical gates. Suggested controllable extrahepatic disease, including bone, conflicts with current NLRB solid-organ-metastasis exclusion. It identifies research needs rather than approving an expansion.

NET-B2High-risk protocol · Initial selection limits

Primary site, grade, spread and disease control

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Require expert pathology with Ki-67 <20% and the applicable mitotic criterion; use the highest reliable representative grade. Exactly 20%, rounding uncertainty or discordance is HOLD for adjudication, not rounding down. Do not impose an unsupported Ki-67 <10 rule or blanket G2 ban.
  • G3, NEC, unknown/nonportal primary and active extrahepatic disease have no active cohort in this edition. Previously resected nodes may qualify after complete clearance and the required disease-free interval; document stations and dates. Prior lung/bone/other solid-organ metastases remain outside the current opening pathway.
  • Require ≥6 months of stable/responding hepatic disease and ≥6 months without recurrence after primary/extrahepatic clearance. Proposed documentation: at least three comparable assessments spanning baseline, about three months and six months. A suspicious new site is HOLD; confirmed progression restarts observation.
Tests and information to review

Expert grade/Ki-67/mitoses and primary origin; SSTR staging and cross-sectional imaging; complete primary/node/EHD clearance history; three-assessment timeline.

Allocation and donor route

Current NET guidance and clinical consensus differ on some boundaries; favorable guidance does not remove the need to prove B1 benefit.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

Evidence supports carefully selected well-differentiated G1/G2 portal-drained GEP primaries with cleared primary/extrahepatic disease and observed control. G2 has worse prognosis than G1 in the multicentre series but still meaningful selected survival, so a universal Ki-67 <10% rule would discard potentially suitable G2 without validation. Well-differentiated G3 and poorly differentiated NEC must be separated diagnostically, yet neither has an established LT-benefit pathway. Prior primary regional nodal positivity is not equivalent to active unresected extrahepatic disease.

Evidence against expansion

Milan LT cohorts included many patients whose primary nodes had been positive, so 'ever node-positive means never LT' is too broad. However, retrospective final-pathology positive nodes do not negate the current cleared-disease requirement. Spanish OUT cases and the tiny G3 subgroup are too heterogeneous to validate expansion. NETTER-2 proves PRRT activity in selected G3, not LT efficacy after response; seven post-LT PRRT cases cannot guarantee safe salvage. ENETS controllable bone disease conflicts with NLRB's solid-organ exclusion.

Certainty

Low-to-moderate certainty for G1/G2 prognostic distinction and cautious standard selection; very low certainty for G3, nonportal/unknown primary, active nodes or prior solid-organ metastasis expansions. Official allocation exclusions should be reported separately from biological evidence.

Basis for proposed thresholds

Ki-67 <20% and six months after primary/extrahepatic clearance reflect current NLRB wording; modern WHO grading and the exact-20 boundary need explicit reconciliation. Six months stability derives from selection traditions, not a randomized timing trial. Three serial scans at roughly 0/3/6 months are a proposed local demonstration schedule, not a national mandated scan count.

Boscà 2026 · Spain

Design and population. Spanish 13-centre retrospective series of 91 NET LT recipients, 1995–2024; 41 transplanted before 2007; 71 Milan-IN and 20 Milan-OUT.

Selection. Mixed primary sites/eras and deviations. Pathology data missing in more than 30%; Ki-67 available in 51, with only one documented >20%. Milan-OUT combines different burden, primary, grade and other deviations; it is not a controlled trial of a single burden expansion.

Results. Time zero LT; median follow-up 6.5 years. Table 5: five-year OS 71.0% IN versus 58.7% OUT, ten-year 55.5% versus 32.0%, fifteen-year 52.2% versus 16.0% (OS comparison P=0.03). Five-year DFS 58.8% versus 36.3%, not statistically significant. Recurrence 52/91; ninety-day mortality 9/91 (9.9%). Nine retransplants: two primary nonfunction, six hepatic artery thrombosis, one rejection.

Limits. Era, missing pathology, small OUT group and composite deviations preclude validating 50–70% burden, age ≥60, G3 or extrahepatic expansion individually. Nonsignificant DFS is not equivalence. The abstract rounds OUT five-year OS differently; use Table 5 value with locator.

Le Treut 2013 · ELTR

Design and population. European Liver Transplant Registry retrospective multicentre study, 213 recipients at 35 centres, 1982–2009; 107 before 2000 and 106 thereafter. Published primary abstract reviewed.

Selection. Heterogeneous historical NET pathology and selection; 55% had hepatomegaly. Major additional resection in 24 and minor in 30. Historical poor differentiation is not interchangeable with modern well-differentiated G3.

Results. Three-month mortality 10%. Five-year OS 52% measured from LT, versus 73% measured from liver-metastasis diagnosis; five-year DFS 30%. Post-2000 five-year OS 59%. Poor differentiation, hepatomegaly and concurrent major resection were adverse; age >45 was adverse in a later-era analysis.

Limits. The striking time-origin difference illustrates why survival curves cannot be compared casually. Registry era, incomplete grading and nonrandom selection limit modern applicability. Age 45 association does not establish a biological exclusion threshold. Cohort overlap with later multicentre reports is likely.

NETTER-2

Design and population. NETTER-2 open-label phase III randomized trial: 261 screened, 226 randomized, 151 Lu-177-Dotatate plus octreotide and 75 high-dose octreotide.

Selection. Newly diagnosed advanced well-differentiated G2 Ki-67 10–20% and G3 >20–55%, SSTR-positive in all target lesions. G3 NET is biologically distinct from poorly differentiated NEC. This was not a trial of transplant eligibility or transplant bridging.

Results. Time zero randomization. Median PFS 22.8 versus 8.5 months, HR 0.276 (95% CI 0.182–0.418); objective response 43% versus 9.3%. OS was not mature. Safety populations 147 and 73: any grade ≥3 events 35% versus 27%, related grade ≥3 events 16% versus 4%; one myelodysplastic syndrome case and three grade-3 nephrotoxicity events in Lu-treated patients; no drug-related deaths reported.

Limits. Compelling systemic disease-control evidence for selected well-differentiated G2/G3, not evidence that responding G3 becomes suitable for LT or that marrow/renal injury is irrelevant to future transplantation. No NEC efficacy extrapolation. Full article reviewed now, upgrading prior abstract-only status.

Chen 2026 · PRRT after LT

Design and population. Retrospective seven-recipient case series of PRRT for recurrent NET after LT; abstract-only verification.

Selection. Post-LT recurrence, not pretransplant bridging or a selection study.

Results. One partial response, five stable disease, one progression. Two grade-III myelosuppression events, one treatment cessation; proteinuria in four. No rejection reported over limited follow-up.

Limits. No control, tiny denominator and short follow-up; does not demonstrate durable salvage, long-term graft safety, or justify enrolling G3/extrahepatic cases because PRRT is available later.

NLRB oncology guidance

Design and population. Official Adult MELD Exceptions for Transplant Oncology guidance, December 2025 posting checked 24 October 2026. Allocation-review guidance, not national clinical policy or proof of efficacy.

Selection. CRLM: BRAF wild type, microsatellite stable, R0 primary, at least 12 months from CRLM diagnosis and six months chemotherapy control; pretreatment largest lesion not >10 cm; no post-primary-resection extrahepatic disease, including nodes outside the primary field. Initial CEA exclusion is >80 ng/mL; extensions require <80. NET: known portal-drained GEP primary, well-differentiated G1/G2, Ki-67 <20%, primary and extrahepatic disease resected with at least six months NED; no active extrahepatic disease. Lung/bone/other solid-organ metastasis history is a permanent exception-guidance exclusion.

Results. No efficacy denominator. NET guidance has no explicit numerical age or hepatic-volume cutoff. CRLM wording excludes >10% diameter growth or new lesions and labels this RECIST 1.1, although conventional RECIST 1.1 progression generally uses at least 20% summed-diameter increase plus an absolute change; record both, do not silently equate them.

Limits. Clinical judgment, exception endorsement and graft availability are distinct. A reasonable research hypothesis or living donor does not override allocation guidance or demonstrate acceptable benefit. At exactly CEA 80 the initial/extension wording differs; the draft's strict <80 harmonization is a conservative local rule.

NET-B3High-risk protocol · Individual review; expansion not activated

Age and liver tumor burden

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Age ≥60 triggers explicit physiological-reserve, competing-mortality and benefit review; no absolute chronological-age ceiling or invented NET-specific frailty score is adopted.
  • Reproducibly estimate liver involvement. <50% is the conservative Milan-derived reference; exactly 50% through 70% is a separate prospective expansion stratum, not automatically active. >70% is outside the initial local program for conservative risk control, not because published evidence proves futility. The 50–70/>70 choices are local proposals, not national limits.
  • Low burden, particularly <25%, defaults to non-LT care unless a documented NET-B1 benefit trigger overrides that default. Do not give harmful downstaging solely to cross a percentage boundary.
Tests and information to review

Method/date of volumetric burden; functional reserve/frailty; competing illness; symptom/benefit trigger; current treatment alternatives.

Allocation and donor route

NLRB guidance has no explicit age or liver-percentage ceiling; identify these as local clinical/research choices.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

The <50% burden and relative age <60 framework has the best selected-cohort lineage, not proof of a sharp treatment-benefit boundary. Registry associations with age >45 or hepatomegaly do not establish universal bans. More burden may increase need for organ replacement but also worsen prognosis; low burden may predict good post-LT survival while yielding little extra benefit. NLRB's lack of explicit age/burden limits does not validate any expansion.

Evidence against expansion

Spanish Milan-OUT survival is lower, but OUT combines many deviations and cannot isolate >50% burden or age ≥60. Matched outside-Milan confidence intervals are wide, not proof of futility. Kjaer's non-LT survival cautions against assuming any eligible patient needs LT. ENETS 25–70% proposal is an expert benefit-oriented hypothesis, not outcome-validated proof that 70% is a biological ceiling.

Certainty

Low certainty for any causal age/burden threshold; moderate confidence that these variables and liver function must be measured and followed. No prospective trial validates 25%, 50%, 70% or age 60 as a universal increment-benefit boundary.

Basis for proposed thresholds

<50% and age <60 relative originate in Milan selection; age >45 is historical prognostic association, not selection validation. ENETS 25–70% and six-month stability are pragmatic proposals. The V6 50–70% unopened stratum and >70% initial closure are local conservative design choices. Serial volumetry and trajectory matter more than a single imprecise percentage.

Propensity-adjusted restricted mean survival gain was 6.82 months at five years and 38.43 months at ten years. These are cohort estimates, not individual calculators.

Limits. Major selection/age/treatment-era differences, small numbers and residual confounding remain despite statistical handling of delayed treatment. Only 4/46 non-LT patients received PRRT, so this is not a modern sequential-care comparator. Patients overlap subsequent Milan series and some multicentre LT studies; do not sum them as independent evidence.

Maspero 2022 · Milan long-term

Design and population. Retrospective Milan single-centre series, 1984–2019: 149 liver operations (53 LT, 96 LR), with 104 Milan-eligible cases analysed (48 LT, 56 LR).

Selection. Portal-drained G1/G2 primary previously resected, no extrahepatic disease, <50% hepatic involvement, at least six months stable disease, age <60 relative. LT and resection had different resectability/age indications. Minimum six-month follow-up inclusion needs care when interpreting early risk; this is not an all-referral cohort.

Results. Time zero liver operation; median follow-up 158 months LT and 126 LR. Five-year OS 95.5% versus 90%; ten-year OS 93% versus 75%. Ten-year DFS 52% versus 18%; recurrence 25/48 versus 42/56 and median recurrence time 78 versus 24 months. LT recurrence was more often multisite; LR recurrence predominantly liver. Nine LT deaths included three cancer and six non-cancer deaths, including new malignancy, sepsis/lymphoproliferative and cardiovascular causes.

Limits. Not randomized or anatomically exchangeable; prolonged selected follow-up and differing age can favor LT. Strong overlap with 2016 Milan-NET makes it long-term/alternative-comparator information, not independent confirmation. Late recurrence and immunosuppression-related competing causes matter even with excellent survival.

Eshmuminov 2023 · LT versus resection

Design and population. International retrospective comparison from 15 centres, 1988–2021: 455 patients (225 LT, 230 liver resection); median follow-up 97 months. Propensity matching produced 96 patients per group.

Selection. Surgically selected patients, matched on age, grade and primary location. Twenty-seven percent of LT cases had unknown grade; burden, era and treatment history remain imperfectly controlled. Resection and LT do not necessarily address anatomically interchangeable patients.

Results. Use main Results p.e1067/Table 4: matched five-year OS 75% LT versus 68.3% LR, HR 0.56 (95% CI 0.35–0.90); five-year PFS 64.2% versus 14.2%, HR 0.25 (0.16–0.39), measured from operation. Ninety-day deaths 13/225 LT (5.8%) versus 3/230 LR (1.3%). LT G1 versus G2 five-year OS 92.9% versus 70%; adjusted G2 HR 2.52 (1.15–5.52). Outside-Milan matched OS HR 0.64 (0.31–1.33), not statistically conclusive.

Limits. Material abstract/body discrepancy: abstract reports matched five-year OS 73% versus 52.8%, whereas main Results/Table 4 report 75% versus 68.3%. The latter is used with explicit discrepancy flag; clarification would be desirable. Matching does not remove unmeasured confounding, missing pathology, era or selection bias. The nonsignificant outside-Milan estimate does not prove no benefit, and ten G3 cases do not validate G3 LT. Overlaps centre cohorts including Milan.

Kjaer 2023 · eligible non-LT panNET

Design and population. Single-centre retrospective non-LT panNET cohort, 2000–2021: 519 screened, 41 G1/G2 metastatic patients included; 16 met at least one historical LT framework (Milan 11, ENETS 8, UNOS 13, overlapping).

Selection. Resected primary, liver metastases, no extra-abdominal disease, age ≤75 and no G3. Definitions modified historical criteria, including Milan age <65 and two 'UNOS' patients with stable regional nodes, so not all meet today's NLRB requirements. Multimodal care included five liver resections, 15 ablations, six embolizations and later-line PRRT, not purely medical therapy.

Results. Time zero was date LT criteria fulfilled (for remaining patients, initial institutional visit after primary resection with liver metastases). Mean follow-up 5.4 years. Overall 41-patient median OS 9.3 years; five-year OS 64.7% (95% CI 48.2–81.2). Milan subset five-year OS 64.9% (32.2–97.6), ENETS 85.7% (59.8–100), historical UNOS 55.4% (26.0–84.8).

Limits. Small retrospective selected subgroups with very wide intervals; no internal LT comparator and treatment-era limitations. It challenges the assumption that eligible stage IV NET has uniformly poor non-LT survival; it cannot prove that non-LT is superior to LT. Avoid comparing its eligibility-based survival directly with post-LT curves as if randomized.

Boscà 2026 · Spain

Design and population. Spanish 13-centre retrospective series of 91 NET LT recipients, 1995–2024; 41 transplanted before 2007; 71 Milan-IN and 20 Milan-OUT.

Selection. Mixed primary sites/eras and deviations. Pathology data missing in more than 30%; Ki-67 available in 51, with only one documented >20%. Milan-OUT combines different burden, primary, grade and other deviations; it is not a controlled trial of a single burden expansion.

Results. Time zero LT; median follow-up 6.5 years. Table 5: five-year OS 71.0% IN versus 58.7% OUT, ten-year 55.5% versus 32.0%, fifteen-year 52.2% versus 16.0% (OS comparison P=0.03). Five-year DFS 58.8% versus 36.3%, not statistically significant. Recurrence 52/91; ninety-day mortality 9/91 (9.9%). Nine retransplants: two primary nonfunction, six hepatic artery thrombosis, one rejection.

Limits. Era, missing pathology, small OUT group and composite deviations preclude validating 50–70% burden, age ≥60, G3 or extrahepatic expansion individually. Nonsignificant DFS is not equivalence. The abstract rounds OUT five-year OS differently; use Table 5 value with locator.

Le Treut 2013 · ELTR

Design and population. European Liver Transplant Registry retrospective multicentre study, 213 recipients at 35 centres, 1982–2009; 107 before 2000 and 106 thereafter. Published primary abstract reviewed.

Selection. Heterogeneous historical NET pathology and selection; 55% had hepatomegaly. Major additional resection in 24 and minor in 30. Historical poor differentiation is not interchangeable with modern well-differentiated G3.

Results. Three-month mortality 10%. Five-year OS 52% measured from LT, versus 73% measured from liver-metastasis diagnosis; five-year DFS 30%. Post-2000 five-year OS 59%. Poor differentiation, hepatomegaly and concurrent major resection were adverse; age >45 was adverse in a later-era analysis.

Limits. The striking time-origin difference illustrates why survival curves cannot be compared casually. Registry era, incomplete grading and nonrandom selection limit modern applicability. Age 45 association does not establish a biological exclusion threshold. Cohort overlap with later multicentre reports is likely.

ENETS 2026

Design and population. ENETS 2026 position statement explicitly addressing controversies; expert, hypothesis-generating framework rather than original comparative outcome study.

Selection. Reorients toward incremental benefit and proposes substantial liver burden, young/fit patients and selected controllable extrahepatic disease; broadens beyond traditional Milan in some respects.

Results. No original outcome denominator. Proposed liver-burden bands around 25–70%, age <60 as relative and six-month stability are pragmatic concepts. Illustrative non-LT five-year survival <30% versus post-LT ≥70% are not a calibrated individual benefit model.

Limits. Do not turn an expert future-oriented proposal into validated numerical gates. Suggested controllable extrahepatic disease, including bone, conflicts with current NLRB solid-organ-metastasis exclusion. It identifies research needs rather than approving an expansion.
