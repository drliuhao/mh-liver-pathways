CRLM-B1High-risk protocol · Confirm the treatment options

Confirm that complete resection is not feasible

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Proposed requirements: two HPB surgeons, including one who is not the proposed transplant surgeon, agree that complete nontransplant clearance is infeasible. Include radiology, oncology and an ablation/HAI-capable clinician; disagreement is HOLD with independent external review.
  • Review parenchymal-sparing, staged, combined resection/ablation and hypertrophy-assisted options. Use FLR benchmarks of about 20% in normal liver, 30% in chemotherapy-injured liver and 40% in cirrhosis to assess resection—not as automatic LT criteria. Assess function, inflow, outflow and biliary drainage as well as volume.
  • Renew certification after meaningful response or material treatment change and within 30 days before a final decision to proceed with transplantation. Restage approximately every eight weeks on active treatment. Consider HAI conversion when appropriate; do not mandate HAI treatment for every patient.
Tests and information to review

Pretreatment/current lesion maps including disappearing sites; contrast liver MRI and systemic staging; volumetry/function; vascular/biliary map; prior treatments; signed alternative-clearance assessment. Obtain a specialized conversion/HAIP opinion when clinically appropriate and accessible; document why it is omitted otherwise. A mandatory opinion is not a requirement to expose every patient to HAIP.

Allocation and donor route

Persistent unresectability is a clinical finding; the separate NLRB molecular, timing, imaging and response requirements still apply if exception review is pursued.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

The best evidence supports LT only after a credible expert determination that complete, safe hepatic clearance cannot be achieved. TransMet excluded equivocal unresectability and used independent adjudication. The HAIP series shows that a subset labelled transplant-like can convert, but does not prove mandatory HAIP or equal survival. Long-term SECA-II supports durable benefit in narrowly selected unresectable disease, not a shortcut around reconstruction, ablation or FLR review.

Evidence against expansion

Many initially unresectable patients can be converted; HAIP brought 18/23 highly selected pretreated patients to resection, although most recurred and only 10/18 were R0. A high lesion count, bilobar distribution, chemotherapy injury or a low raw FLR alone is insufficient. Conversely, mandatory repeated conversion attempts can expose patients to biliary injury and progression without validated benefit.

Certainty

Moderate-to-high certainty for the TransMet treatment strategy in its restricted randomized population; low certainty for an individual unresectability threshold, comparative HAIP effect, and broad expansions. FLR cutoffs are technical risk guidance, not predictive LT-benefit biomarkers.

Basis for proposed thresholds

20/30/40% FLR values originate in hepatectomy planning, not validated LT-selection trials. Two reviewers and reassessment within 30 days before activation, with imaging about every eight weeks during treatment, are proposed local workflow intervals. The national exception application requires its own current staging intervals; a 30-day local review does not replace them.

TransMet 2024

Design and population. Multicentre open-label randomized trial, 20 European centres, 157 screened and 94 randomized (47 LT-plus-chemotherapy, 47 chemotherapy); 63 screened excluded, including 13 with equivocal unresectability and 36 with progression.

Selection. Age 18–65; ECOG 0–1; R0 colorectal primary; BRAF nonmutated; no prior or current extrahepatic disease/local recurrence; chemotherapy response or stable disease for at least 3 months, at most three lines. CEA <80 ng/mL OR at least 50% reduction from peak was permitted. Independent committee of at least two surgeons, two oncologists and one radiologist reviewed original and current lesion maps. These are trial criteria, not identical to current NLRB guidance.

Results. Primary time origin was randomization. Median follow-up 59.3 months. Five-year ITT OS was 56.6% versus 12.6% (HR 0.37, 95% CI 0.21–0.65); per-protocol OS 73.3% versus 9.3%. Of 47 assigned LT, 38 received LT and 36 were per-protocol; nine had progression-related exclusion/dropout. The control arm included seven subsequent resections and two LT crossovers. Safety population comprised 40 grafted patients. Among 36 per-protocol LT recipients, 26 recurred; 19/26 received chemotherapy and 12/26 local curative-intent treatment. Median per-protocol PFS was 17.4 versus 6.4 months, and secondary PFS after salvage was 35.4 months in the LT arm. Three of 36 per-protocol recipients required retransplantation (primary nonfunction, donor gallbladder cancer, caval obstruction/large-for-size); one postoperative death was reported. Serious adverse events occurred in 32/40 safety LT recipients. Median wait to LT was 50.5 days, which limits transfer to longer-wait systems.

Limits. Strong evidence for the selected trial population, not for BRAF-mutant, ECOG 2, active extrahepatic, truly NED, resectable or broadly expanded disease. PP survival conditions on reaching treatment and must not replace ITT. The Results recurrence denominator is 26/36; Figure 3 caption says 28 recurrences, consistent with a broader all-grafted report, so do not mix these denominators. QoL collection had only 55/74 per-protocol baseline questionnaires and is descriptive, not proof of a large QoL gain. Priority allocation and rapid access were part of the treatment strategy.

Chandra 2026 · HAIP comparator

Design and population. Single-centre retrospective HAIP cohort: 1,110 patients treated in 2006–2016, 483 initially unresectable after exclusions; 83 met modified transplant criteria, but only 23 were pretreated, chemotherapy-controlled transplant-like candidates.

Selection. Selected patients had liver-confined disease without progression on pre-HAIP chemotherapy. The study modified SECA/TransMet criteria: PET, molecular profile, CEA and standardized RECIST response were not mandatory, and primary resection could occur during/after HAIP. Applying at least one year from CRC diagnosis to HAIP left only five of 483 patients. Unresectability considered adequate functional remnant, vascular/biliary preservation and intraoperative ultrasound, not lesion number alone.

Results. Time zero was HAIP placement, not a common LT-versus-HAIP eligibility date. In the 23 selected pretreated patients, 18/23 (78%) converted to resection; only 10/18 were R0, and 17/18 recurred, with salvage treatment in 15. Median follow-up was 97.7 months; median OS 61 months (95% CI 36–92), five-year OS 53% (36–79), PFS 13 months (9.6–22), hepatic PFS 16 months (11–61). Median treatment comprised five HAIP cycles and nine systemic cycles. Biliary sclerosis requiring stents occurred in 3/23 (13%); one pump-arterial dissection required revision.

Limits. Selection from a large denominator, modified eligibility, expert-centre availability, retrospective ascertainment and a different time origin prohibit equivalence claims against TransMet. The high conversion rate is clinically important counterevidence to declaring unresectability before specialist conversion review. It does not make HAIP compulsory or establish that its toxicities are an indication to create for LT.

Dueland 2020 · SECA-II

Design and population. Prospective single-centre SECA-II initial report, 15 highly selected recipients transplanted 2012–2016, without a randomized non-LT comparator.

Selection. R0 primary; ECOG 0–1; liver-only disease with no prior extrahepatic metastases; at least one year from diagnosis to listing; at least 10% chemotherapy response. Additional size/number and response restrictions applied. All 15 had Oslo score 0–1. Median largest lesion at LT 24 mm, number five; only one KRAS-mutated case. Median wait 29 days, no wait-list dropout.

Results. Time zero LT. Median follow-up 36 months (range 5–60). Reported one-, three-, five-year OS 100%, 83%, 83%; sparse five-year tail. Median DFS 13.7 months, with one-, two-, three-year DFS 53%, 44%, 35%. Eight patients recurred, six in lung. Seven of 15 had Clavien-Dindo IIIa–IVa complications requiring intervention. No adjuvant chemotherapy.

Limits. The 15-patient cohort is included in the later 25-patient SECA-II report, not independent corroboration. Selected response, low burden and short wait strongly limit generalizability. The better OS than DFS demonstrates salvage opportunities but not that recurrence is harmless. A 10% response criterion is not the same as the current NLRB >10% growth exclusion or standard RECIST 1.1 progression.

Dueland 2026 · SECA-II ten-year

Design and population. Prospective SECA-II extended follow-up, 25 recipients from April 2012 to November 2023; analysis cutoff 1 January 2025. Includes the original SECA-II cohort.

Selection. All grafted under SECA-II included. At least one year diagnosis to inclusion and no progression required; one inadvertently transplanted patient had progression on a scan not available to the transplant centre. One early postoperative death was included in survival analyses but excluded from other analyses, so baseline Table 1 denominator is 24. Among these 24, median largest lesion 24 mm (4–48), CEA 3.5 (1–33), PET MTV 7.3 cm³ (0–139); only two MTV >70, two KRAS-mutated, one right-sided ascending primary and one pN2. Oslo scores 0/1/2 were 9/13/2.

Results. Time zero LT; median follow-up of survivors 97.2 months. Median OS 128.2 months (95% CI 74.8–181.6); five- and ten-year OS 77.8% and 51.5%. Median RFS 24.3 months; 12/25 relapsed, six initially lung-only. Median post-recurrence survival 62.9 months (25.7–101.1); nine had surgery as first recurrence treatment, but only three were classified curative-intent at that first treatment. Twelve had no recurrence over median 74 months; four exceeded ten years without recurrence. Four patients died of metastases more than five years after LT. Reported serious complications: five grade IIIa, three IIIb, one grade V hepatic-artery-thrombosis death.

Limits. No contemporaneous control and only 25 recipients; highly favorable biology cannot justify broad expansion. Abstract speculates up to 15 potentially cured, body p.5 says up to 13; neither is an observed, validated cure fraction. Use observed survival/recurrence instead. The discussion's assertion that RFS is not valid for efficacy should not erase recurrence burden, treatment harms or QoL; RFS is insufficient alone, not unimportant. Updated survival supersedes rather than adds an independent 83% SECA-II estimate.

IHPBA 2021

Design and population. 2021 international multidisciplinary consensus; not a prospective eligibility-strategy trial.

Selection. Distinguishes technical unresectability, biology and organ-allocation ethics. Recommends BRAF V600E exclusion; RAS mutation alone is not an absolute exclusion. MSI-H/dMMR cases should receive effective systemic options and are not routinely recommended for LT.

Results. No original randomized outcome denominator. Consensus suggests response/observation, CEA trajectory and PET burden (MTV 70 cm³/TLG 260) as risk criteria. Rising CEA >80 is treated more adversely than falling CEA.

Limits. Expert recommendations differ from current NLRB wording and TransMet criteria; do not merge them into a falsely universal threshold. Non-V600E BRAF is not thereby proven safe for LT, and contemporary guidance requests BRAF-wild-type tumours.

Bilhim 2024

Design and population. CIRSE consensus standards for portal-vein embolization and liver venous deprivation, 2024; not a transplant-versus-resection comparative trial.

Selection. Assesses future liver remnant volume and function, vascular/biliary anatomy, parenchymal quality and capacity for hypertrophy before major hepatectomy.

Results. Frequently used planning benchmarks are approximately >20% normal parenchyma, >30% chemotherapy-exposed/injured liver and >40% cirrhosis. These are resection-risk benchmarks, not an LT eligibility test or a guarantee of safe resection.

Limits. Volume cannot substitute for function, and the relevant injury severity differs by patient. Failure of a numerical FLR threshold alone does not establish persistent oncological unresectability if PVE/LVD, staged or parenchymal-sparing treatment remains feasible.

CRLM-B2High-risk protocol · No transplant for presumed microscopic disease

No tumor is visible after treatment

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Residual unresectable disease: convincing viable/suspicious disease on appropriate cross-sectional imaging may proceed to B1 review even if PET is negative. PET negativity alone does not prove cure.
  • Radiographic NED/occult-residual-risk: no convincing remaining disease on high-quality staging. Maintain the original lesion map, continue appropriate oncology care and reassess in about eight weeks; LT for presumed microscopic risk requires a separately approved NED study.
  • Irreversible treatment-related liver failure: evaluate a separate liver-failure indication with independent hepatology and oncologic-suitability review. Do not relabel it active unresectable cancer. Do not stop effective treatment to provoke recurrence or biopsy every scar.
  • Keep all original lesion sites mapped. Disappearance does not establish eradication, but uncertain microscopic viability alone does not establish persistent unresectability or transplant benefit; diagnostic accuracy and treatment benefit are different questions.
Tests and information to review

Expert comparison of pretreatment and current liver MRI/CT; systemic staging; treatment dates and PET interpretation; liver reserve; separate adjudication of NED versus suspicious residual sites.

Allocation and donor route

A separate liver-failure indication does not automatically meet the CRLM exception pathway. Obtain case-specific clinical and allocation adjudication.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

CT/MRI/PET negativity cannot reliably establish pathological eradication. However, uncertain residual microscopic disease also does not automatically establish clinically meaningful persistent unresectability or an LT benefit. DREAM provides the crucial balance: substantial residual risk despite disappearance, but no adequately powered proof that removing every invisible site improves survival. The small US LT series is genuine NED/injury experience but remains selected and uncontrolled.

Evidence against expansion

Some disappearing lesions are genuinely nonviable: DREAM classified 95/152 CT-plus-MRI disappearing lesions as nonviable by pathology or two-year observation; Oba found only 3/68 viable lesions after negative MRI plus CE-IOUS. Conversely, 4/7 complete imaging responders in the explant series had viable cancer. Both prevent a binary 'all scars count' or 'none count' rule. NED patients with preserved liver function lack comparative evidence of benefit from prophylactic LT.

Certainty

Moderate certainty that imaging disappearance does not reliably prove nonviability; low certainty about the best lesion-management strategy; very low certainty for LT in true radiographic NED or treatment-injury subgroups. Explant findings are diagnostic, not a causal efficacy comparison.

Basis for proposed thresholds

No validated chemotherapy-cycle minimum, scar count, PET-negative interval, ctDNA-negativity requirement or NED duration establishes LT benefit. Proposed approximately eight-week imaging while on active treatment is local workflow, adjustable for clinical need; more sensitive imaging/CE-IOUS informs resection planning but is not a transplant clearance test.

Chávez-Villa 2023 · occult disease

Design and population. Retrospective radiology–explant study of 14 selected LT recipients; eight indications were unresectable disease and six treatment-associated liver failure/biliary complications.

Selection. Median 11 original lesions and 24 chemotherapy cycles; extensive systemic/local therapy. Seven had PET-defined complete radiographic response. This was a transplanted-only, highly selected sample.

Results. Explant contained viable carcinoma in 11/14; nine had additional lesions not accounted for on pre-LT imaging. Four of seven imaging complete responders still had viable tumour. The three pathological complete responders were all in the liver-failure indication group.

Limits. Demonstrates imperfect imaging negative predictive value, not that every scar remains viable or that prophylactic LT improves survival for radiographic NED. No non-LT or observation comparator; complete-response definition and treatment injury matter. Likely overlap with subsequent US protocols.

Kataoka 2025 · DREAM

Design and population. Prospective international DREAM diagnostic study at 21 centres: 233 registered initially unresectable/borderline CRLM patients, 112 planned conversion surgery, 109 operated. Primary diagnostic analysis had 152 evaluable CT-plus-MRI disappearing lesions from 44 patients.

Selection. Post-treatment CT plus MRI (diffusion, T1/T2 and contrast); disappearance on both defined confirmed DLM. Reference was pathology for resected sites or no site recurrence over two years for unresected sites. Fifty-one confirmed lesions were non-evaluable, including deaths and incomplete imaging follow-up.

Results. Nonviability NPV was 62.5% (95/152; 90% CI 50.8–74.2), failing the prespecified >85% benchmark; CT-alone NPV 52.9%. Resected confirmed DLM: 50/88 nonviable; left-behind: 45/64 without site recurrence. In the n=45 R0/1, no-extrahepatic-disease subgroup, resecting all confirmed DLM (19 patients) versus leaving at least one (26) yielded DFS HR 0.92 (95% CI 0.48–1.77), OS HR 0.80 (0.21–3.08), with time measured after surgery; survival comparisons were not powered or randomized. Median follow-up 26.1 months.

Limits. Lesion-level diagnostic probability is not patient-level benefit. Two-year non-recurrence is not pathology; evaluability selection and noncentral imaging review are limitations. Neither disappearance equals cure nor uncertain viability requires removal/LT. A nonsignificant survival comparison is not equivalence or evidence that resection is futile.

Oba 2018 · disappearing lesions

Design and population. Retrospective hepatectomy imaging series, 184 patients, 59 with 275 CT-disappearing metastases; primary published abstract verified, full PDF pending.

Selection. Hepatocyte-specific MRI followed by contrast-enhanced intraoperative ultrasound (CE-IOUS); surgically selected population, unlike an all-transplant referral cohort.

Results. MRI identified 71 CT-disappearing lesions, of which 65 were viable; CE-IOUS found 94 additional lesions. Of 110 undetected by both modalities, 68 were resected and three viable (4%); among 42 left behind, six recurred (14%) over median 27 months.

Limits. Lesion-level retrospective selection and assay sequence differ from DREAM. More sensitive imaging can identify residual disease, but these data do not prove that every original site must be resected or that NED warrants LT.

Wehrle 2024 · intensive LRT

Design and population. Single-centre intensive pre-LT locoregional-treatment protocol: 27 evaluated, 16 included, nine transplanted (seven living donor). Abstract-only verification.

Selection. Median 16 systemic cycles (10–40); Y90 in five, ablation in four, resection in four and HAIP in three. Five grafted patients had no detectable disease and four had treatment-induced cirrhosis.

Results. Median follow-up 700 days; three recurrences. Reported RFS one year 6/8 (75%) and two years 3/5 (60%) reflects very small mature denominators.

Limits. This is actual published experience with NED/injury routes, so it must not be described as nonexistent; it is not controlled proof of benefit or a universal cycle-count requirement. Patients likely overlap occult-explant/PET/ctDNA cohorts. Do not intensify treatment to manufacture liver failure.

CRLM-B3High-risk protocol · Initial expansion limits

Molecular findings and previous extrahepatic disease

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Initial proposed exclusions: BRAF-mutant disease, MSI-H/dMMR disease, R1 primary margins, prior post-primary extrahepatic metastases, pretreatment liver lesion >10 cm, or progression despite treatment. Preserve these as separate research questions; do not combine several relaxations.
  • Proposed CEA requirement: strictly <80 ng/mL, including initial activation. Repeat a borderline/unexpected result and review the trajectory; a falling value still ≥80 does not clear this local rule.
  • Require ≥6 months of documented control and the applicable ≥12-month CRLM-diagnosis interval. Report ordinary RECIST separately from the NLRB >10% wording; new lesions or concerning growth are HOLD pending central measurement review.
  • Confirmed progression resets the proposed six-month control clock after subsequent effective treatment; one improved scan is insufficient. Use the actual application imaging/CEA windows. Do not delay indicated primary surgery to manufacture eligibility.
Tests and information to review

Primary R0 pathology; BRAF, RAS and MMR/MSI; original maximum lesion diameter; complete EHD history; dated CEA; dated response and primary/CRLM diagnosis; current staging.

Allocation and donor route

National guidance, study criteria and local choices remain separate. Confirm current NLRB wording at application and do not substitute RECIST for an expressly different guidance threshold.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

BRAF-wild-type, controlled liver-only disease with a cleared primary has the strongest treatment evidence. KRAS mutation alone was allowed in TransMet (17/47 assigned LT), so a blanket KRAS ban would exceed evidence; that does not prove every molecular subgroup derives equal benefit. Arm D is a warning against stacking expansions. PET/Oslo/CEA markers forecast prognosis in recipients but have not demonstrated that crossing a threshold changes the incremental benefit of LT over modern non-LT care.

Evidence against expansion

IHPBA specifically excludes BRAF V600E, whereas NLRB requests BRAF wild type; non-V600E cases are therefore a true guideline/research gap, not automatically eligible. TransMet accepted CEA <80 OR a ≥50% decline, unlike the draft strict <80 rule. SECA-II's excellent long-term outcomes mostly occurred with CEA ≤33, small lesions and low MTV; they cannot establish safety at every nominal upper limit. Negative ctDNA has not been validated to rescue other exclusion criteria.

Certainty

Moderate-to-high for narrow TransMet eligibility; low/very low for non-V600E BRAF, prior pulmonary disease, R1 primary, ECOG 2, or ctDNA-directed selection. Prognostic association does not equal predictive treatment-benefit validation. Allocation criteria have authoritative administrative status but are not randomized cutoff evidence.

Basis for proposed thresholds

Current NLRB contains six-month/12-month/10-cm/CEA requirements and >10% growth wording. TransMet used at least three months control and a CEA-decline alternative. Oslo >5.5 cm, CEA >80, <2 years and progression are prognostic score components; MTV ~70 is data-derived; TLG 260 is consensus, not the same as Grut's 238.8. Resetting a six-month clock after confirmed progression and requiring repeat CEA are proposed local safeguards, not validated rescue rules.

SECA-II arm D

Design and population. Prospective SECA-II arm D expanded-criteria cohort, ten recipients, without control.

Selection. Expanded both recipient and donor criteria, including resectable pulmonary metastases and omission of previous stringent response/time restrictions. Eight had pN2 primaries, six poorly differentiated or signet-ring pathology, five right-sided cancers; median 20 liver lesions and largest diameter 59 mm. This was a bundle of adverse features, not an isolated prior-lung-metastasis experiment.

Results. From LT, median DFS four months and median OS 18 months. Rapid recurrence and limited survival contrast with strictly selected SECA-II, despite use of donor grafts intended to broaden access.

Limits. Very small and uncontrolled; simultaneous biologic, anatomic and donor expansions prevent estimating the independent effect of prior pulmonary disease, BRAF status, grade or donor quality. Negative experience argues against opening several expansions at once, not that every single deviation has proven futility.

Grut 2022 · MTV

Design and population. Retrospective prognostic analysis of 40 SECA recipients (23 SECA-I, 17 SECA-II); overlapping, not a new transplant efficacy cohort.

Selection. Pretransplant FDG-PET MTV quantified across liver lesions. Outcome-derived ROC cutoff 66.09 cm³ was rounded to 70; TLG cutoff was 238.8, not identical to IHPBA's 260 recommendation. PET timing relative to chemotherapy and LT affects measurement.

Results. MTV <70 group n=26 versus higher MTV n=14: five-year OS 76% versus 21%, ten-year OS 50% versus 7%, measured from LT. This is a prognostic association in transplanted patients.

Limits. Data-derived cutoff, small sample, SECA overlap and no non-LT interaction test. Reducing MTV below 70 with treatment has not been shown to cause improved LT benefit; PET negativity cannot certify microscopic eradication.

Wehrle 2025 · PET

Design and population. Two-US-centre retrospective study of 26 recipients, 2017–2023; 24 received living-donor grafts. Published abstract reviewed; complete PDF unavailable in searched depots/Downloads.

Selection. Highly selected CRLM recipients with pre-LT PET, not an unselected screening cohort. Possible institutional and patient overlap with US intensive-LRT, occult-explant and ctDNA reports.

Results. Eight recurrences; median follow-up 609 days. Abstract reports two-year OS 11/13 and RFS 8/13 among those reaching that follow-up, and median RFS 0.83 versus 4.1 years with MTV at least 70 versus below 70.

Limits. Small, short follow-up and abstract numerical/CI inconsistencies. Do not reproduce its inconsistent hazard ratio or call the 70-cm³ rule prospectively validated. Retrieve full methods before using for a binding operational cutoff.

Wehrle 2023 · ctDNA

Design and population. Observational mixed resection/LT cohort, 52 patients with ctDNA data, 34 preoperative and 18 postoperative tests; only nine paired samples. Abstract-only verification.

Selection. Guardant360 profiling, variable timing; the 29 mutation types reported are not 29 patients. Not a ctDNA-directed LT eligibility trial.

Results. All nine paired patients were ctDNA-positive preoperatively; three remained positive postoperatively. Abstract reports postoperative recurrence 57% versus 0% by ctDNA status (P=0.0419).

Limits. Small numbers, mixed operations, incomplete pairs, possible overlap with other centre cohorts and no strategy comparison. Association supports research and risk discussion, not mandatory negative ctDNA, empirical treatment until negative, or declaring cure from a negative result.

IHPBA 2021

Design and population. 2021 international multidisciplinary consensus; not a prospective eligibility-strategy trial.

Selection. Distinguishes technical unresectability, biology and organ-allocation ethics. Recommends BRAF V600E exclusion; RAS mutation alone is not an absolute exclusion. MSI-H/dMMR cases should receive effective systemic options and are not routinely recommended for LT.

Results. No original randomized outcome denominator. Consensus suggests response/observation, CEA trajectory and PET burden (MTV 70 cm³/TLG 260) as risk criteria. Rising CEA >80 is treated more adversely than falling CEA.

Limits. Expert recommendations differ from current NLRB wording and TransMet criteria; do not merge them into a falsely universal threshold. Non-V600E BRAF is not thereby proven safe for LT, and contemporary guidance requests BRAF-wild-type tumours.

SUNLIGHT: FTD/TPI plus bevacizumab

Application. Offer a later-line disease-control option when marrow reserve and anti-VEGF safety permit. Record bleeding, wound-healing and operative timing; do not translate disease control automatically into LT eligibility.

CRLM-B4High-risk protocol · Benefit and graft-use review

Expected benefit, recurrence and graft use

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Before considering transplantation: a patient-specific benefit comparison against accessible non-LT care, feasible recurrence-treatment options, and a realistic local wait/treatment plan. Reassess about every eight weeks while waiting; no unsupported maximum-wait exclusion is imposed.
  • Plan one graft per recipient; no planned retransplantation for oncologic recurrence. Rescue of early nononcologic graft failure remains a separate standard transplant/allocation decision and is never promised or withheld by this rule.
  • No low recurrence-rate/RFS cutoff is substituted for survival or quality-of-life benefit. Record all-screened outcomes, recurrence treatment burden, time off therapy, graft loss/retransplantation and functional recovery. Apply the one-event early safety pause in ALLOC-B4 to newly activated experimental cohorts.
Tests and information to review

Contemporary comparator; current fitness/control; recurrence options; local access estimate; patient priorities; graft-use and follow-up plan.

Allocation and donor route

Do not promise trial survival where waiting/access differ materially. Count all grafts and downstream allocation consequences, including LDLT recipients who later need DDLT.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

The relevant endpoint is the entire strategy from a shared eligibility/randomization date: access to a first graft, wait-list progression, perioperative death, graft failure, recurrence, salvage treatment, long-term survival and QoL. Recurrence does not erase all benefit, but is a substantial burden; SECA-II shows both long post-recurrence survival and late metastatic death. TransMet's 56.6% ITT survival is the appropriate randomized headline, with 73.3% PP provided only secondarily.

Evidence against expansion

TransMet's nine progression dropouts and three retransplants show why post-LT survival alone is inadequate. The 2026 SECA-II ten-year OS was 51.5%, not its five-year 77.8%; four metastatic deaths occurred after five years. Arm D's 18-month median OS cautions against assuming every expanded candidate obtains sufficient benefit. Selected HAIP/conversion patients can have durable survival without a graft, but cross-study comparisons cannot calculate an individual's LT gain.

Certainty

Moderate-to-high for a survival advantage in selected TransMet patients; low for an individual numerical benefit model, long-wait extrapolation, high-risk graft strategies or predetermined retransplant policy. Long-term SECA-II is informative but small and uncontrolled.

Basis for proposed thresholds

No validated minimum RFS, individual five-year benefit percentage or fixed number of salvage operations establishes acceptable LT value. The two-year/three-year thresholds sometimes used in economic models are not recipient-selection validation. One planned graft and safety-review triggers are institutional ethical/operational proposals.

TransMet 2024

Design and population. Multicentre open-label randomized trial, 20 European centres, 157 screened and 94 randomized (47 LT-plus-chemotherapy, 47 chemotherapy); 63 screened excluded, including 13 with equivocal unresectability and 36 with progression.

Selection. Age 18–65; ECOG 0–1; R0 colorectal primary; BRAF nonmutated; no prior or current extrahepatic disease/local recurrence; chemotherapy response or stable disease for at least 3 months, at most three lines. CEA <80 ng/mL OR at least 50% reduction from peak was permitted. Independent committee of at least two surgeons, two oncologists and one radiologist reviewed original and current lesion maps. These are trial criteria, not identical to current NLRB guidance.

Results. Primary time origin was randomization. Median follow-up 59.3 months. Five-year ITT OS was 56.6% versus 12.6% (HR 0.37, 95% CI 0.21–0.65); per-protocol OS 73.3% versus 9.3%. Of 47 assigned LT, 38 received LT and 36 were per-protocol; nine had progression-related exclusion/dropout. The control arm included seven subsequent resections and two LT crossovers. Safety population comprised 40 grafted patients. Among 36 per-protocol LT recipients, 26 recurred; 19/26 received chemotherapy and 12/26 local curative-intent treatment. Median per-protocol PFS was 17.4 versus 6.4 months, and secondary PFS after salvage was 35.4 months in the LT arm. Three of 36 per-protocol recipients required retransplantation (primary nonfunction, donor gallbladder cancer, caval obstruction/large-for-size); one postoperative death was reported. Serious adverse events occurred in 32/40 safety LT recipients. Median wait to LT was 50.5 days, which limits transfer to longer-wait systems.

Limits. Strong evidence for the selected trial population, not for BRAF-mutant, ECOG 2, active extrahepatic, truly NED, resectable or broadly expanded disease. PP survival conditions on reaching treatment and must not replace ITT. The Results recurrence denominator is 26/36; Figure 3 caption says 28 recurrences, consistent with a broader all-grafted report, so do not mix these denominators. QoL collection had only 55/74 per-protocol baseline questionnaires and is descriptive, not proof of a large QoL gain. Priority allocation and rapid access were part of the treatment strategy.

Dueland 2026 · SECA-II ten-year

Design and population. Prospective SECA-II extended follow-up, 25 recipients from April 2012 to November 2023; analysis cutoff 1 January 2025. Includes the original SECA-II cohort.

Selection. All grafted under SECA-II included. At least one year diagnosis to inclusion and no progression required; one inadvertently transplanted patient had progression on a scan not available to the transplant centre. One early postoperative death was included in survival analyses but excluded from other analyses, so baseline Table 1 denominator is 24. Among these 24, median largest lesion 24 mm (4–48), CEA 3.5 (1–33), PET MTV 7.3 cm³ (0–139); only two MTV >70, two KRAS-mutated, one right-sided ascending primary and one pN2. Oslo scores 0/1/2 were 9/13/2.

Results. Time zero LT; median follow-up of survivors 97.2 months. Median OS 128.2 months (95% CI 74.8–181.6); five- and ten-year OS 77.8% and 51.5%. Median RFS 24.3 months; 12/25 relapsed, six initially lung-only. Median post-recurrence survival 62.9 months (25.7–101.1); nine had surgery as first recurrence treatment, but only three were classified curative-intent at that first treatment. Twelve had no recurrence over median 74 months; four exceeded ten years without recurrence. Four patients died of metastases more than five years after LT. Reported serious complications: five grade IIIa, three IIIb, one grade V hepatic-artery-thrombosis death.

Limits. No contemporaneous control and only 25 recipients; highly favorable biology cannot justify broad expansion. Abstract speculates up to 15 potentially cured, body p.5 says up to 13; neither is an observed, validated cure fraction. Use observed survival/recurrence instead. The discussion's assertion that RFS is not valid for efficacy should not erase recurrence burden, treatment harms or QoL; RFS is insufficient alone, not unimportant. Updated survival supersedes rather than adds an independent 83% SECA-II estimate.

SECA-II arm D

Design and population. Prospective SECA-II arm D expanded-criteria cohort, ten recipients, without control.

Selection. Expanded both recipient and donor criteria, including resectable pulmonary metastases and omission of previous stringent response/time restrictions. Eight had pN2 primaries, six poorly differentiated or signet-ring pathology, five right-sided cancers; median 20 liver lesions and largest diameter 59 mm. This was a bundle of adverse features, not an isolated prior-lung-metastasis experiment.

Chandra 2026 · HAIP comparator

Design and population. Single-centre retrospective HAIP cohort: 1,110 patients treated in 2006–2016, 483 initially unresectable after exclusions; 83 met modified transplant criteria, but only 23 were pretreated, chemotherapy-controlled transplant-like candidates.

Selection. Selected patients had liver-confined disease without progression on pre-HAIP chemotherapy. The study modified SECA/TransMet criteria: PET, molecular profile, CEA and standardized RECIST response were not mandatory, and primary resection could occur during/after HAIP. Applying at least one year from CRC diagnosis to HAIP left only five of 483 patients. Unresectability considered adequate functional remnant, vascular/biliary preservation and intraoperative ultrasound, not lesion number alone.

Results. Time zero was HAIP placement, not a common LT-versus-HAIP eligibility date. In the 23 selected pretreated patients, 18/23 (78%) converted to resection; only 10/18 were R0, and 17/18 recurred, with salvage treatment in 15. Median follow-up was 97.7 months; median OS 61 months (95% CI 36–92), five-year OS 53% (36–79), PFS 13 months (9.6–22), hepatic PFS 16 months (11–61). Median treatment comprised five HAIP cycles and nine systemic cycles. Biliary sclerosis requiring stents occurred in 3/23 (13%); one pump-arterial dissection required revision.

Limits. Selection from a large denominator, modified eligibility, expert-centre availability, retrospective ascertainment and a different time origin prohibit equivalence claims against TransMet. The high conversion rate is clinically important counterevidence to declaring unresectability before specialist conversion review. It does not make HAIP compulsory or establish that its toxicities are an indication to create for LT.

Wehrle 2024 · intensive LRT

Design and population. Single-centre intensive pre-LT locoregional-treatment protocol: 27 evaluated, 16 included, nine transplanted (seven living donor). Abstract-only verification.

Selection. Median 16 systemic cycles (10–40); Y90 in five, ablation in four, resection in four and HAIP in three. Five grafted patients had no detectable disease and four had treatment-induced cirrhosis.

Results. Median follow-up 700 days; three recurrences. Reported RFS one year 6/8 (75%) and two years 3/5 (60%) reflects very small mature denominators.

Limits. This is actual published experience with NED/injury routes, so it must not be described as nonexistent; it is not controlled proof of benefit or a universal cycle-count requirement. Patients likely overlap occult-explant/PET/ctDNA cohorts. Do not intensify treatment to manufacture liver failure.
