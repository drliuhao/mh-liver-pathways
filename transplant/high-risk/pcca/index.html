Tumor size, biopsy history or previously malignant nodes

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • For the conventional standardized-exception mass criterion, retain a single mass <3.0 cm in radial diameter. Exactly 3.0 cm is not rounded down; obtain central measurement review. Do not activate a 3–5 cm expansion.
  • Exclude any prior transperitoneal primary-tumor biopsy exposure from the initial transplanting study. Record trajectory and whether pathology was positive, negative or nondiagnostic; this is a conservative selection rule, not proof of dissemination in every exposed patient.
  • Exclude previously proven malignant nodes from the initial transplant cohort even after radiographic/pathologic clearance. Nodal EUS sampling that does not traverse the primary is distinct from primary-tumor biopsy. Do not combine these excluded categories in one expansion cohort.
Tests and information to review

Original MRI/MRCP or CT with perpendicular radial measurement; full biopsy reports/needle trajectory; node station and original pathology; operative staging plan; resection assessment. Record whether EUS sampled a regional node or traversed the primary mass, the route, pathology result and date. These are not equivalent exposures.

Allocation and donor route

Clinical review, standardized exception eligibility and research expansion are separate. A favorable response or living donor does not erase the original size/biopsy/node history.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

Size, biopsy exposure and nodal biology must remain separate. Larger-mass LT has directly unfavorable recurrence data. Biopsy evidence is conflicting across settings, so the mechanism is plausible and current policy is restrictive, but an inevitable-seeding claim is unjustified. No reviewed study validates a cleared-node rescue stratum. Imaging-negative nodes do not equal pathological clearance.

Evidence against expansion

Aveson challenges a universal causal inference from Heimbach. Absence of PM in nine negative TP-FNA cases does not establish safety. Soliman's occult-node recipients do not validate deliberate nodal expansion. The exact 3.0-cm boundary is a current policy boundary, not a biological discontinuity proven by these studies.

Certainty

Moderate confidence that conventional selection yields better observed outcomes; low certainty for causal effects of size and biopsy; very low/no direct evidence for a previously positive-node clearance pathway.

Basis for proposed thresholds

POLICY: OPTN 9.5. A specifies radial mass < 3 cm, unresectability, NAT and staging; primary TP-biopsy must be avoided. STUDY: >3 cm associated with lower RFS; positive TP-FNA associated with PM in a very small LT cohort. LOCAL: closing all prior-biopsy and cleared-node strata is a conservative first-study choice, not proof of universal futility.

Darwish Murad 2012

Design and population. Retrospective 12-US-center NAT-to-LT cohort; 287 entered, 71 dropped out, 214 transplanted.

Selection. Mostly conventional localized unresectable/PSC-associated pCCA. The expanded subgroup included larger tumors and other departures from eligibility.

Results. From treatment-pathway entry, 2-/5-year OS was 68%/53%; post-LT 2-/5-year recurrence-free survival 78%/65%. Among 21 transplanted patients with masses >3 cm, 5-year RFS was 32% versus 69% for smaller tumors. The combined outside-criteria group had recurrence hazard ratio 2.98.

Limits. Larger tumors were not randomized. Composite outside-criteria effect cannot be assigned separately to size, biopsy or metastasis. Small >3-cm recipient subset; selection and center effects.

Darwish Murad 2012

Design and population. Mayo 1993–2010; 199 protocol entrants, 62 dropouts, 131 local LT and six elsewhere.

Selection. Localized unresectable disease; historical size definitions were not identical to current policy.

Results. Dropout predictors included >3-cm mass (adjusted HR 2.14, 95% CI 1.08–4.24), CA19-9≥ 500 (HR 2.29), positive/suspicious intraluminal sampling (HR 3.63) and MELD ≥ 20 (HR 3.48). Local recipients had 26 recurrences; 5-year OS 71%, RFS 68% from LT.

Limits. Strong overlap with Murad multicenter and Heimbach cohorts; not independent replication. These are prognostic associations, not validated universal exclusion thresholds.

Heimbach 2011 · biopsy

Design and population. 191 NAT-to-LT protocol entrants; 16 underwent primary-tumor TP-FNA.

Selection. 13 percutaneous and three EUS primary-tumor biopsies; not EUS nodal staging.

Results. Peritoneal metastases at staging: 5/6 with positive TP-FNA versus 14/175 without TP-FNA; 9 negative TP-FNA patients had none; one result was equivocal.

Limits. Only six positive biopsies. Nonrandom sampling and more obvious tumor may confound association. Negative-biopsy subgroup is far too small to establish safety or equivalence.

Aveson 2022

Design and population. MSK retrospective planned-resection cohort 1991–2014; 262 patients, 37 TP biopsy versus 225 intraluminal/no biopsy.

Selection. Resection/exploration setting rather than NAT-to-LT. Median biopsy-to-surgery interval 32 versus 38 days.

Results. Peritoneal metastases at surgery 5.4% versus 7.6%; among 243 initially PM-negative patients, 2-year cumulative PM 20.3% versus 20.1%(p=.7).

Limits. Direct counterevidence to universal biopsy-seeding claims, but shorter exposure interval, no LT waiting/immunosuppression comparison and only 37 exposed patients. Does not overturn allocation policy.

Soliman 2025 · GemCis

Design and population. Houston recipient-only NAT comparison; 27 LT, 26 analyzed after one treatment-intolerance exclusion.

Selection. Pre-LT radiological criteria and staging excluded known nodal/extrahepatic disease; six nevertheless had N1/N2 on explant.

Results. Occult positive nodes in 6/26(23.1%); nodal disease associated with worse OS and RFS. Nine recurrences overall; main-text 1-/3-year OS 83.0%/52.3% from LT.

Limits. Occult nodes discovered after LT are not evidence supporting knowingly positive nodes cleared by therapy. No all referred or all-treated denominator. Abstract/body OS rounding differs; use full Results values.

Dong 2026 · resection versus LT

Design and population. Mayo de-novo pCCA 1993–2023; 127 analyzed LT-pathway entrants, 52 dropouts, 75 LT.

Selection. Protocol staged localized tumors; PSC excluded.

Results. Dropouts included 15 with regional nodal metastases and 34 with distant metastases; three medical deterioration.

Limits. Provides the consequence of detecting nodes, not outcomes of clearing them and transplanting. Overlaps historical Mayo cohorts.

Resection remains a potentially curative option

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Proposed planning target for cholestatic/cirrhotic liver: FLR/TLV >40%, combined with function, drainage and infection assessment. Volume alone never establishes operative safety; 40% is not an OPTN requirement.
  • If insufficient remnant is the only barrier, assess selective drainage and augmentation/PVE; reassess remnant volume and function about 2–4 weeks after PVE. Vascular reconstruction or PVE need alone does not prove unresectability.
  • Return to LT eligibility review only if an R0 resection remains infeasible or unsafe after reasonable optimization. Do not transplant otherwise resectable de novo pCCA outside a specifically approved comparative study.
Tests and information to review

Two specialist HPB/transplant reviews; vascular/ductal map; CT volumetry; functional assessment where available; cholangitis/drainage status; PVE response and reconstruction plan.

Allocation and donor route

Policy 9.5.A requires unresectability from technical considerations or underlying liver disease; comparative recipient survival alone does not satisfy this requirement.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

Evidence supports LT as a serious option for carefully selected unresectable pCCA. It does not resolve LT versus feasible optimized R 0 resection. Ethun favors LT even after restricted ITT analysis, whereas Dong loses statistical advantage when dropout is counted. Neither randomization nor comparable entry clocks remove the confounding. PVE can materially change the surgical risk assessment.

Evidence against expansion

Do not cite Dong's nonsignificant ITT p-value as equivalence or proof that resection must always be better. Conversely, recipient-only LT survival cannot erase donor opportunity cost, NAT dropout and wait-list hazards. Reconstructible vascular involvement or a correctable small FLR alone is not proof of permanent unresectability.

Certainty

Low comparative certainty; stronger but still nonrandomized evidence for FLR optimization before declaring resection unsafe.

Basis for proposed thresholds

POLICY: standardized pCCA exception requires unresectability or underlying liver disease. STUDY: PVE growth was assessed at median 22 days in the matched study; comparisons remain observational. GUIDANCE: CIRSE supports higher remnant targets in cholestatic/cirrhotic liver. LOCAL: >40% planning target, reassessment 2–4 weeks and required expert review are workflow proposals; inadequate function/infection can prevent surgery despite adequate volume.

Dong 2026 · resection versus LT

Design and population. Single-center 1993–2023 de-novo pCCA; 288 intended resection (207 resected, 81 dropout) and 127 intended NAT/LT(75 LT, 52 dropout).

Selection. Resection required attainable R 0 and sufficient FLR; PVE and vascular reconstruction were used. LT recipients were selected for unresectability and conventional tumor biology.

Results. As-treated median OS: LT 78 months, resection without vascular resection 58.2, with vascular resection 25.8; 5-year 58.6%, 49.6%, 26.7%. ITT median OS 38.5 months LT versus 31.7 resection (p=.19).

Limits. Not equivalence and not proof resection is superior. Historical era, selection and dropout differ. Methods measure LT time from NAT enrollment but resection time from surgery, so even ITT clocks are not perfectly aligned; as-treated LT percentages must not be called purely post-LT.

Ethun 2018

Design and population. Multicenter 2000–2015; 234 attempted resections and 70 entered LT protocols; 191 curative-intent resections and 41 confirmed-CCA LT in recipient analysis.

Selection. LT patients were younger (52 vs 65), more often PSC(61% vs 2%), and more often treated with chemo/RT(98% vs 57%).

Results. Recipient 3-/5-year OS 72%/64% LT versus 33%/18% resection. Restricted non-PSC,<3 cm, node-negative comparison favored LT at 5 years 54% vs 29%(p=.03); adjusted/restricted ITT comparison remained significant (p=.049).

Limits. Conflicting observational evidence versus Dong; major selection/era/therapy confounding. Exact ITT exclusions and time origin require full-PDF recheck; no unsupported ITT rate is reproduced here.

Vugts 2021

Design and population. 732 consecutive Dutch pCCA presentations 2002–2014; retrospective application of Mayo eligibility.

Selection. Only 34(5%) potentially LT-eligible after resection/biology/fitness/age screening; not an LT-treated cohort.

Results. Without LT, median survival 13 months (95% CI 3–23) from diagnosis/presentation. This shows unmet need among unresectable selected patients, not benefit over R 0 resection.

Limits. Historical non-LT care; no matched LT comparator, suspected/confirmed nodes pooled and infection criterion incompletely reconstructible. Abstract 24 PSC versus body 29 PSC inconsistency is irrelevant to the decision and not propagated.

Olthof 2020

Design and population. 20-center retrospective 1667 patients; 1484 combined liver/bile-duct resections analyzed; 298 PVE. Complete-data propensity analysis 98 PVE versus 98 controls.

Selection. High-risk major resections; exploration-only and LT excluded.

Results. Matched PVE versus noPVE: ISGLS B/C liver failure 8% vs 36%; 90-day mortality 7% vs 18%; bile leak 10% vs 35%. Median remnant-volume gain 42% after 22 days.

Limits. Unmatched outcomes were worse in PVE patients because they were higher risk. Matching omits unmeasured confounding and excludes missing volumetry; does not prove one FLR cutoff. Operated-only denominator omits patients failing to reach surgery.

Darwish Murad 2012

Design and population. 287 NAT/LT entrants across 12 US centers; 214 transplanted.

Selection. Localized unresectable or PSC-associated disease, not a trial in patients eligible for both R 0 resection and LT.

Results. 5-year pathway-entry OS 53% and post-LT RFS 65% are useful feasibility benchmarks, but answer a different question from randomized LT-versus-resection benefit.

Limits. Substantial overlap with Mayo comparisons; do not count as an independent comparative test.

Alternative treatment before transplantation

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Default to the established fluoropyrimidine/EBRT-based pathway under the approved radiation and pharmacy protocol. Do not treat a pilot dose/fractionation or cycle count as a universal order.
  • Develop a separately governed prospective GemCis-only feasibility/comparative arm for otherwise conventionally selected pCCA. Retain SBRT/capecitabine as another documented pilot option, not the uniquely evidence-preferred alternative. Neither arm is activated by this document.
  • Do not combine either alternative with an initial size, primary transperitoneal-biopsy or previously malignant-node expansion. Keep ICI-containing induction outside these initial arms unless separately approved on pre-LT safety evidence.
  • Before opening, specify regimen delivery, staging, stopping rules, radiation-field and arterial/biliary reconstruction review. Capture every entrant, failure to reach LT, cholangitis, HAT/PVT, biliary injury, graft loss, recurrence and all graft use.
Tests and information to review

Written radiation/chemotherapy plan and dose modifications; drainage/infection plan; biliary/vascular anatomy; actual prior dose maps; operative staging and graft-safety endpoints.

Allocation and donor route

Policy requires a written neoadjuvant protocol and operative staging, not this exact regimen. A regimen study does not relax the standardized tumor criteria.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

Conventional fluoropyrimidine-based chemoradiation remains the most mature benchmark. Alternatives have genuine LT evidence, but none has demonstrated noninferiority. The evidence does not justify singling out SBRT as the only acceptable research alternative while categorically closing GemCis-only. Vascular/biliary harm needs prospective surveillance alongside recurrence and dropout.

Evidence against expansion

RT may reduce recurrence yet increase vascular morbidity. Chemo-only favorable selected-recipient outcomes do not reveal who progressed before LT. No-NAT European recipients are not a rationale to omit all induction in a US exception pathway. GCS/RT and SBRT are not interchangeable.

Certainty

Moderate confidence in feasibility of conventional selected NAT/LT; very low comparative certainty between named regimens. Direct vascular safety signal deserves action despite nonrandomization.

Basis for proposed thresholds

POLICY: OPTN requires an approved written protocol including NAT/staging but does not mandate a particular radiation dose. STUDY: SBRT 50–60 Gy/3–5 fractions and GCS/56 Gy are published pilot recipes only. LOCAL: GemCis-only prospective arm, no simultaneous biology expansion, and initial ICI exclusion are risk-management proposals. No validated minimum cycle count or safe vascular dose threshold is inferred.

Darwish Murad 2012

Design and population. 287 entrants/214 LT across 12 US centers.

Selection. Localized unresectable/PSC-associated pCCA; 99% EBRT, 98% fluoropyrimidine, 75% brachytherapy.

Results. 5-year entry OS 53%; post-LT RFS 65%. No clear RFS difference by brachytherapy receipt was detected.

Limits. Strongest mature multi-center pathway benchmark, but no randomized regimen comparison; lack of detected brachytherapy effect does not establish interchangeability.

Welling 2014 · SBRT

Design and population. Single-center pilot; 17 evaluated, 12 NAT, 9 listed, 6 LT.

Selection. Unresectable localized pCCA under historical conventional size/staging criteria.

Results. SBRT 50–60 Gy in 3–5 fractions over 2 weeks, followed by capecitabine; 6 LT with 1-year OS 83%.35 pre-LT adverse events and 11 post-LT complications were reported.

Limits. Very small feasibility cohort, short oncologic follow-up, no randomized comparator. Regimen is a study description, not a universal radiation prescription or proof SBRT is the preferred alternative.

Soliman 2025 · GemCis

Design and population. Recipient-only retrospective 2008–2023; 26 analyzed LT, 12 GemCis-only, 14 RT-based including six also receiving GemCis.

Selection. Localized radiologically eligible pCCA; known metastatic disease excluded.

Results. Main-text 1-/3-year OS 83.0%/52.3%; 9/26 recurrences; 1-/3-year RFS 77.8%/59.0%. No statistically significant OS or explant-response difference between treatment strata; RT had more operative blood-loss/transfusion signals.

Limits. No pretreatment denominator or differential dropout data; small nonrandom groups, era/selection confounding and occult nodal disease. Supports studying omission, not equivalence. Use main-text values rather than abstract 84%/55%.

Hoogwater 2023 · radiation

Design and population. Multicenter 2011–2020; 49 LT, 27 with NAT, 22 without.

Selection. Similar broad transplant selection but nonrandom center-specific NAT and PSC distributions.

Results. Post-LT 5-year OS 41% NAT versus 53% no NAT(not significant); NAT associated with less recurrence (adjusted HR 0.30, 95% CI 0.09–0.97). Major vascular complications 11/27 vs 3/22; hepatic-artery complications 9/27 vs 2/22.

Limits. Competing recurrence benefit and treatment harm, not proof RT can routinely be omitted. Abstract 9 vs 2 vascular wording should not replace Table 3's broader 11 vs 3 major-vascular count.

Ito 2025 · LDLT

Design and population. Prospective Kyoto pilot; 10 enrolled, 5 LDLT, 5 dropout.

Selection. Unresectable localized pCCA; GemCisS 1 induction plus EBRT, typically 56 Gy/28 fractions.

Results. All 5 recipients alive at 1 year, median follow-up 23.7 months; one recurrence and 2/5 hepatic-artery thromboses. Dropouts included progression, positive nodes, toxicity/complication, financial withdrawal and one conversion to resection.

Limits. Five recipients cannot establish long-term oncologic safety; different allocation/LDLT setting and demanding regimen. This is not support for adding immunotherapy.

Darwish Murad 2012

Design and population. 131 local LT within 199 protocol entrants.

Selection. Mayo conventional NAT; historical exposure overlaps other Mayo reports.

Results. 11/131 required retransplantation, including six for HAT; 5-year post-LT OS 71%, RFS 68%.

Limits. Illustrates clinically significant graft risk; not an independent estimate additive to the multicenter series.

ASCO BTC living guideline 2026.1.0 · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Full 56-page accepted, unedited living-guideline manuscript (October 2026). Advanced BTC systemic care, not pCCA transplant-neoadjuvant therapy or a transplant eligibility guideline.

Broad tissue DNA/RNA profiling, MMR and HER2 testing at diagnosis. For eligible MSS/pMMR disease: GemCis plus durvalumab or pembrolizumab (high-quality/strong); GemCis without ICI if unsuitable (conditional). After progression without an actionable target: FOLFOX plus active symptom control or active symptom control alone (conditional).

For oxaliplatin-ineligible patients, irinotecan/nal-IRI-based options have low-quality conditional support, not equal default status. Rare targets often rely on small basket cohorts. Accepted-manuscript tables/narrative contain inconsistent estimates; primary trial results govern this update.

All 56 pages; recommendations Table 1 pp 40–42; Figure 1 p 34 visually inspected. Separate data supplement not reviewed.

NALIRICC: negative BTC randomized trial

Application. Show NALIRICC alongside NIFTY. Do not call nal-IRI/5-FU an unqualified preferred second-line BTC regimen. Consider only a qualified alternative after target review, especially if oxaliplatin is unsuitable, with explicit benefit/toxicity discussion.

NALIRICC: negative BTC randomized trial · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

German randomized phase II, 100 patients (49 versus 51), ECOG 0–1, after gemcitabine-based therapy; 64% iCCA. nal-IRI plus 5-FU/LV versus 5-FU/LV.

PFS 2.6 versus 2.3 months, HR 0.87 (0.56–1.35); OS 6.9 versus 8.2 months, HR 1.08 (0.68–1.72). ORR 14% versus 4% did not translate into PFS/OS benefit. Grade ≥3 events 71% versus 50%; treatment-related serious events 33% versus 2%.

Small phase II trial; subsequent therapy differed (more control patients received later therapy). This does not erase the negative primary PFS result. Contrasts with positive NIFTY; population and treatment differences do not resolve the inconsistency.

Main PDF pp 1–11; Figure 2 and Tables 1–3.
