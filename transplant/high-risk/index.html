Before any proposed study can open

Clinical selection, allocation review, donor protection and research approval are separate decisions. These shared requirements must be resolved before a proposed cohort is activated.

ALLOC-B1High-risk protocol · Proposed operating requirement

Separate clinical, allocation, donor and study approval

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Complete six separate dated determinations: clinical suitability; standardized-exception eligibility; nonstandard NLRB review status; calculated-MELD access; LDLT donor clearance; and research authorization. Mark nonapplicable domains explicitly with a reason—do not manufacture six approvals for every patient.
  • Any required missing, pending or adverse determination is a HOLD. An exception application is not approval, a donor is not recipient benefit, and clinical approval is not study activation.
  • Recheck the current policy/guidance version before each request; record actual decision and priority rather than predicted entitlement.
Tests and information to review

Signed suitability record; exact allocation route and policy version; exception outcome; donor-team status; study and institutional activation record.

Allocation and donor route

Policy 9.4 permits discretionary requests where appropriate; absence of favorable diagnosis-specific guidance is not absence of a review mechanism, and a request does not promise approval.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

The separation is a governance requirement, not an empirical survival cutoff. A clinically appropriate patient can lack exception eligibility, and an approved exception does not prove clinical benefit. LDLT requires an independent donor determination; a registry entry is not permission to perform experimental transplantation.

Evidence against expansion

Not every case needs six separate affirmative approvals: several routes are alternatives or not applicable. An indiscriminate six-approval rule could obstruct appropriate standard care.

Certainty

High confidence in the separation of applicable authorities; no trial establishes that six boxes improve survival. Local workflow must map to current institutional obligations.

Basis for proposed thresholds

  • Six domains: local checklist architecture, not six mandatory national approvals.
  • 100% applicable completion: governance safeguard, not an outcome-derived percentage.
  • Hold pending required authorization: operational implementation of separate clinical, donor and research responsibilities.

NLRB oncology guidance

Design and population. Current NLRB hepatic-adenoma guidance.

Selection. Four alternative routes; route four contains conjunctive clinical requirements.

Results. GSD/Abernethy; OR unresectable β-catenin-mutated HCA; OR unresectable adenomatosis >10 lesions; OR unresectable, nonoperative-unresponsive and progressive/complicated HCA.

Limits. These are review routes, not proof of benefit or a mandatory transplant recommendation. Current text does not require all four conditions simultaneously or cirrhosis for all routes.

HHS human-subject protections

Design and population. Federal human-subject protection regulation.

Selection. Applicability determined by institutional research/regulatory review.

Results. 46.111 requires risk minimization, reasonable risk–benefit, equitable selection, informed consent and appropriate monitoring. IRB approval is separate from OPTN allocation and clinical acceptance.

Limits. No prescribed ten-patient cap, five-patient interim or one-event statistical boundary; those require protocol-specific design and independent approval.

OPTN ethics principles

Design and population. OPTN Ethics Committee white paper, not a trial or binding allocation algorithm.

Selection. Scarce-organ allocation must balance utility, justice and respect for persons.

Results. High predicted recipient survival alone is insufficient: opportunity cost, equity and access also matter. Donor autonomy does not remove clinical obligations.

Limits. Provides an ethical framework, not numeric weights, a universal survival threshold or permission to depart from allocation rules.

ALLOC-B2High-risk protocol · Proposed operating requirement

Assess expected recipient benefit and organ use

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Proposed requirements: two reviewers not both responsible for recruitment must document expected incremental benefit over the actual contemporary alternative, with unresolved disagreement referred to the full committee.
  • For DDLT, show local expected access/dropout and sensitivity scenarios at 6, 12 and 18 months, total graft use including retransplantation, and plausible alternative recipients. These are planning scenarios, not imposed waiting periods.
  • For LDLT, separately show healthy-donor risk and potential downstream deceased-donor graft use. Do not call DCD, extended-criteria grafts or machine perfusion “zero opportunity cost.” No priority changes follow from an AI-selected survival threshold.
Tests and information to review

Same-eligibility/time-zero comparator; expected access; dropout; donor/graft type; uncertainty intervals; graft loss/retransplant estimate; written independent reviews.

Allocation and donor route

Only the applicable allocation process determines priority. Favorable post-LT prognosis, evidence grade and urgency are not interchangeable measures.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

Use pathway-level benefit from an agreed entry date, not recipient-only survival. Compare with contemporary tumor-specific care and actual local access. LDLT avoids one direct deceased-donor allocation but creates healthy-donor risk and may generate rescue graft use. Graft quality/perfusion changes do not make opportunity cost zero.

Evidence against expansion

An ambitious prognosis threshold can exclude patients with substantial incremental benefit, while an indolent tumor can have excellent post-LT survival and little incremental benefit. Local wait times and unequal access can reverse a study’s apparent advantage.

Certainty

Benefit evidence differs by disease: randomized selected CRLM/HCC pathways cannot be generalized to all expanded indications. Ethical principles are authoritative frameworks, not empirically optimized allocation weights.

Basis for proposed thresholds

  • Two reviewers: local independence safeguard, not validated optimum.
  • 6/12/18 months: sensitivity scenarios for access/dropout, not mandatory waits.
  • HCC ≥60% five-year ITT OS: one expert trial-design benchmark; not a cross-cancer allocation policy.
  • All grafts: count initial and rescue/retransplant grafts; donor morbidity remains a separate outcome.

Kelly 2026

Design and population. National SRTR donor cohort 1989–2023: 8,619 with mortality ascertainment; 8,314 with any recovery record.

Selection. Adults 18–65; 362 eligible donors excluded for missing mortality status.

Results. Eight deaths by 90 days and 18 by two years; contemporary-era perioperative mortality approximately 0.04%. Only 37–39% had two-year recovery data.

Limits. All-cause deaths, mostly unavailable cause; not oncology-specific. Low mortality cannot substitute for complete morbidity, psychological and financial follow-up.

XXL 2020

Design and population. Multicenter randomized phase 2b/3 trial: 74 entered downstaging; 29 dropped out before randomization; 45 responders were randomized, 23 to LT and 22 to nontransplant treatment.

Selection. Child-Pugh A–B7, ECOG 0–1, predicted five-year post-LT survival ≥50%, and ≥3 months sustained response. AFP rules depended on baseline value and response, not an unconditional AFP <400 entry rule.

Results. From randomization, five-year overall survival was 77.5% with LT versus 31.2% without LT; tumor-event-free survival 76.8% versus 18.3%; median follow-up 71 months.

Limits. Strongest causal evidence for LT among selected responders, not for all 74 entrants or unlimited initial burden. Small, prematurely stopped trial. Appendix allowed uncertain bland-versus-neoplastic segmental/type-I portal thrombosis under additional response conditions; the actual PVTT denominator/outcomes are not reported. It must not be relabeled a proven-PVTT trial.

TransMet 2024

Design and population. Multicentre open-label randomized trial, 20 European centres, 157 screened and 94 randomized (47 LT-plus-chemotherapy, 47 chemotherapy); 63 screened excluded, including 13 with equivocal unresectability and 36 with progression.

Selection. Age 18–65; ECOG 0–1; R0 colorectal primary; BRAF nonmutated; no prior or current extrahepatic disease/local recurrence; chemotherapy response or stable disease for at least 3 months, at most three lines. CEA <80 ng/mL OR at least 50% reduction from peak was permitted. Independent committee of at least two surgeons, two oncologists and one radiologist reviewed original and current lesion maps. These are trial criteria, not identical to current NLRB guidance.

Results. Primary time origin was randomization. Median follow-up 59.3 months. Five-year ITT OS was 56.6% versus 12.6% (HR 0.37, 95% CI 0.21–0.65); per-protocol OS 73.3% versus 9.3%. Of 47 assigned LT, 38 received LT and 36 were per-protocol; nine had progression-related exclusion/dropout. The control arm included seven subsequent resections and two LT crossovers. Safety population comprised 40 grafted patients. Among 36 per-protocol LT recipients, 26 recurred; 19/26 received chemotherapy and 12/26 local curative-intent treatment. Median per-protocol PFS was 17.4 versus 6.4 months, and secondary PFS after salvage was 35.4 months in the LT arm. Three of 36 per-protocol recipients required retransplantation (primary nonfunction, donor gallbladder cancer, caval obstruction/large-for-size); one postoperative death was reported. Serious adverse events occurred in 32/40 safety LT recipients. Median wait to LT was 50.5 days, which limits transfer to longer-wait systems.

Limits. Strong evidence for the selected trial population, not for BRAF-mutant, ECOG 2, active extrahepatic, truly NED, resectable or broadly expanded disease. PP survival conditions on reaching treatment and must not replace ITT. The Results recurrence denominator is 26/36; Figure 3 caption says 28 recurrences, consistent with a broader all-grafted report, so do not mix these denominators. QoL collection had only 55/74 per-protocol baseline questionnaires and is descriptive, not proof of a large QoL gain. Priority allocation and rapid access were part of the treatment strategy.

Allen 2024

Design and population. UK national liver-offering policy evaluation: separate simulation dataset of 1,287 registrations/629 donors plus early real-world NLOS implementation, 2018–2020.

Selection. Transplant-benefit scoring governed national deceased-donor offers; not a high-risk-HCC validation sample.

Results. Simulation compared observed 2013 deaths/removals 93 (7%) and 4,581 patient-years with modeled TBS outcomes 48 (4%) and 5,262 patient-years. During the first two implementation years, 68% of DBD livers were allocated by TBS; observed waitlist mortality decreased (P<.0001), postlisting survival improved (P=.005), with no adverse post-LT survival signal.

Limits. Simulated gains are not observed outcomes. Before/after implementation is nonrandomized and can reflect secular changes. Nonetheless it is meaningful evidence that a governed benefit-based system can be deployed, unlike a claim that all such models are unusable.

ALLOC-B3High-risk protocol · Proposed operating requirement

Protect the living donor

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Proposed readiness requirements: independent donor evaluation/advocacy, documented freedom to withdraw, recipient-benefit uncertainty consent, and funded medical, psychological, recovery and financial follow-up before investigational LDLT.
  • Proposed local follow-up: baseline, six weeks, six months, one year, two years, then annually through five years. Meet current OPTN reporting duties separately; this five-year support plan is not claimed to be a national reporting requirement.
  • One donor death, life-threatening complication, permanent major disability or liver failure requiring transplantation triggers an immediate pause of the relevant investigational LDLT activity and independent review. Other serious donor harm also warrants review; reopening needs independent authorization.
Tests and information to review

Graft/resection-specific and center-specific risk; donor recovery/employment baseline; independent consent and advocate records; funded follow-up/support; sentinel-event response owner.

Allocation and donor route

LDLT clearance is separate from recipient selection; the potential need for retransplantation must be included in organ-use discussions.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

Donor mortality is low but not zero; complication, recovery and financial burdens are more frequent. Registry missingness and selected healthy donors limit reassurance. Consent must include uncertain recipient benefit, recurrence and possible early graft loss, without pressure to proceed or relax donor criteria.

Evidence against expansion

Older A2ALL complication rates and modern SRTR mortality are different outcomes from overlapping eras/cohorts; do not pool them. Five-year support is a reasonable local response to late morbidity, not a proven optimal schedule. Social vulnerability should trigger assistance, not categorical exclusion.

Certainty

Moderate certainty for the existence of material donor burden; lower certainty for individual risk and long-term recovery because of incomplete follow-up. No oncology-specific randomized donor-safety comparison.

Basis for proposed thresholds

  • Baseline/6 weeks/6 months/1/2 years then annually to five: proposed support schedule; current mandatory reporting remains separately checked.
  • One sentinel event: conservative review/pause trigger, not a statistically estimated harm boundary.
  • No relaxed donor criteria: protection against shifting uncertain recipient benefit onto a healthy person.

Abecassis 2012

Design and population. A2ALL cohort: 760 donor operations begun; 740 completed, mostly right lobes.

Selection. Multicenter selected donors, older era; standardized complication ascertainment.

Results. 296/740 (40%) had ≥1 complication; 20/760 operations aborted. Five grade-3 residual-disability events and three grade-4 deaths were reported; two deaths were late, not perioperative.

Limits. Different grading/eras from modern cohorts; A2ALL partly overlaps US registry data. Late hernia/psychological events support follow-up beyond surgical recovery.

DiMartini 2017

Design and population. Prospective A2ALL-2 interviews: 271/297 eligible donors, before and through 24 months after donation.

Selection. Costs and social recovery reported by participants, not randomized financial support.

Results. Across follow-up, 75% reported nonmedical out-of-pocket expenses and 45% lost wages. Clinical recovery alone therefore misses an important burden of donation.

Limits. Response/recall and selected-center limitations. Does not validate a particular five-year schedule or predict an individual donor’s financial outcome.

Xiao 2024

Design and population. Published meta-analysis of 60,829 donors; primary abstract reports 87 articles.

Selection. Heterogeneous centers, eras and complication definitions; full overlap audit pending.

Results. Pooled any complication 24.7% (95% CI 21.6–28.1), major 5.5% (4.5–6.7); mortality 0.06% among 49,027 with mortality data.

Limits. Primary-abstract extraction; not an independent extra 60,829 patients beyond registries. Do not combine with Kelly/A2ALL or promise these rates locally.

ALLOC-B4High-risk protocol · Proposed operating requirement

Monitor all patients considered for the study

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Proposed initial cap: ten transplants per separately approved biological stratum. Review after the first five have 90-day follow-up; no expansion decision until all first ten have at least 12-month follow-up. Reconcile vital status, graft events and known serious harms for every participant; require ≥90% completeness for other prespecified key outcomes.
  • In an initial experimental cohort, one all-cause recipient death, graft loss or retransplantation within 90 days triggers a pause of further experimental enrollment/transplantation pending independent review. A later possibly treatment-related death/graft loss or a donor sentinel event also triggers review/pause. Rescue care is never withheld; reopening requires independent authorization.
  • These are proposed conservative review triggers, not statistically validated efficacy/futility boundaries. Before opening, biostatistics and the independent board must ratify or replace them with indication-specific rules, denominators, pause authority and reopening criteria.
  • Capture every screened candidate, including nonrecipients, conversion to resection, dropout, deaths and access barriers; report survival from entry and from LT, recurrence, graft use, donor recovery and quality of life. Ten survivors alone do not establish benefit or authorize expansion.
Tests and information to review

Locked eligibility strata; prospective entry/time-zero dataset; modern comparator; adverse-event definitions; follow-up-completeness report; independently signed statistical and safety plan.

Allocation and donor route

Study monitoring never changes exception criteria or guarantees future access. Audit access by referral, geography, resources and donor availability without turning protected characteristics into exclusion rules.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

A small pilot can test feasibility, staging fidelity, data capture and acute safety, not prove durable survival benefit. Define time zero, all-screened enrollment, waitlist failures, post-LT survival/recurrence, competing death, all grafts, donor harms and patient-reported outcomes before opening. An independent board needs actual pause/reopening authority.

Evidence against expansion

Zero observed events among ten fully followed independent patients still has a one-sided exact 95% binomial upper bound of 25.9% (1−0.05^(1/10)). Thus ten survivors cannot establish a <5% event risk. Ninety percent data completeness permits one missing patient; safety events and vital status require active ascertainment, not tolerance of unaccounted loss.

Certainty

Strong methodological rationale for predefinition and ITT accounting; no empirical validation of our exact cap/interim/completeness thresholds. Late recurrence demands multi-year follow-up.

Basis for proposed thresholds

  • Ten per stratum and interim after five at 90 days: local feasibility cap and safety review cadence.
  • Twelve-month minimum: earliest expansion discussion, not proof of long-term cancer benefit.
  • ≥90%: proposed non-vital key-measure completeness threshold; not permission to ignore deaths/graft events or unknown status.
  • One early death/graft loss/re-LT: review/pause trigger, not automatic futility or withholding rescue.

SYS-TARE 2024

Design and population. Prospective institutional SYS-TARE pathway; 13 entered, 4 LT, 9 dropout.

Selection. Single bulky measurable iCCA±peritumoral satellites; diffuse/multisegment multifocal disease excluded; intrahepatic vessel encasement permitted without tumor thrombus.

Results. Four recipients alive without recurrence at 73, 40, 12, 8 months; dropouts: three unrecovered intrahepatic progression, four with extrahepatic disease, two occult nodes.

Limits. Only 4 recipients; no validated maximum diameter or comparative proof of satellites being equivalent to a solitary tumor.

Yaqub 2025

Design and population. Norwegian prospective early report; 7 referred, 6 enrolled, 5 LT.

Selection. Response-selected liver-confined iCCA; median imaging lesion count 7, median diameter 11.5 cm; no size/number cap.

Results. All five alive at 10–55 months; 2 recurrences at 12/13 months, both patients with >20 lesions. One HAT required re-LT; one occult 2-mm positive node.

Limits. Research letter, very small denominator; recurrence pattern argues caution with diffuse multifocal disease, not a validated lesion-count cutoff.
