Use this review when a finding falls outside the standard pathway or needs a separate assessment of benefit, safety or study eligibility.

Describe the distribution before considering an expanded route

One mass with adjacent satellite nodules
A dominant mass with nearby peritumoral nodules. Record their relationship and exact distribution; nearby nodules may still represent spread within the liver.
Multifocal disease
Separate tumor foci, including lesions in other liver segments. This describes the imaging pattern; it does not prove whether they arose independently.
Intrahepatic metastases
Spread from the primary tumor to other locations in the liver. This is a biological interpretation, supported by the full imaging, pathology and clinical picture, rather than a synonym for every additional nodule.

The initial advanced-disease proposal permits a single mass-forming focus with adjacent peritumoral satellites; it excludes separate multifocal tumors in different segments. This is a proposed study boundary, not a validated universal transplant rule. Preserve the actual lesion map and uncertainty.

iCCA-B1High-risk protocol · Proposed study cohort

Advanced disease: tumor distribution and grade

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Proposed advanced entry phenotype: pure iCCA, G1–2, one mass-forming focus with or without adjacent peritumoral satellites; no separate multifocal deposits across distinct segments. No universal 8 cm/three-lesion rule is adopted.
  • Exclude known G3/poor differentiation, mixed HCC-iCCA, macroscopic tumor thrombus, proven malignant nodes, peritoneal or other extrahepatic disease from the initial advanced pilot. Negative core sampling cannot guarantee the whole tumor grade; record this limitation.
  • Intrahepatic vascular encasement without tumor thrombus may be considered if complete explant/reconstruction is technically feasible. Larger size needs individualized technical/biological review, not an automatic permission.
  • Do not apply the advanced-study mixed-histology exclusion to the separate NLRB small-cirrhotic route. Preserve ≤3 cm guidance wording and flag the exactly-3.0 cm clinical discrepancy with <3 cm candidate-evaluation prose.
Tests and information to review

Expert core-pathology review including pure versus mixed and grade; MRI/CT segment map; chest/systemic staging; nodal resolution; vascular thrombus versus encasement; resection review.

Allocation and donor route

Advanced noncirrhotic or larger disease does not inherit the small-cirrhotic favorable NLRB pathway. Organ-route and research decisions remain explicit.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

Small incidental iCCA/mixed disease cannot simply be pooled with known advanced pure iCCA. Large unifocal tumors can have durable post-LT survival after selection, but recurrence remains common and the screened-to-LT yield is low. Multifocality and poor differentiation are adverse signals; their precise exclusion boundaries remain unvalidated. Known advanced mixed tumors have particularly weak prospective support.

Evidence against expansion

Houston included multifocal/G3 explants and TESLA intentionally enrolled extensive burden; therefore G1–2/unifocal restrictions are local conservatism, not universal trial criteria. Conversely, small mixed-tumor success does not rescue beyond-UCSF outcomes or validate advanced mixed inclusion. Negative limited biopsy cannot eliminate heterogeneous high-grade/mixed components.

Certainty

Low for small retrospective cohorts; very low for benefit and optimal thresholds in advanced disease. No randomized advanced-iCCA LT comparison or validated universal diameter/lesion-count limit.

Basis for proposed thresholds

GUIDANCE: current NLRB small-cirrhotic unresectable solitary iCCA/mixed ≤ 3 cm after 6 months stability is a separate reviewed-exception lane; AASLD candidate wording uses < 3 cm. STUDY: SYS-TARE/Rennes use restricted burden; Houston/TESLA use broader burden. LOCAL: G1–2 and unifocal±adjacent satellites is a conservative first-study definition, not an evidence-proven optimum.

Sapisochin 2016 · very early iCCA

Design and population. International retrospective explant-defined pure iCCA; 48 recipients, 15 very-early solitary ≤ 2 cm, 33 larger/multifocal.

Selection. Cirrhosis and predominantly incidental/misclassified tumors, not a planned advanced-iCCA induction pathway.

Results. Five-year post-LT OS 65% very-early versus 45% advanced; 5-year recurrence 18% versus 61%.

Limits. Small retrospective pathological strata, not validation of ≤ 3 cm guidance or response-selected large disease. Preoperative sampling/recognition differs from known iCCA.

De Martin 2020

Design and population. Three-center retrospective cirrhosis cohort; 49 LT versus 26 resection, iCCA/mixed ≤ 5 cm.

Selection. All LT diagnoses were incidental; 44/49 presumed HCC, 5 decompensated cirrhosis.

Results. From surgery, 5-year OS 67% LT versus 40% resection (p=.17), RFS 75% vs 36%(p=.004), recurrence 18% vs 46%. Differentiation independently predicted recurrence (HR 3.74).

Limits. Not proof to prospectively transplant known 5-cm pure/mixed disease; heterogeneous histology, small numbers, per-protocol selection.

Dageforde 2021

Design and population. 12-center retrospective; 208 mixed tumors, 99 LT and 109 resection; 67 LT within Milan, 14 beyond Milan/within UCSF, 18 beyond UCSF.

Selection. Only 2/99 LT mixed diagnoses known preoperatively; most transplanted for presumed HCC.

Results. Within Milan 5-year post-LT OS 70.1%, recurrence 23.1%; HCC comparators 73.4%/11.5%. Beyond UCSF mixed 5-year OS 15.8%. Within Milan LT versus resection 5-year DFS 70.3% vs 33.6%.

Limits. Benefit is concentrated in small selected disease; does not validate advanced known mixed inclusion. Explant classification and preoperative misdiagnosis constrain applicability.

McMillan 2022 · Houston update

Design and population. 65 referred, 37 listed; five converted to resection, 32 LT-pathway analysis, 18 LT.

Selection. No imposed size/number limits; 10/18 multifocal, median cumulative burden 10.4 cm; six poorly differentiated on explant.

Results. Five-year OS 49% all 32 and 57% recipient subgroup, each FROM LISTING; 7/18 recurrences.

Limits. Overlap with Lunsford; selection and immortal-time bias. Inclusion of G3/multifocal disease does not establish their safety, but contradicts claiming such patients were universally excluded by evidence.

SYS-TARE 2024

Design and population. Prospective institutional SYS-TARE pathway; 13 entered, 4 LT, 9 dropout.

Selection. Single bulky measurable iCCA±peritumoral satellites; diffuse/multisegment multifocal disease excluded; intrahepatic vessel encasement permitted without tumor thrombus.

Results. Four recipients alive without recurrence at 73, 40, 12, 8 months; dropouts: three unrecovered intrahepatic progression, four with extrahepatic disease, two occult nodes.

Limits. Only 4 recipients; no validated maximum diameter or comparative proof of satellites being equivalent to a solitary tumor.

Yaqub 2025

Design and population. Norwegian prospective early report; 7 referred, 6 enrolled, 5 LT.

Selection. Response-selected liver-confined iCCA; median imaging lesion count 7, median diameter 11.5 cm; no size/number cap.

Results. All five alive at 10–55 months; 2 recurrences at 12/13 months, both patients with >20 lesions. One HAT required re-LT; one occult 2-mm positive node.

Limits. Research letter, very small denominator; recurrence pattern argues caution with diffuse multifocal disease, not a validated lesion-count cutoff.

Giguet 2026

Design and population. Rennes retrospective SIRT pathway; 147 treated, 44 younger controlled cases, 19 resections, eight listed including two external referrals, six LT.

Selection. Large predominantly unifocal tumors; median baseline total 10 cm; diffuse multifocal, MVI and EHD excluded.

Results. Post-LT 5-year OS 100%, PFS 44.4%; 3/6 recurrences.

Limits. Only six recipients over 15 years; eight refers to listing, not completed LT. No size cap validated.

iCCA-B2High-risk protocol · Proposed response requirements

Response, progression and the observation period

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Proposed clock: ≥6 consecutive months of radiological response/stability from initiation of the effective strategy after the most recent confirmed progression, with baseline imaging, no extrahepatic disease and persistent unresectability. Image approximately every eight weeks and restage at completion.
  • Dose reduction, toxicity-driven substitution or planned consolidation does not automatically reset the clock when control is continuous. Confirmed liver-only progression pauses eligibility; allow one separately reported salvage re-entry after a fresh six months of control.
  • Further progression after that salvage strategy, proven malignant nodes or extrahepatic spread excludes the patient from the initial study. Reassess resection after each major response and immediately before LT; conversion to feasible curative resection redirects to resection.
Tests and information to review

Dated regimen/response timeline; comparable liver imaging and systemic staging; RECIST and multidisciplinary adjudication; CA19-9 in obstruction context; repeated HPB review. Record latest confirmed progression, current effective-strategy start, first control, each imaging assessment and the proposed activation date; repeat R0/functional-FLR review after meaningful response.

Allocation and donor route

This is the advanced-study clock; do not substitute it for the separate small-cirrhotic NLRB observation/exception requirements.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

A six-month control requirement is a defensible tested entry rule, not an established biological truth. SYS-TARE, TESLA and Rennes used different selection intervals. Stable measurable disease can qualify without RECIST partial response. New lesions or extrahepatic spread cannot be dismissed as stable biology. Regression can change the correct operation to resection.

Evidence against expansion

Successful later-line cases refute a categorical ban on all salvage but do not validate unrestricted repeated resets. Patients controlled for six months already have better non-LT prognosis. Comparing their post-LT survival with unselected advanced-cancer median survival exaggerates benefit. Unresolved lung, bone or nodal findings require adjudication before activation.

Certainty

Very low certainty for the optimal control clock, imaging metric or allowable salvage count; low certainty for sustained control as a useful selection approach.

Basis for proposed thresholds

STUDY: six months in Houston/Rennes, four months in SYS-TARE and twelve months of treatment response in the TESLA flow diagram. LOCAL: approximately eight-week monitoring and one salvage re-entry. GUIDANCE: preserve the separate NLRB small-cirrhotic clock ('from diagnosis or last treatment'); do not silently replace it with the advanced research clock.

Lunsford 2018 · Houston

Design and population. Prospective case series: 21 referrals, 12 accepted and six LT recipients.

Selection. Unresectable, liver-confined pure iCCA without macrovascular invasion. Six months of response/stability required; second/third-line therapy allowed after six months of control on that regimen.

Results. Median diagnosis-to-LT interval 26 months. Post-LT 1-/3-/5-year OS 100%/83.3%/83.3%; five-year CI 27.3–97.5. Three of six recurred, median 7.6 months; RFS 50%.

Limits. Six highly selected recipients, wide intervals and substantial pre-LT survival. Does not establish six months as optimal or one salvage line as the maximum.

McMillan 2022 · Houston update

Design and population. Expanded Houston cohort, overlapping Lunsford.

Selection. Six-month gemcitabine-based induction; liver progression deferred LT, with reconsideration after control; extrahepatic progression prompted delisting.

Results. Five listed patients converted to resection; seven others delisted and seven remained listed at cutoff.

Limits. No timing or salvage comparator; keep referral, listing and LT clocks distinct.

SYS-TARE 2024

Design and population. Thirteen entrants; four LT recipients.

Selection. At least four months of response/stability after systemic treatment/Y 90 before staging; renewed control after salvage could qualify.

Results. All four recipients had RECIST stable disease and Choi response, with median density reduction 52.5%. One FGFR 2-targeted pemigatinib rescue reached LT. Two of six staged patients had positive nodes.

Limits. One rescue does not validate a general reset rule. PET-negative imaging cannot replace pathological staging.

Yaqub 2025

Design and population. Early prospective report; five LT recipients.

Selection. The referral-flow figure specifies twelve months of treatment response; median diagnosis-to-LT interval 26 months.

Results. Two extensively multifocal patients recurred despite prolonged selection.

Limits. Supplementary eligibility needs checking; time alone does not neutralize burden.

Giguet 2026

Design and population. Six-recipient retrospective series.

Selection. At least six months of response/stability, CT/MRI every 2–3 months; median diagnosis-to-listing 351 days.

Results. Nineteen controlled younger patients underwent secondary resection.

Limits. Observed longer selection does not prove a twelve-month requirement.

Schleimer 2026 · non-LT comparator

Design and population. Two-center retrospective: 1, 407 total iCCA, 327 advanced liver-confined; 180 progressed/died before six months, 47 converted to resection, 39 potentially LT-eligible.

Selection. Response-selected non-LT patients; 22 of 39 received HAIP.

Results. Median survival 39 months from diagnosis; 3-/5-year survival 46%/26% from the six-month estimated-eligibility landmark.

Limits. Favorable biology selects survivors before any LT. Historical 2008–2018 cohort, not a randomized treatment comparison.

PUMP-2

Design and population. Prospective phase II: 50 pump placements, 48 received FUDR, 37 concurrent GemCis.

Selection. Regional/systemic treatment for selected unresectable iCCA, not an LT trial.

Results. Partial response 44%, disease control at six months 84%; five resected, one pathological complete response. Median survival 22.3 months, three-year 31.5%.

Limits. Does not validate a pre-LT clearance test or subsequent arterial safety. Reassess resection after response.

ASCO BTC living guideline 2026.1.0 · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Full 56-page accepted, unedited living-guideline manuscript (October 2026). Advanced BTC systemic care, not pCCA transplant-neoadjuvant therapy or a transplant eligibility guideline.

Broad tissue DNA/RNA profiling, MMR and HER2 testing at diagnosis. For eligible MSS/pMMR disease: GemCis plus durvalumab or pembrolizumab (high-quality/strong); GemCis without ICI if unsuitable (conditional). After progression without an actionable target: FOLFOX plus active symptom control or active symptom control alone (conditional).

For oxaliplatin-ineligible patients, irinotecan/nal-IRI-based options have low-quality conditional support, not equal default status. Rare targets often rely on small basket cohorts. Accepted-manuscript tables/narrative contain inconsistent estimates; primary trial results govern this update.

All 56 pages; recommendations Table 1 pp 40–42; Figure 1 p 34 visually inspected. Separate data supplement not reviewed.

NALIRICC: negative BTC randomized trial

Application. Show NALIRICC alongside NIFTY. Do not call nal-IRI/5-FU an unqualified preferred second-line BTC regimen. Consider only a qualified alternative after target review, especially if oxaliplatin is unsuitable, with explicit benefit/toxicity discussion.

NALIRICC: negative BTC randomized trial · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

German randomized phase II, 100 patients (49 versus 51), ECOG 0–1, after gemcitabine-based therapy; 64% iCCA. nal-IRI plus 5-FU/LV versus 5-FU/LV.

PFS 2.6 versus 2.3 months, HR 0.87 (0.56–1.35); OS 6.9 versus 8.2 months, HR 1.08 (0.68–1.72). ORR 14% versus 4% did not translate into PFS/OS benefit. Grade ≥3 events 71% versus 50%; treatment-related serious events 33% versus 2%.

Small phase II trial; subsequent therapy differed (more control patients received later therapy). This does not erase the negative primary PFS result. Contrasts with positive NIFTY; population and treatment differences do not resolve the inconsistency.

Main PDF pp 1–11; Figure 2 and Tables 1–3.

Kobayashi 2018: combined HCC-CCA

Application. Supports cautious platinum-based treatment discussion and expert pathology/molecular review, not a definitive superiority claim or transplant-selection benefit. Adding ICI is extrapolation and requires LT-intent safety review.

Kobayashi 2018: combined HCC-CCA · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Retrospective 36 patients at 15 Japanese centers, 2002–2015, pathologically diagnosed unresectable/recurrent combined HCC-CCA. GemCis n=12, fluoropyrimidine/cisplatin n=11, sorafenib n=5, other n=8.

Overall OS 8.9 months, PFS 2.8 months, ORR 5.6%. Pooled platinum regimens had longer observed OS than sorafenib; adjusted sorafenib-versus-platinum mortality HR 15.83 (2.25–111.43). All seven sorafenib exposures including later line had progression.

Tiny nonrandomized groups, major confounding and very wide CI. Abstract and body reverse individual GemCis/5-FU-cisplatin median ordering; do not present those medians as settled. Old pathology classification; no modern ICI comparison.

All nine main-PDF pages; Table 2 and Figure 3 p 5 visually inspected; Table 3 p 6.

iCCA-B3High-risk protocol · Study development only

Living-donor transplantation: study readiness

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Before this study can open: all six readiness domains complete—approved recipient rules; independent benefit/comparator review; separate donor/advocate clearance; explicit uncertainty/graft-use consent; funded donor follow-up; and an all-screened registry with independently approved safety monitoring.
  • Donor availability never lowers iCCA-B1/B2 selection or control requirements. Retain ordinary donor safety criteria and independent withdrawal rights.
  • Do not use predicted five-year survival ≥50%, or another unvalidated number, as proof of readiness. Until approvals are complete: screening/registry and study development only, no protocol-directed LT.
Tests and information to review

Written study, statistical/safety plan, non-LT alternatives including HAI/conversion, donor resources, consent forms and documented independent approvals.

Allocation and donor route

LDLT avoids allocation of that deceased-donor graft but adds healthy-donor risk and potential retransplant demand; it does not itself prove net benefit.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

The seven-recipient LDLT experience supports developing a carefully governed study, not announcing a standard indication. A shorter wait may avoid dropout but can also remove the biological observation that selected successful cohorts. Donor availability does not erase recipient uncertainty or non-LT alternatives. Benefit and donor harm require separate analyses and independent consent.

Evidence against expansion

No randomized advanced-iCCA LDLT-benefit trial or validated minimum five-year survival forecast establishes study readiness. Response-selected non-LT patients may live for years. National mortality is small but nonzero and does not capture all financial, psychological or functional burden. Seven living donors reported alive is not a safety guarantee.

Certainty

Very low certainty for causal LDLT benefit and the disease-specific donor-risk tradeoff; moderate confidence in broad national donor-mortality estimates, with limited individual transferability.

Basis for proposed thresholds

POLICY: donor independence, informed consent and withdrawal protections remain mandatory. STUDY: seven-recipient LDLT feasibility with wide confidence intervals; no validated minimum survival forecast. LOCAL: assessment of the six readiness domains. Recipient boundaries and safety stopping rules require prospective institutional approval before the first pair.

Sethi 2026 · iCCA LDLT

Design and population. Retrospective single-center seven-recipient LDLT series.

Selection. Six received systemic treatment, four locoregional treatment; median MELD 8 and wait 38 days.

Results. Median follow-up 5.1 years; two recurrences, one death. Five-year OS 85.7% (CI 33.4–97.9), RFS 71.4% (CI 25.8–92.0). Two gradeIII complications within six months; all donors alive.

Limits. Abstract independently checked; full selection, screened denominator and donor morbidity require PDF. Seven recipients cannot establish net benefit.

Lunsford 2018 · Houston

Design and population. Twenty-one referrals, twelve accepted, six recipients.

Selection. Six-month controlled, liver-confined unresectable iCCA.

Results. Five-year post-LT survival 83.3% with very wide CI; three of six recurred.

Limits. Feasibility, not LDLT-specific causal benefit; overlaps McMillan.

Giguet 2026

Design and population. Six selected deceased-donor recipients.

Selection. French declined-graft route, not LDLT.

Results. Arterial complications 3/6 and biliary 4/6; one late retransplant and one septic-cholangitis death at 6.4 years despite five-year OS 100%.

Limits. High survival is not low morbidity or cure; SIRT, surgery and graft-quality effects cannot be separated.

Schleimer 2026 · non-LT comparator

Design and population. Thirty-nine selected non-LT patients from 327 advanced liver-confined cases; 22 received HAIP.

Selection. Retrospective LT-eligibility assessment after six months of control.

Results. Median 39 months from diagnosis; five-year survival 26% from the six-month landmark.

Limits. More relevant than unselected metastatic BTC but historical and nonrandomized; cannot yield precise individual LT benefit.

PUMP-2

Design and population. Fifty pump placements; 48 received FUDR; single-arm phaseII.

Selection. Non-LT unresectable iCCA.

Results. Median survival 22.3 months; three-year 31.5%; five conversion resections. Arterial dissection prevented treatment in one patient.

Limits. A current regional option, not a randomized LDLT comparator or proof of later graft-arterial safety.

Oh 2025

Design and population. Randomized phaseIII advanced BTC: 685 participants, 341 durvalumab/GemCis and 344 placebo/GemCis.

Selection. Mixed biliary sites and locally advanced/metastatic disease, not a six-month LT-selected population.

Results. Median OS 12.9 versus 11.3 months; HR 0.74 (CI 0.63–0.87), 36-month OS 14.6% versus 6.9%, median follow-up 41.3 months.

Limits. High-level evidence for non-LT therapy, not pre-LT ICI safety or an exchangeable control for LT recipients.

Kelly 2026

Design and population. SRTR 1989–2023: 8, 981 eligible donors, 8, 619 mortality analysis and 8, 314 secondary follow-up.

Selection. Donors aged 18–65 across all indications, not iCCA-specific.

Results. Eight 90-day deaths (0.1%), 18 within two years (0.2%). Contemporary 90-day mortality approximately 0.04%. Secondary-outcome completeness 60–67% at six months and 37–39% at two years.

Limits. All-cause deaths are not all donation-attributable. Missingness, historical eras and mixed graft types limit individual risk estimates; employment burden also matters.
