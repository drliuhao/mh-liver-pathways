HCC-B1High-risk protocol · Proposed study cohort

Tumor burden beyond UNOS downstaging criteria

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Proposed entry ceiling: original pretreatment tumor count plus largest tumor diameter in cm ≤12; AFP <100 ng/mL; Child-Pugh A; no macrovascular invasion, extrahepatic disease or unmeasurable infiltrative tumor. These restrictions apply to the expanded arm, not to otherwise standard-eligible HCC.
  • Before LT: viable tumor burden within Milan, or complete nonviability, with at least six months of documented stability from the first assessment meeting that target. Repeat liver CT/MRI and AFP at least every three months; confirmed progression holds the route and resets the study control clock after new successful downstaging.
  • Preserve baseline measurements and AFP. A prior AFP ≥100 or Child-Pugh B/C at expanded-arm entry belongs to a separately approved stratum; do not erase that history after response. Do not combine this expansion with the portal-vein tumor invasion study.
Tests and information to review

Central review of pretreatment and current multiphasic CT/MRI; chest CT; dated AFP series; Child-Pugh components; resection/ablation assessment; dated response and progression record. Record first treatment, first successful downstaging, latest progression, listing, first exception request and LT dates separately; report all-entry, waitlist and recipient outcomes with their own time origins.

Allocation and donor route

Reaching Milan/T2 does not retrospectively create standardized downstaging entry eligibility. A nonstandard exception request, calculated-MELD route or LDLT review remains separate; apply the actual allocation clock.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

Evidence supports offering a bounded downstaging pathway to selected beyond-UNOS patients, not unrestricted transplantation. Attrition before LT materially changes the counseling estimate: survival among recipients is consistently better than survival from treatment entry. Randomized XXL supports LT after sustained response but does not validate the proposed baseline burden/AFP/reserve combination. The ≤12, AFP <100, Child-Pugh A pilot envelope is a deliberately conservative candidate rule derived from observational risk signals. Observation is a test of behavior, not proof that dormant metastases are absent.

Evidence against expansion

Favorable MERITS recipient survival and the randomized responder benefit argue against declaring all beyond-UNOS disease futile. Conversely, high dropout in historical all-comers, worse entry survival, increased long-term recurrence and explant understaging argue against accepting a radiographic Milan endpoint alone. Child-Pugh B/C patients may need transplantation most, yet poorer downstaging tolerance makes an initial Child-Pugh-A-only pilot a local safety restriction rather than a universal oncologic exclusion.

Certainty

Moderate for a benefit of LT in carefully selected sustained responders, limited by one small responder-randomized trial; low for the exact expanded-entry limits; very low for a jointly validated ≤12/AFP <100/Child-Pugh A/six-month rule. This is an informal certainty assessment, not a formal GRADE exercise.

Basis for proposed thresholds

Number-plus-largest-diameter ≤12 and AFP <100/Child-Pugh A: cohort-derived risk refinement, not validated cutoff. Six months: proposed conservative operational observation period; studies used differing clocks and intervals. Imaging/AFP every three months: proposed monitoring cadence. A five-year ≥60% intention-to-treat target is a research-design benchmark, not an achieved or validated eligibility estimate for this pilot. The clinical stability clock must remain separate from OPTN/NLRB exception timing.

Yao 2015

Design and population. Single-center prospective downstaging protocol; 118 downstaging patients versus 488 initially T2 controls, treated in 2002–January 2012.

Selection. Entry: one lesion ≤8 cm; two/three lesions each ≤5 cm and total ≤8 cm; or four/five each ≤3 cm and total ≤8 cm; no macrovascular invasion; ≥3 months observation after successful downstaging.

Results. Downstaging succeeded in 77/118 (65.3%); 64/118 (54.2%) underwent LT. Five-year post-LT survival was 77.8% versus 81.0%; recurrence-free probability 90.8% versus 88.0%. Five-year intention-to-treat survival was 56.1% versus 63.3%; two-year dropout 34.2% versus 25.6%. Five transplanted downstaging patients developed recurrence.

Limits. Bounded protocol, older treatments and selected recipients; recipient survival excludes downstaging failure. Recurrence-free probability is not automatically a death-inclusive DFS endpoint.

Mehta 2021

Design and population. Prospective MERITS-LT cohort: 209 UNOS-DS patients at seven centers in four US regions, 2016–2019.

Selection. UNOS-DS entry limits; predominantly Child-Pugh A/B; bilirubin ≤4 mg/dL; Child-Pugh B/C with AFP >1,000 excluded. Initial TACE versus Y90 was not randomized.

Results. 174/209 (83.3%) achieved downstaging; two-year estimated success 87.7%. Observed dropout was 75/209, including 51 tumor progressions and nine liver-related deaths; two-year competing-risk dropout 37.3%. LT occurred in 63/209 by cutoff; three-year LT probability 46.6%. Median post-LT follow-up 1.7 years; two-year recipient survival 95%, three-year 83.1%; recurrence 5/63. Three-year treatment-entry survival 73%. Explants exceeded Milan in 42.8%, with microvascular invasion in 17.5%.

Limits. Early post-LT follow-up and substantial radiology/explant discordance; success of an imaging endpoint does not establish absence of viable cancer. Overlaps later MERITS expansions.

Sinha 2019

Design and population. Single-center all-comers analysis: 74 beyond-UCSF-DS patients versus 133 bounded UCSF-DS patients.

Selection. All-comers exceeded the established downstaging burden limits; full-text confirmation of era and entry exclusions remains pending.

Results. Downstaging success: 64.8% versus 84.2%. Three-year dropout: 80.0% versus 36.1%. LT: 10/74 (13.5%) all-comers versus 59.0% of bounded patients. Five-year intention-to-treat survival: 21.1% versus 56.0%; three of ten all-comer recipients recurred. Number plus largest diameter >8 and Child-Pugh B/C predicted dropout.

Limits. Strong counterevidence to unlimited entry, but small historical single-center cohort. Primary abstract only; treatment-entry timing and endpoint definitions require full text. Not a validation of a ≤12 cutoff.

Natarajan 2023

Design and population. Prospective MERITS-LT expansion: 82 all-comers versus 229 UNOS-DS patients, seven centers, 2015–2020.

Selection. All-comers exceeded UNOS-DS. Liver reserve and AFP identified poor-prognosis subgroups rather than a randomized selection rule.

Results. Twelve-month downstaging: 67% versus 83%. Three-year entry survival: 58% versus 69%; approximately 30% in all-comers with Child-Pugh B/C or AFP ≥500. LT occurred in 20/82 versus 85/229; five-year LT probability 42.2% versus 73.6%. Recipient three-year survival: 90.9% versus 80.6%; recurrence 1/20 versus 7/85. Median post-LT follow-up was only about 18.5 months. All-comer dropouts numbered 39: 25 before and 14 after downstaging.

Limits. Immature recipient estimates and substantial attrition. Cohort overlaps earlier MERITS and Xu 2025; do not sum patients or treat as independent validation.

Xu 2025 · all-comers

Design and population. National prospective cohort, nine centers, 2015–2023: 190 all-comers and 326 UNOS-DS patients; intention-to-treat clock starts at first locoregional treatment.

Selection. No extrahepatic disease or macrovascular invasion; all-comers exceeded UNOS-DS. Proposed burden and biologic refinements were derived from this cohort, not prospectively locked eligibility criteria.

Results. Two-year downstaging: 66% versus 82%. Among all-comers, number plus largest diameter <10, 10–12 and >12 had success of 72%, 51% and 39%. Five-year treatment-entry survival: 45.0% versus 58.8%. LT occurred in 76/190 versus 155/326; median downstaging-to-LT 10.6 months. Five-year recipient survival: 71.5% versus 73.6%; recurrence: 29.8% versus 13.7%, with median post-LT follow-up 27.4 months. AFP ≥100 and Child-Pugh B predicted worse entry survival.

Limits. Selected-recipient success cannot replace entry survival; five-year tails are immature. Y90/TACE comparisons were observational. The proposed number-plus-diameter ≤12, AFP <100 and Child-Pugh A combination remains unvalidated.

XXL 2020

Design and population. Multicenter randomized phase 2b/3 trial: 74 entered downstaging; 29 dropped out before randomization; 45 responders were randomized, 23 to LT and 22 to nontransplant treatment.

Selection. Child-Pugh A–B7, ECOG 0–1, predicted five-year post-LT survival ≥50%, and ≥3 months sustained response. AFP rules depended on baseline value and response, not an unconditional AFP <400 entry rule.

Results. From randomization, five-year overall survival was 77.5% with LT versus 31.2% without LT; tumor-event-free survival 76.8% versus 18.3%; median follow-up 71 months.

Limits. Strongest causal evidence for LT among selected responders, not for all 74 entrants or unlimited initial burden. Small, prematurely stopped trial. Appendix allowed uncertain bland-versus-neoplastic segmental/type-I portal thrombosis under additional response conditions; the actual PVTT denominator/outcomes are not reported. It must not be relabeled a proven-PVTT trial.

Huang 2023

Design and population. UNOS national cohort, 2010–2019: 23,398 HCC exception applicants—20,579 initially Milan, 2,151 UNOS-DS, 668 all-comers.

Selection. Time zero was first HCC exception application, not first downstaging treatment. Patients had already reached the allocation/application stage.

Results. Two-year dropout: 19.3%, 25.3% and 29.7%, respectively; the direct all-comer versus UNOS-DS difference was not significant (P=.07). In all-comers with initial AFP >100, two-year dropout was 52%, versus 26% in the comparison beyond-Milan patients. Higher MELD and total tumor diameter predicted dropout.

Limits. Useful real-world waitlist counterevidence, but excludes patients lost before exception application and cannot supply first-LRT intention-to-treat survival. National registry overlaps other US registry studies.

Tabrizian 2022

Design and population. Five-center US recipient cohort, 2001–2015: 2,645 transplanted patients, including 2,122 always within Milan, 341 downstaged and 182 still beyond Milan.

Selection. Only 168 of the 341 downstaged patients originally met UNOS-DS limits. All analyses start with patients who actually received a graft.

Results. Ten-year post-LT overall survival was 61.5%, 52.1% and 43.3%; ten-year recurrence was 13.3%, 20.6% and 41.1%, respectively. Median follow-up was 55.3 months.

Limits. Longer-term cancer risk persists after successful downstaging. Recipient-only retrospective data do not estimate entry survival or probability of ever reaching LT; long-term tails have fewer patients.

HCC B1.1 — AFP-only failure / imaging discordance

OPEN review, not activated. Confirmed AFP ≥500 as a broader clinical hold and AFP ≥200 with serial rise/doubling are proposed local rules, not universal OPTN policy. Do not require AFP <100 for every within-Milan patient.

Interactive criteria, evidence and next steps · B1.1a–f meeting decisions

Mehta 2019: AFP response

Application. Supports response-based biological review and the existing >1000-to-<500 allocation framework. Does not require AFP <100 for every within-Milan candidate or prove a safe AFP-only exception.

Mehta 2019: AFP response · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Retrospective UNOS recipient cohort, 2005–2015; 407 patients with AFP >1000 at least once and final AFP within 90 days of LT. Living-donor and multiorgan recipients excluded; 93.6% initially within Milan.

Five-year post-LT OS: 48.8% with final AFP >1000, 67.0% at 101–499, and 88.4% at ≤100. Corresponding Kaplan–Meier recurrence estimates: 35.0%, 13.3%, and 7.2%. The 500–1000 group had only 17 patients. Differences between ≤100 and 101–499 were not statistically significant.

Recipient-only, selected historical cohort; treatment and waiting time differed substantially between groups. Non-HCC death was censored for recurrence. No proof that forcing AFP lower causes benefit, no AFP-only/imaging-complete-response subgroup, and no LDLT validation.

Main PDF pp 3–9; Tables 1–3 and Figures 1–3.

Goldman 2022: final AFP ≥500

Application. Supports discussing a conservative clinical hold at confirmed AFP ≥500, even without a prior >1000 result. This is an OPEN local proposal, not a universal OPTN contraindication or a proven transplant-benefit cutoff.

Goldman 2022: final AFP ≥500 · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

UNOS 2005–2015; 1766 LT recipients with initial AFP 100–999. Only 879 had serial values; final AFP ≥500 subgroup n=109. LDLT excluded.

Five-year OS at final AFP <100 / 100–499 / ≥500: 72.7% / 70.4% / 56.1%; recurrence: 13.1% / 18.7% / 24.2%. Final AFP ≥500: adjusted mortality HR 1.50 (95% CI 1.07–2.10), recurrence HR 1.88 (1.13–3.14).

Historical recipient-only study, not a comparison of transplant versus no transplant. Many had only one AFP. In the ≥500 subgroup, 40.4% were ≥1000; small subgroups cannot establish equivalence. Recurrence estimates censor competing death.

Main PDF pp 4–9; Tables 1–3 and Figures 1–3.

Giard 2018: AFP slope

Application. Use slope as an alert to reassess biology, imaging and inflammation, not a stand-alone absolute stop or permanent delisting rule.

Giard 2018: AFP slope · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Single-center 336 transplanted T2/Milan patients, 2003–2013; 98.2% received LRT. Median five AFP measurements over 9.8 months. Slope fitted to all values; >7.5 subgroup n=23.

AFP slope >7.5 ng/mL/month: adjusted recurrence HR 3.0 (1.1–8.1), microvascular invasion OR 6.8 (1.6–28.7). Five-year recurrence-free probability 69.8% versus 87.4%; survival without recurrence 59.5% versus 75.3%. These are different endpoints, neither pair should be labeled overall survival.

Small, data-selected cutoff group; no prospective hold/release validation. The long-term fitted slope cannot be inferred reliably from two closely spaced noisy results. Death was censored for recurrence-free probability.

Main PDF pp 2–6; Tables 1–4 and Figure 1.

Halazun 2021: dynamic AFP/NYCA

Application. Use dynamic AFP as complementary risk information. The NYCA definition of response does not supersede the stricter OPTN requirement to fall below 500 after AFP >1000.

Halazun 2021: dynamic AFP/NYCA · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

External retrospective validation in 2236 LT recipients at eight centers, 2001–2013; 545 beyond Milan. Composite score combines tumor size, count and AFP response. Deaths within 90 postoperative days excluded.

Five-year competing-risk cumulative recurrence: 9.5%, 20.5%, 40.5% for low/intermediate/high NYCA categories. Discrimination C statistic 0.66. These are composite-score categories, not isolated AFP ranges.

Recipient selection, exclusion of early deaths, no waitlist dropout or transplant-benefit comparison. Maximum-to-final AFP can miss a rebound below the original peak. Extreme-AFP response subgroup was tiny.

Main PDF pp 1–8; Table 1, Figures 1–3. Trailing commentary is not a separate reviewed study.

Moriyama 2026: markers before imaging recurrence

Application. Supports prompt repeat markers and high-quality/alternate imaging for an unexplained rise. Does not prove occult recurrence in every individual, justify empiric systemic/LRT treatment without a target, or validate an AFP-only transplant cutoff.

Moriyama 2026: markers before imaging recurrence · paper · TMC full-text access

Population, results and limitations

Retrospective post-RFA, nontransplant cohort. Among 1620 initially treated patients, 907 episodes in 509 patients had elevated AFP, AFP-L3 and/or DCP without imaging evidence. Episodes are not independent patients and this is not AFP alone.

Recurrence was subsequently identified in 886 episodes; median detection interval 89 days. Local curative treatment 83.5% versus 78.8% and post-recurrence OS 3.55 versus 3.31 years for detection ≤180 versus >180 days; differences were not statistically significant.

RFA-specific, repeated episodes and competing losses; no LT cohort or tested optimal recall interval. Routine imaging is described as every four months in methods versus quarterly in abstract. Case-level AFP discrepancy between Figure 3 and Table 4 is not used.

All nine main-PDF pages; methods, Tables 2–4, Figures 1–3. External Tables S1/S2 not supplied.

HCC-B2High-risk protocol · Proposed study cohort

Previously treated portal-vein tumor invasion

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Proposed anatomy: original Vp1 or Vp2 only, centrally confirmed. Exclude Vp3, Vp4/main or contralateral branch, hepatic-vein tumor invasion, previous tumor rupture and combined expanded-burden risk from the initial pilot. These are study boundaries, not universal declarations of futility.
  • Require complete absence of enhancing viable vascular tumor, remaining viable parenchymal disease within Milan or completely nonviable, no extrahepatic disease, and ≥12 continuous months meeting these conditions. Scan approximately every three months; count from the first qualifying scan.
  • Any recurrent vascular enhancement or new metastasis stops progression to LT. Indeterminate thrombus or imaging discordance is a hold, not automatic clearance.
Tests and information to review

Original vessel-by-vessel map with Vp grade; expert serial contrast CT/MRI; chest staging; AFP; separate vascular and parenchymal response; resection options. Crosswalk the original study/anatomic vascular definition to current Vp grade. Record vascular response separately from overall mRECIST response; neither excludes microscopic viable PVTT.

Allocation and donor route

Clinical study eligibility cannot override the main-portal/hepatic-vein exception contraindications or grant priority. Obtain a separate documented organ route.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

Anatomy, treatment response and biology all matter, but the available cohorts do not validate a sharp universal Vp2/Vp3 boundary or a single control-duration requirement. Several selected branch or even main-trunk recipients survived long term; this argues against a blanket futility claim. Conversely, small anatomy strata, highly selective transplantation, exclusion of perioperative deaths in some cohorts and absent entry denominators prevent a routine-policy expansion. Overall mRECIST response is not synonymous with vascular sterilization, and imaging disappearance can coexist with viable explant PVTT.

Evidence against expansion

Poorer outcomes for lobar/main-trunk groups in Choi, Yang and Ye favor caution, while Yoon's selected Vp4 survivors and Sposito's downstaged cohort show that advanced anatomy is not invariably fatal. Assalino included hepatic-vein and mixed first-order/main-trunk disease, but its selected-recipient results cannot establish that these groups should enter an initial US pilot. Differences in anatomical definitions prevent a valid pooled Vp-grade survival estimate.

Certainty

Very low for a specific anatomy or waiting-time threshold; low for an association between response/biologic selection and better recipient outcomes. No reviewed randomized study directly established the transplant strategy's net benefit from confirmed-PVTT treatment entry.

Basis for proposed thresholds

Vp1–2 restriction, complete vascular radiographic response and 12-month control are proposed local safeguards, not a jointly tested published rule. Sposito accepted sustained vascular CR/PR for ≥6 months; Assalino reported a median, not a minimum, 11.4-month interval. NLRB guidance permits selected branch-invasion/rupture cases after prolonged stability but does not mandate clinical acceptance or convert main-trunk/hepatic-vein disease into a standard exception. Preserve separate original and current vascular extent.

Yoon 2026

Design and population. Retrospective Asan LDLT cohort, 1997–2022, 137 selected PVTT recipients; median follow-up 38.3 months.

Selection. Original Vp1/Vp2/Vp3/Vp4 counts were 51/36/36/14. Overall mRECIST CR/PR defined responders, not necessarily complete vascular response.

Results. Vp1 five-year OS 69.5%, DFS 56.2%; Vp4 OS 50.0%, DFS 34.3%. Exact Vp2/Vp3 five-year values were not tabulated. Vp4 recurrence was 9/14 (64.3%) by Table 2 count, not a five-year risk. Responders: 83 patients, five-year OS 66.6%, DFS 64.7%, recurrence 28/83; nonresponders: 54, OS 35.1%, DFS 19.7%, recurrence 42/54. Viable PVTT remained in 65/83 responder explants.

Limits. Recipient-only, heterogeneous treatment eras and small grade subgroups. Nonsignificant grade comparisons do not prove equivalence. Discussion/conclusion transpose responder OS and DFS; retain Results/Figure 1 values.

Choi 2017

Design and population. Retrospective selected LDLT recipients with PVTT, n=34.

Selection. Segmental involvement n=27; first-order/lobar involvement n=7. These are study-defined anatomical groups.

Results. Five-year OS was 50.3% for segmental and 14.3% for first-order/lobar involvement. Segmental five-year DFS was 63.9%; a numeric lobar DFS estimate was not available in the verified primary abstract.

Limits. Very small lobar group, retrospective recipient selection and no all-downstaging-entry denominator. OS and DFS are retained as reported, not converted into recurrence risk.

Yang 2020

Design and population. Retrospective 75-recipient PVTT cohort.

Selection. Vp2 and Vp3 were combined; Vp4 was separately analyzed. Subgroup sample sizes remain unverified without complete text.

Results. Three-year OS/RFS were 65.4%/40.0% for Vp2–3 combined and 30.6%/21.4% for Vp4.

Limits. Do not split combined Vp2–3 results into invented grade-specific estimates. No verified all-entry denominator or grade-specific recurrence counts; endpoint death handling needs full text.

Ye 2024

Design and population. Retrospective DDLT cohort of 141 vascular-invasion recipients: 13 segmental PVTT, 68 lobar PVTT, 60 microvascular invasion.

Selection. All had HBV. Lobar PVTT included first-order branches or main portal vein; perioperative deaths and early follow-up losses were excluded.

Results. Segmental three-year OS 75.0%, RFS 58.3%, crude recurrence 4/13. Lobar three-year OS 45.9%, RFS 9.1%, crude recurrence 60/68.

Limits. Exclusion of early deaths biases recipient survival upward. Lobar is not an isolated Vp3 group. Crude recurrence and RFS are distinct measures; the cohort is not directly transportable to Western mixed-etiology candidates.

Soin 2020

Design and population. Cohort following a PVTT downstaging protocol: 43 treated, 27 responders, 25 LDLT recipients.

Selection. Among recipients, Vp1/Vp2/Vp3/Vp4 counts were 1/12/11/1. Transplant was conditional on successful downstaging.

Results. Reported five-year OS 57% and RFS 51% exclude two postoperative deaths, leaving a 23-recipient survival subset. Recurrence occurred in 6/25 (24%) of the whole transplanted group.

Limits. Neither reported survival curve represents all 25 recipients or all 43 treatment entrants. Small selected cohort without randomized control; grade-specific outcomes unavailable.

Assalino 2020 · vascular invasion

Design and population. Multicenter retrospective series: 45 transplanted patients reviewed, 30 with centrally validated macrovascular invasion retained; 11 centers, 2004–2018.

Selection. Vascular radiologic disappearance after treatment; 24 portal and six hepatic-vein cases. Portal groups: seven Vp1, 12 Vp2, five study-defined Vp3. Its Vp3 included first-order branch OR main trunk. Median downstaging-to-LT 11.4 months (range 0.8–43.3; timing available in 27).

Results. Median post-LT follow-up 39 months; one/three/five-year OS 76.7%/66.2%/59.6%; DFS 63.3%/56.3%/56.3%. Recurrence 8/30, median 6.5 months after LT. Three explants retained major vascular invasion despite radiologic clearance. An exploratory AFP <10 subgroup had 11% recurrence and five-year OS 83.3%.

Limits. The 15 excluded records are not downstaging failures. No all-treated denominator. AFP threshold was post hoc; subgroup denominator not verified. Neither median waiting time nor the mixed Vp3 label validates a 12-month or modern Vp3 cutoff.

Sposito 2026

Design and population. Five-center Italian Y90 cohort, 2010–2023: 240 consecutive HCC/PVTT patients; 61 downstaged, 37 transplanted.

Selection. Sustained vascular CR or PR for ≥6 months plus center-specific morphology/AFP requirements. Median Y90-to-LT interval 17 months.

Results. Downstaging: 61/240 (25.4%); LT: 37/240 (15.4%). Among downstaged patients, 60-month cancer death was 15.8% with LT versus 45.2% without LT (subdistribution HR .221, 95% CI .071–.683). Recipients had three/five-year OS 67.2%/61.6% and RFS 79.3%/79.3%; complete explant necrosis 56.7%.

Limits. Abstract-only verification. Grade denominators, landmark/selection adjustment and RFS death handling require full text; RFS exceeding OS must not be converted to recurrence as 1−RFS. No verified all-240 survival estimate.

HCC-B3High-risk protocol · Proposed safety approach

Previous checkpoint immunotherapy

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Proposed elective cutoff: ≥90 days from last ICI dose to LT. No interval is proven safe; a longer interval does not eliminate rejection or graft-loss risk.
  • Require no active clinically significant non-endocrine immune toxicity: grade ≤1 and no escalating immunosuppression for toxicity. Stable endocrine replacement may qualify. Prior grade 3–4 non-endocrine toxicity or myocarditis is excluded from the initial pilot pending separate specialist review.
  • A shorter interval has no automatic urgency waiver. An exceptional-care decision must document near-term mortality without LT, alternatives, donor/graft risk, consent and rescue capability. Biology, ICI and allocation clocks may overlap; do not add them mechanically.
  • Consent must address rejection, graft loss, uncertain comparative oncologic benefit and progression during washout. Neither elapsed time nor complete radiological response guarantees immune safety. Obtain the missing primary full texts before ratifying these provisional thresholds.
Tests and information to review

Drug and last-dose dates; complete irAE history and grade; treatment and recovery; graft-risk review by hepatology, oncology and pharmacy; current staging.

Allocation and donor route

Prior ICI alone is not an automatic HCC exception exclusion. The proposed clinical timing requirement is separate from tumor eligibility and allocation waiting time.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

ICI can help some selected patients reach transplantation, but efficacy and safety denominators must be kept separate: response among treated patients, probability of reaching LT, and rejection among recipients answer different questions. Very short washout is consistently concerning; evidence does not establish an interval at which risk becomes zero. Prior irAE adds information beyond elapsed time. Neither the apparent safety of some matched cohorts nor promising recipient survival cancels the graft-loss signal.

Evidence against expansion

ImmunoXXL demonstrates feasibility with intervals shorter than 90 days, and Aceituno found similar rejection proportions in matched groups; these challenge a claim that <90 days is an absolute biological contraindication. They do not prove equivalence or validate elective shortening. Ong suggests oncologic benefit but also higher rejection, while Fang highlights risk missed by washout alone. The literature supports individualized exceptional review, not automatic urgency-based exemption.

Certainty

Low for the association between shorter washout and rejection; low for prior-any-irAE risk; very low for a precise safe interval, a grade-specific irAE exclusion, an optimal immunosuppression regimen or an ICI-caused treatment-entry survival benefit. No reviewed trial randomized washout or transplant versus continued systemic therapy after ICI.

Basis for proposed thresholds

Ninety days: conservative local planning rule informed by retrospective timing associations and IPD modeling, not a validated threshold. Grade ≤1 recovery and exclusion of prior severe nonendocrine/myocarditis cases: proposed operational safety judgment, not a proven transplant-specific cutoff; Fang's severe-event sample is only six. No laboratory or immune assay identified in this review validates clearance for LT. Center-specific immunosuppression should not be portrayed as evidence-proven protection from ICI-associated rejection.

VITALITY · Tabrizian

Design and population. Multicenter observational US cohort: 117 consecutive ICI-treated HCC patients assessed for LT, 2016–2023.

Selection. 86 were initially beyond Milan; 94% also received locoregional treatment. Patients were selected for transplant evaluation rather than representing all advanced HCC treated with ICI.

Results. 65/86 (75.6%) beyond-Milan patients downstaged, median 5.6 months; 43/117 (36.7%) received LT. Three-year intention-to-treat survival was 71.1%; three-year recipient survival 85%. Rejection occurred in 7/43; six had last ICI <3 months before LT; one rejection-associated graft loss.

Limits. The abstract's categories—18 initially within Milan plus 23 downstaged recipients—sum to 41, not 43; the two-patient discrepancy awaits full flow-diagram review. Multimodal treatment prevents attribution to ICI alone; full paper unavailable.

ImmunoXXL 2026

Design and population. Prospective phase II experience: 61 potentially eligible atezolizumab–bevacizumab-treated patients; 16 enrolled/listed/transplanted responders.

Selection. The 45 not included comprised 25 treatment discontinuations (15 progression, four adverse events, six decompensation) and 20 failing downstaging criteria. Eight of 16 had portal thrombosis, grades not verified; 15 had prior locoregional treatment.

Results. Median washout 57.5 days (IQR 29–87), median follow-up 16 months. Acute rejection 4/16 (25%); three irAEs. Ninety-day morbidity 62.5%, mortality 1/16. Explants: ten complete and six partial responses; one recurrence. Two-year recipient OS 94%, RFS 90%.

Limits. These are selected-16 outcomes, not 61-patient ITT efficacy. Small, nonrandomized, short follow-up; a shorter median washout does not establish safety. Retransplantation count was not independently extractable from accessible primary text and is not asserted.

Moeckli 2025

Design and population. International retrospective series of 119 ICI-exposed LT recipients.

Selection. Only transplanted patients; washout categories <30, 30–50 and >50 days.

Results. Rejection occurred in 24/119 (20.2%), median nine days after LT. Six of 24 rejection cases lost the graft—6/119 (5.0%) of all recipients. Relative to >50 days, univariate rejection odds ratios were 21.3 (95% CI 5.93–103) for <30 days and 9.48 (2.47–46.8) for 30–50 days.

Limits. Retrospective association, broad confidence intervals and selection by transplant timing; no untreated comparator or all-ICI-treatment denominator. >50 days is not a prospectively validated safe threshold.

Rezaee-Zavareh · IPD

Design and population. Secondary systematic review and individual-patient-data analysis: 91 recipients from 30 predominantly case reports/series and cohorts.

Selection. Published ICI-exposed transplanted cases; not a randomized or consecutive all-treated population.

Results. 24/91 rejections (26.4%), nine recurrences and nine deaths; median follow-up 690 days. Adjusted rejection hazard ratio per additional washout week .92 (95% CI .86–.99). Ninety-four days was the median washout among modeled patients with predicted rejection probability ≤20%, not an observed universal safety cutoff.

Limits. Publication/selection bias, heterogeneous regimens, small events and overlap with later cohorts. A modeled ≤20% probability does not justify telling an individual that three months eliminates rejection risk.

Fang · irAEs

Design and population. Multicenter Chinese retrospective cohort, 2018–2024: 209 ICI-exposed LT recipients.

Selection. Prior irAEs of any grade were recorded; six patients had grade 3–4 events.

Results. Rejection occurred in 36/209 (17.2%); 61.1% biopsy proven, median ten days after LT. Correct risks from Table 3: 21/44 (47.7%) with prior irAE versus 15/165 (9.1%) without. Any irAE adjusted OR 9.17 (95% CI 4.00–21.02); washout <30 days OR 3.071 (1.211–7.787). The six grade 3–4 cases split three with and three without rejection.

Limits. The discussion's 58.3%/13.3% are irAE prevalence within rejection/nonrejection groups, not rejection probabilities. Too few severe irAEs to validate a grade-specific exclusion or a recovery interval.

Aceituno 2026

Design and population. Ten-center ICI series, 48 recipients, propensity matched with 144 Toronto non-ICI controls; 20/48 exposed cases previously published.

Selection. Matching used age, sex, liver disease and LT year, not tumor burden or immunosuppression. Prior ICI adverse events were not captured.

Results. Biopsy-proven rejection 9/48 (18.8%) versus 28/144 (19.4%), P=.916. Two exposed cases were steroid resistant; one rejection-related graft failure required retransplant. Overall graft failure 2/48 versus 3/144, retransplant 2 versus zero, recurrence 5/48 versus 15/144, death 7/48 versus 21/144. Median follow-up 2.6 versus 3.2 years. Table 1 washout median 60 days, IQR 22–196.

Limits. No detected difference is not equivalence. Substantial tumor-risk imbalance and published-case overlap. Abstract washout IQR 13–96 conflicts with Table 1 and appears to match rejection-onset timing; use table and flag discrepancy. It cannot reassure about prior irAE risk.

LITCHI 2026

Design and population. Multinational European registry, ten centers, 18 recipients, 2018–2024.

Selection. 15 received atezolizumab–bevacizumab; 14/18 had washout >100 days. Only two centers used routine biopsy; others used clinical/LFT triggers.

Results. Four of 18 developed biopsy-confirmed rejection (22%; 95% CI 6.4–47.6). One fatal rejection occurred 21 days after LT following 13-day washout; three other events responded to intensified immunosuppression. One HCC recurrence; one-year overall survival 77%.

Limits. Small, noncomparative and largely long-washout cohort. Late rejection and incomplete protocol-biopsy surveillance preclude a guaranteed safe interval or extrapolation of the aggregate rate to short-washout patients.

HCC ICI cohort · JAMA 2026

Design and population. Eight-center Chinese recipient cohort, 2019–2024; initially beyond Milan; 208 ICI recipients propensity matched to 208 non-ICI recipients.

Selection. Downstaging followed by actual transplantation; not treatment-entry randomization. Treatment was multimodal.

Results. Median post-LT OS 4.57 versus 2.53 years, HR .63 (95% CI .43–.92); RFS 1.47 versus .89 years, HR .59 (.44–.80); recurrence subdistribution HR .54 (.40–.72). Ninety-day rejection was 34/208 (16.3%) versus 17/208 (8.2%).

Limits. Favorable oncologic association coexists with greater rejection. Residual confounding and selection remain; there is no all-downstaging-entry ITT comparison. Possible center/year overlap with Fang and other Chinese cohorts must be checked before pooling. Abstract-only verification.

HCC-B4High-risk protocol · Research use only

Survival-benefit prediction: research use only

Proposed position for discussion — not an activated protocol

  • Proposed decision threshold: no score-driven listing, exclusion, exception priority, organ acceptance, LDLT selection or treatment change before external validation and prospective impact review.
  • Require a locked reproducible formula and data dictionary, clarification of the MELD/AFP definitions and time zero, independent external calibration and utility testing against current care, equity assessment, and appropriate policy authorization before deployment.
  • Do not extrapolate the within-Milan, non-LDLT model to PVTT, LDLT or other cancers. No numerical score cutoff is proposed because its clinical use is not yet justified.
Tests and information to review

Reproducible model code; independent cohort; time-dependent transplant analysis; competing-event definitions; calibration, utility and subgroup audit.

Allocation and donor route

Neither a model estimate nor an internal committee can independently rewrite national allocation priority.

Evidence, limitations and supporting papers

Evidence summary

Benefit modeling is clinically important because acceptable post-LT survival alone ignores deaths or progression without transplantation. However, a prognostic model, a causal benefit estimate and an allocation policy are different products. High internal discrimination or correlation cannot establish calibration, appropriate treatment-time handling, fairness or improved decisions. The existing score should therefore be studied, not used to override eligibility or priority. The UK experience provides a constructive precedent for eventual governed implementation after population-specific validation and policy integration.

Evidence against expansion

The UK implementation and longitudinal causal methods show that benefit-based allocation need not remain hypothetical. Favorable internal performance and the larger-benefit signal at higher tumor burden justify further evaluation, not automatic adoption. Conversely, an imputed nontransplant counterfactual, recipient-only scope or overlapping registry sample cannot serve as independent validation. The proper critique is specific to estimand, calibration and transport—not a blanket claim that observational modeling is invalid or that time-dependent LT was never considered.

Certainty

Moderate that benefit-based allocation is feasible within an established national governance system; low that any particular observational score improves individual HCC decisions; very low for deployment of the 2026 Liu score in expanded-risk/PVTT/LDLT patients. No independent prospective impact or external high-risk calibration study of that exact score was identified in this bounded search.

Basis for proposed thresholds

Zero current decision weight is a proposed governance rule, not a published numerical efficacy threshold. No arbitrary calibration/C-index cutoff is endorsed here. Acceptance criteria should be prespecified with biostatistics and allocation stakeholders using clinically meaningful calibration error, decision consequences and equity, then independently tested. Findings in within-Milan DDLT cannot simply be extrapolated to PVTT or LDLT.

Liu 2026

Design and population. SRTR 2002–2022 HCC cohort restricted to Milan: 30,565 candidates, randomly split into development 15,282 and internal validation 15,283. Table 1 records 19,997 LT and 8,249 dropout events.

Selection. DDLT pathway; LDLT excluded. The 30,565 are not all transplanted recipients despite inconsistent Methods wording.

Results. Proposed score: 0.65×(C-MELD−6)+1.99×(TBS−2.25)+0.68×log2(AFP). Reported internal correlation .93, R² .87 and C-index .91.

Limits. Internal split is not external validation. Abstract refers to MELD 3.0 while Methods §1.6 gives an original-MELD formulation; authors/code clarification is needed. Listing versus post-LT time origins, benefit units and model implementation require reproducibility audit. These are audit questions, not proof of a specific bias. No validated transport to PVTT, LDLT or other cancers.

Liu 2025

Design and population. SRTR HCC-exception candidates 2002–2019, follow-up to December 2023: 36,634 candidates; 24,457 transplanted, 9,582 dropout.

Selection. Tumor burden score is √(largest diameter²+number²), grouped ≤3, 3.1–5 and >5. At listing, 91% were within Milan, 5.3% within UCSF but outside Milan, 3.6% beyond UCSF.

Results. Five-year unadjusted restricted-mean survival benefit was 15.7/22.1/25.0 months across increasing TBS groups. Adjusted five-year survival-probability differences were 21.9/34.5/39.4 percentage points. These are different measures, not interchangeable units.

Limits. Overlapping SRTR population is not independent validation of the 2026 score. Methods explicitly report time-dependent LT modeling; do not falsely accuse the study of ignoring treatment timing. Counterfactual assumptions and differing clocks still require scrutiny.

Lai 2017

Design and population. European ten-center HCC listing cohort, 1987–2015: 2,103 listed, 1,857 transplanted; 636 outside Milan at listing.

Selection. Benefit stratification used MELD, AFP, Milan status and radiological response.

Results. The model classified 405/2,103 (19.2%) as no-benefit and 108/2,103 (5.1%) as large-benefit, the latter with a modeled 60-month difference.

Limits. Non-LT counterfactual survival was imputed using MESIAH at the final LT/dropout assessment, not observed in a comparable randomized untreated group. Historical treatment eras and modeling assumptions limit transport. The paper's proposal to delist the no-benefit group is not independently validated clinical utility.

Goudsmit 2023

Design and population. UNOS 2010–April 2019, adult first-LT population; recipient table: 24,503 non-HCC, 6,922 T2 and 5,448 initially outside criteria.

Selection. Excluded LDLT and non-HCC exceptions; longitudinal two-week sequential-trial approach with inverse-probability censoring weights; 67%/33% internal split.

Results. Estimated mean benefit over five years from LT was 2.30 years for non-HCC, 1.19 for T2 and 1.45 for initially outside-criteria HCC.

Limits. The calculator is explicitly for patients who actually underwent transplant and met study criteria; it does not directly validate prioritizing every waiting candidate. No external test in PVTT or high-risk LDLT. Do not equate an average-on-treated estimand with conditional benefit for the whole waiting list.

Allen 2024

Design and population. UK national liver-offering policy evaluation: separate simulation dataset of 1,287 registrations/629 donors plus early real-world NLOS implementation, 2018–2020.

Selection. Transplant-benefit scoring governed national deceased-donor offers; not a high-risk-HCC validation sample.

Results. Simulation compared observed 2013 deaths/removals 93 (7%) and 4,581 patient-years with modeled TBS outcomes 48 (4%) and 5,262 patient-years. During the first two implementation years, 68% of DBD livers were allocated by TBS; observed waitlist mortality decreased (P<.0001), postlisting survival improved (P=.005), with no adverse post-LT survival signal.

Limits. Simulated gains are not observed outcomes. Before/after implementation is nonrandomized and can reflect secular changes. Nonetheless it is meaningful evidence that a governed benefit-based system can be deployed, unlike a claim that all such models are unusable.

Prosepe 2026

Design and population. Methods study with simulation and Eurotransplant cirrhosis cohort, 2007–2019: 9,868 candidates; 4,998 LT, 3,013 deaths before LT, 1,361 deaths within three years after LT.

Selection. Conditional three-year restricted-mean benefit at each available graft; sequential marginal structural models and time-varying treatment weighting account for candidate and donor characteristics.

Results. The in-sample allocation exercise estimated 0.46 additional life-years per graft versus MELD allocation.

Limits. Modeled, not observed implementation benefit; not HCC-specific validation. Identification requires consistency, exchangeability, positivity and correct models. The whole-waitlist conditional estimand differs from average benefit among actual recipients.
