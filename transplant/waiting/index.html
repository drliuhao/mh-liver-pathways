How priority becomes an offer

Houston donor-hospital example

The September 2026 OPTN table gives MMaT 34 for Houston donor hospitals, including Hermann, Methodist and St. Luke’s. An MMaT − 3 exception would therefore be 31 for those donors; MMaT − 20 would be 15 after the policy floor.

Recheck for each donor offer

MMaT belongs to the donor hospital’s surrounding transplant area. A liver offered from elsewhere can have a different value. The higher applicable laboratory or exception score determines allocation priority; donor distance and other allocation rules still apply.

Living donation has a separate timetable. The deceased-donor exception delay does not set the living-donor operation date. Donor assessment, recipient suitability and the disease-specific observation or treatment requirements still have to be met.

How these estimates were calculated

Population and time zero. One person per cohort. The HCC cohort starts at the first recorded HCC exception application nationally, if it occurred at one of the three programs in the entry window and the candidate was ≥18. Approved and unapproved requests are included. This excludes HCC patients with no recorded HCC application and people whose first request was elsewhere. The all-diagnosis cohort starts at the first recorded national liver registration at age ≥18 in the same window and programs. Candidates were not selected for receiving a transplant.

What the curves measure. Deceased-donor transplantation and death/removal as too sick before the index registration ends. Living-donor transplant, transplant with unknown donor source, and other removal (including transfer) are competing outcomes. A transplant at another center is recognized when linked by the encrypted person identifier within the index-registration period. A later transplant after transfer/removal is outside this analysis. This is registration-based access, not lifetime survival or the probability of eventual transplant anywhere.

Follow-up and uncertainty. Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence; events precede censoring on tied dates. Administrative follow-up ends at the recorded registration end or 31 December 2025, whichever is earlier. Recent entrants have shorter follow-up. They contribute observed time until censoring; 12–24-month estimates draw more on earlier entrants. The pooled estimator assumes non-informative censoring, which may be limited by changes in outcomes over calendar time. Pointwise 95% intervals use 2,000 person-level bootstrap resamples (seed 20261026). The curves include inactive time and are not adjusted for blood group, disease severity, donor preferences, center practice or changes in policy. Center mapping and residual confounding uncertainty are not included in the intervals.

Interpretation. The six-month HCC policy delay and a six-month observation interval before an exception request are different clocks. Neither is the observed time to transplant. These cohorts span changing policy and clinical practice. Small rare-cancer groups are not reported; all-diagnosis or HCC outcomes must not be substituted for disease-specific CRLM, CCA, NELM, HEHE or adenoma predictions.

Verification. Direct reconstruction from raw liver registry and exception files; exact registration joins, date/order and missingness checks, independent cumulative-incidence arithmetic, censoring checks, and independent R estimates at 6, 12 and 24 months. Every included entry is in 2021–2025; follow-up ends by 31 December 2025. Patient-level files remain local. This is a descriptive aggregate analysis, not an official OPTN/SRTR report or an independently reviewed prediction model.
