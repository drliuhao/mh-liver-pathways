# Clinical presentation hierarchy

Use the same hierarchy on every roadmap and transplant pathway. A recent revision is not a reason for greater prominence.

1. **Disease / pathway**: navigation between diseases and clinical pathways. Treatment matrices are resources; individual studies are never disease-level navigation.
2. **Major clinical decision**: always-visible pinned topic strip. Only clinical questions, care states, or assessments belong here. Viewing a topic does not record a finding or establish eligibility.
3. **Decision workspace**: Tests needed / Results & criteria / Tools & resources. Three columns on desktop; tabs on phones. Decision team appears first in Tools.
4. **Current clinical work**: relevant criteria; recommended options and possible results (or the matched result in optional finding mode); then conditional next actions. Keep critical cautions and approval status visible. High-risk reviews are orange, with their triggering conditions and approval status shown beside the relevant decision.
5. **Decision support**: scores and classification definitions in Tools for the relevant decision; individual studies and detailed evidence after recommendations and next actions. Indications and contraindications remain peer disclosures. Unrelated evidence stays in the evidence library.
6. **Background**: full source library, document provenance, operational approvals, and change log. These must not compete with clinical choices.

## Adding or revising content

- Assign a clinical owner before adding a panel. `tools/content_hierarchy.json` maps each inherited reference to its owning decisions and its role: tool, criteria, guide, or evidence.
- Do not add a top-level banner, quick-reference item, new primary tab, or colored callout simply because material is new.
- A trial supports a treatment decision. It is not itself a clinical option. A prognostic association is not an eligibility rule.
- Scores, clinical options, source papers, administrative documents, and proposed study protocols must not share an undifferentiated list of buttons.
- Preserve thresholds, cohort qualifiers, source links, recommendation labels, and critical precautions when moving content. Do not regrade evidence as part of layout work.
- Default to the existing shared structure. Avoid disease-specific global cards when the content applies to one assessment.
- Regenerate `assets/content-hierarchy-data.js` with `node tools/build_content_hierarchy.cjs`; run `node tools/test_content_hierarchy.cjs` plus the existing presentation and clinical-routing checks.
- Check every changed decision on desktop and phone. Confirm all original reference content remains accessible and unchanged after topic switches.
- **Cross-tumor proposed positions** (29 October 2026: previously treated extrahepatic disease) live on one page, `extrahepatic-disease/`, built by `python3 tools/build_ehd_page.py` from `tools/ehd_positions.json` and checked by `python3 tools/test_ehd_positions.py`. They are reached only from the decisions they concern: a conditional "If …" link at each roadmap decision (`assets/roadmap-links.json`, label ending "(proposed)") and the transplant high-risk review (`#high-risk-<review>`). Roadmap options and recommendations are not edited; the transplant reviews are changed only through `tools/clinical_revisions/`. No top-level menu entry.

## Compact treatment evidence

- Use the shared compact Indications / Contraindications & cautions / Evidence & sources controls on roadmap treatment options. Keep critical safety statements outside optional panels. Phone labels may shorten while accessible names retain their meaning.
- Keep numerical evidence attached to its actual treatment option and named study. State the population, arms, endpoint/time horizon and material limitations. A shared PMID alone does not justify moving another study result into an option.
- Every cited study appears as one line in the liver-directed matrix format: name · PMID · arms · main result · limitation (the PREMIERE row is the model). A result the option's own text reports for that study is shown first; otherwise the line is the study's own result in its own population, labelled by its arms, never restated as the option's effect. Non-PMID sources show their category and citation. Lines are curated in `tools/compact_evidence_source.json`; every number must sit in a quoted passage from the site or the team's V15 evidence decks (`tools/compact_evidence_ppt_quotes.json`, checked by `tools/check_compact_evidence_ppt.py`). Figures from the decks are not published.
- Source chips anywhere (refchips, transplant citations, decision-aid links, systemic source lists) carry a PMID and a short description beside the name.
- Recommendation groups are separate tinted panels: Standard approach (green), Reasonable alternatives (blue), Avoid / cautions (brick, never the orange reserved for high-risk protocols). Items without indication blocks (most Avoid items) use the same peer controls (Why · Evidence & sources); no pop-up explanation links remain inside recommendations.
- Version-history remarks ("v0.2 said…", "the page previously quoted…") do not belong in clinical text. Curated exact strings are hidden in place and listed verbatim under "Revision notes" beside the change log; mixed sentences that carry clinical content stay until the source text is reworded.
- Source documents and clinical data remain the source of truth. Explicit, reviewed reference groups can reuse treatment-reference results when the option's own text is insufficient; preserve their cohort limits and distinguish a response endpoint from survival.
- Do not infer equivalence from nonsignificance, or rank regimens by survival from different trials. Keep study evidence separate from patient eligibility and treatment recommendations.
- Do not display standalone “Outcome evidence for this decision” or “Outcome evidence library” panels (Hao, 28 October). Retain evidence under the actual options and in the source libraries. The former outcome-card assets remain archived, unloaded, for future review.
- The systemic matrix uses tumor tabs, setting filters, a setting overview and compact regimen cards. Its build pipeline applies `tools/systemic_matrix_style.py` after regenerating the source page and data. Evidence row segments and reviewed de-duplication rules are in `tools/systemic_evidence_rows.json`.
- Regenerate shared evidence with `python3 tools/build_compact_evidence.py`. Validate with `tools/test_compact_evidence.py`, `tools/test_systemic_matrix_style.py`, source-preservation checks and browser checks of both desktop and phone.

## Transplant pathway workspace

- Transplant pathways use the roadmap's visual language (`assets/transplant-workspace.css/.js`, presentation only). The decision strip groups the decisions under the pathway's four phases (Diagnosis and extent · Transplant suitability · Preparation and reassessment · Transplant and follow-up) and numbers them when the row fits. Each decision is one card: question, context line, three columns.
- **Default view (Hao, 28 October): nothing has to be chosen.** The middle column lists every possible result of the decision the way a roadmap lists its options: "What this decision assesses" (questions and definitions; its evidence is folded at the bottom), then "Possible results within this decision", grouped as Continue assessment (green) · Clarify before continuing (amber) · Another route (blue) · Stop (brick) · High-risk protocol reviews that cover this decision (orange) · Reasons to reassess (brick). Each option shows its title and action verbatim from the page's protocol data, its next-step links (the page's own renderer, so routing is unchanged), and peer panels for **Applies when** (criteria with sources) and **Evidence & sources** (one matrix-style line per source: studies with PMID, arms, result and limitation; guidance and policy with type, note, locator and link).
- **Optional step-by-step entry** ("Record findings step by step" in Tools) switches to the original finding-by-finding view: findings → criteria → matched result → reasons to reassess → other results. Nodes are regrouped, not rewritten. The choice is a per-viewer convenience in browser storage.
- Orange remains reserved for high-risk protocols. Routing, criteria and wording are unchanged.
- Validate with `tools/test_transplant_workspace_browser.py` (all 46 decisions of the seven pathways compared with their protocol data; next-step links; step-by-step mode; phone).

## Link tree: one page per transplant pathway

- **Each transplant pathway is published once, at `transplant/<slug>/`.** Its sections are views of that page, reached by the tab bar or a hash: decision `#<step>`, meeting record `#meeting` or `#meeting-<question id>` (several question ids are also decision ids, e.g. `#diagnosis`), treatment reference `#care-<id>`, high-risk protocols `#high-risk`, one high-risk review `#high-risk-<review>`, evidence library `#evidence`.
- **Retired copies (Hao, 28 October).** `transplant/alignment/<slug>.html` and `transplant/alignment/<slug>/` were full second copies of each pathway (≈700 KB each, 14 files, 9.5 MB) that opened in the meeting view; discussion links and search results pointed to them. They are now redirects of about 1 KB to `transplant/<slug>/` (`#<question>` becomes `#meeting-<question>`, otherwise `#meeting`), and discussion links and search results open the meeting record on the pathway page (`#meeting-<question id>`). `transplant/high-risk/<slug>/` already redirected to `#high-risk`; it keeps the archived V7 review text for readers without JavaScript. `transplant/<slug>/clinical-roadmap.html` and `transplant/recording-templates/*` are older redirects and are not linked.
- **No repeated toolbar.** The decision tools ("Preview decisions", calculators, term buttons) live in the pathway's Tools column. The other views do not repeat that toolbar or the "Return to decision choices" bar; the tab bar returns to the pathway. On a phone the open section's tab is scrolled into view.
- Validate with `tools/test_link_tree.py` (one page per pathway, redirects, no links to retired copies, every internal link resolves) and the view checks in `tools/test_transplant_workspace_browser.py`.

## Tools & resources column (all roadmaps and transplant pathways)

- **One hierarchy on every decision** (`assets/tools-column.js/.css`, presentation only): Decision team · **Scores & classifications** · **Treatment resources** · **Decision references** · **Evidence & documents**. One row style for every disclosure (name + kind tag: Calculator, Scale, Classification, Criteria, Response criteria, Grade, Allocation, Guide, Reference), one link style (name, tag, →), one rule per section. A page's own quick reference is moved into the right section and keeps its text; content-hierarchy ownership (`tools/content_hierarchy.json`) is unchanged.
- **Scores & classifications** lists every score, grade, classification or criterion the decision names (`assets/clinical-scores.js`, `detect`), plus a few items central to a decision that its text does not name (`extra`). Calculators where a formula applies: MELD 3.0 (existing), Child-Pugh, ALBI, Milan/T2/UNOS downstaging/UCSF with tumor burden score, tumor burden score, Fong, Oslo, standardized FLR, WHO 2019 NET grade, Spigelman. Each definition cites its source; where the page already defines an item, its own text is shown and the calculator added beside it. Calculators store nothing and apply nothing. HEHE-LT is listed for counseling only, as the protocol states.
- **Decision-specific resource tools** (29 October 2026, Analyst A's approved inventory) join the same section; there is no second toolbar and no outcome-evidence panel. Placement is the `extra` map in `assets/clinical-scores.js`, keyed by roadmap slug or `T:<pathway>` and decision id; edit it there, not in page HTML.
  - Calculators: RECIST 1.1 and mRECIST response (nadir for progression, baseline for response), RETREAT, French AFP model, Metroticket 2.0 (no limit above AFP 1000; the 400 boundary is flagged). The treatment-interval, tumor-marker-trend and NELM cytoreduction calculators were removed at Hao's request (29 October 2026); do not re-add them.
  - Hepatic arterial infusion is not a standalone Tools resource on any roadmap or pathway (Hao, 29 October 2026). The roadmaps' HAI selection-and-evidence reference sits with the decisions it supports (kind `evidence` in `tools/content_hierarchy.json`).
  - Reference tables: MELD exception scores by diagnosis (the page's own diagnosis is highlighted), AFP rules for the HCC exception, CSPH (Baveno VII), ISGLS bile leak and bleeding, NETPET, liver-directed feasibility (built from `lrt/matrix.json` `contra`).
  - Shared roadmap tables (`shared: {slug, ...}`) load the roadmap's own quick reference from `<slug>/data/pathway.json` when the row is first opened. They are never copied, so the roadmap stays the single owner.
  - External tools open in a new tab: LiverVolume.com for liver volume and PVE growth (it replaces the earlier in-page FLR form; the Vauthey definition remains), the UCSF Liver Frailty Index and ACS NSQIP. No formula is reproduced for a tool whose source could not be checked.
  - A page's own references of kind criteria or tool sit in Scores & classifications, not Decision references.
- **Treatment resources**: the systemic and liver-directed matrices, deep-linked to the tumor, on every decision of a tumor that has them (systemic: HCC, pCCA, iCCA, CRLM, NELM, cHCC–CCA, PDAC, pNET, ampullary; liver-directed: HCC, pCCA, iCCA, CRLM, NELM); then the transplant treatment reference and treatment guides.
- **Folded at the bottom.** Transplant treatment decisions (HCC curative route, bridging/downstaging and waitlist; pCCA neoadjuvant; iCCA therapy and stability; CRLM unresectability and treatment; NELM options and exposure) end with the decision's evidence and both matrices, folded and loaded only when opened; other transplant decisions end with their evidence, folded. Each roadmap's liver-directed section is folded under "Treatment matrices" with the systemic matrix beside it.
- **Peers** read Indications · Cautions · Evidence; a caution inside its panel takes the panel's single rule. A guideline, policy or consensus chip that names its source carries no grey type label. Related-decision links read "If …" without a "When" label; their two explanations are one.
- Validate with `tools/test_clinical_scores.cjs`, `tools/test_tools_column_browser.py`, `tools/test_resource_tools_browser.py` (every placement, once, tagged, tables loading, phone width) and the tools checks in `tools/test_transplant_workspace_browser.py`.

- **One LRT dataset:** edit `lrt/matrix.json`, then run `python3 tools/build_lrt_data.py` and `python3 tools/test_shared_lrt_data.py`. The standalone page embeds that data; roadmap and pathway views fetch it directly. Four older `*/data/lrt_matrix.json` copies remain for historical source audits and must not be loaded into current pages.
- **Calculator uncertainty:** unanswered binary factors are unknown, never zero. Require differentiation for NET grade, retain unrounded sizes for eligibility comparisons, and identify estimated versus measured liver-volume denominators. Validate boundary and invalid-input cases whenever formulas or forms change.
