HCC-B5 · Previously treated extrahepatic disease

May a patient whose extrahepatic HCC (including proven lymph-node metastasis) was treated and has responded completely ever enter a transplant route, or does this history close transplantation?

Proposed position

Keep closed. A history of extrahepatic metastasis or proven nodal metastasis excludes the standard OPTN route and every HCC high-risk cohort, even after resection, ablation, radiotherapy or a complete response to immunotherapy. Record durable complete responders in a prospective registry; this edition offers no transplant route.

Proposed rules

  • “No extrahepatic disease” in HCC-B1 and HCC-B2 means no current or previous extrahepatic metastasis. Resection, ablation, radiotherapy or a complete radiological response does not clear this history.
  • Count proven lymph-node metastasis, including hilar or hepatoduodenal nodes, as extrahepatic disease. Enlarged hilar nodes without proof are HOLD for characterization. A lesion later proven benign is not prior extrahepatic disease; record how it was resolved.
  • Registry, not listing: enter durable complete responders with dated imaging, AFP and treatment history. Starting points for any future study (not active): ≥12 months without evidence of disease off systemic therapy; PET or tissue confirmation that every extrahepatic site is cleared; current whole-body staging including bone; an immunotherapy washout of ≥90 days and the HCC-B3 safety review.
  • Do not combine with the expanded-burden (HCC-B1) or portal-vein invasion (HCC-B2) cohorts.

Where this applies

  • HCC transplant pathway · high-risk review HCC-B5
  • Related question HCC-R1

Why

The guidelines describe current extrahepatic disease only. Excluding previously treated disease is this program’s inference, stated as such: no series reports transplantation after treated extrahepatic HCC, nodal disease at transplantation strongly predicts recurrence, and a complete response to immunotherapy is not cure.

HCC-R1 · Liver treatment after extrahepatic disease responds

After extrahepatic HCC responds to systemic therapy or is treated locally, when should resection or ablation of the liver tumor be offered?

Proposed position

Offer curative-intent liver resection or ablation, with treatment of every residual extrahepatic site, only to selected patients after MDT review, and present it as life-prolonging multimodal therapy, not cure. Use the JLCA definition of localized extrahepatic spread; all other extrahepatic disease stays systemic-therapy-led. This roadmap position does not open transplantation (HCC-B5).

Proposed rules

  • Localized extrahepatic spread (JLCA 2023, BR1): a solitary node at station 3, 8 or 12; localized peritoneal disease (P1); a unilateral adrenal metastasis; or a few lung metastases. All other extrahepatic disease is BR2: benefit of surgery uncertain.
  • Before surgery: response or stable disease of every extrahepatic site on serial imaging, no new lesions, current whole-body staging, every intrahepatic and extrahepatic site treatable (resection, ablation or stereotactic radiotherapy), adequate liver reserve, ECOG 0–1. No guideline sets the interval; the MDT records the interval it uses.
  • AASLD 2023 does not support systemic therapy before resection outside trials. Record the decision as an MDT exception or enroll in a study or registry; plan continued systemic therapy and surveillance.
  • Counsel that recurrence is the rule: after stereotactic radiotherapy to oligometastases, 2-year progression-free survival was 0%; after borderline-resectable surgery, 3-year recurrence-free survival was 19.7%.

Where this applies

  • HCC roadmap · C · Advanced If extrahepatic disease has responded or been treated and liver resection or ablation is considered
  • Related question HCC-B5

Why

National and multicentre cohorts associate surgery after response with longer survival, but every comparison is biased toward the operated patients. JLCA gives the only explicit definition of the limited extrahepatic disease for which surgery may help.

cHCC-CCA-R1 · Surgery or transplantation after nodal or extrahepatic disease

When combined HCC–cholangiocarcinoma has nodal or extrahepatic disease that responds, is resection or transplantation reasonable?

Proposed position

Resection with regional lymphadenectomy remains the curative option for organ-confined disease. After a response of regional nodal disease, decide case by case using the iCCA rules (iCCA-R1); distant extrahepatic disease stays systemic-therapy-led. Transplantation is closed after any nodal or extrahepatic disease, as in iCCA-B4.

Proposed rules

  • Regional nodal disease that responds: MDT case-by-case resection with lymphadenectomy; confirm response by restaging and tissue where feasible.
  • Distant extrahepatic disease: systemic therapy; no conversion pathway (no published data).
  • Transplantation: the OPTN/NLRB mixed-tumor route requires no extrahepatic disease; previous nodal or extrahepatic disease is excluded (iCCA-B4).

Where this applies

  • Combined HCC–CCA roadmap · 3 · Assess resection If nodal or extrahepatic disease has responded and resection is considered
  • Related question iCCA-B4

Why

In resected cHCC–CCA, nodal metastasis and local extrahepatic invasion carry large hazard ratios, and no study reports surgery after extrahepatic response. After transplantation, recurrence is frequent and mostly extrahepatic.

iCCA-B4 · Previously treated nodal or extrahepatic disease

Should a patient whose iCCA nodal or extrahepatic disease has responded or been resected ever be considered for transplantation?

Proposed position

Keep closed. Proven nodal metastasis or extrahepatic disease, current or previously treated (including resected), excludes the small-cirrhotic route and the advanced-iCCA study. Confirm node-negative status with tissue before listing, and characterize every indeterminate extrahepatic lesion. Applies also to mixed HCC–iCCA.

Proposed rules

  • Previously proven malignant nodes, even after radiological or pathological response, and any previous distant metastasis (peritoneum, lung, bone, distant nodes) are outside the initial iCCA routes. Resection of an extrahepatic site does not clear this history.
  • Suspicious or indeterminate nodes are HOLD until resolved with tissue; an imaging response is not nodal clearance.
  • Advanced-iCCA study: before listing, sample stations 8 and 12 surgically, plus any suspicious node (EUS-FNA or surgical). For living donation, complete this before listing, not only at the combined donor–recipient laparotomy. A positive result ends the route.
  • Indeterminate extrahepatic lesions (subcentimetre lung nodules, bone lesions) need biopsy or interval imaging; “stable on chemotherapy” is not clearance.
  • Previously node-positive disease that has cleared is a separate future research question; do not combine it with multifocal, G3 or living-donor expansions.

Where this applies

  • iCCA transplant pathway · high-risk review iCCA-B4
  • Related question cHCC-CCA-R1
  • Related question iCCA-R1

Why

Every iCCA transplant protocol excludes nodal and extrahepatic disease, and the Milan protocol excludes even resected extrahepatic spread. In the published series, early recurrences came from nodal disease missed by imaging or from indeterminate lesions accepted as stable.

iCCA-R1 · Surgery after nodal or extrahepatic disease responds

When regional nodes or distant metastases of iCCA respond to chemotherapy, is conversion resection reasonable?

Proposed position

Regional nodal disease (hepatoduodenal and common hepatic artery area) that responds may proceed to conversion resection with lymphadenectomy after MDT review. Nodes beyond the regional basin and distant metastases stay systemic-therapy-led; conversion surgery only exceptionally, on very low-certainty evidence, inside a registry or study.

Proposed rules

  • Regional nodes: restage after response; sample suspicious residual nodes where feasible, because residual nodal disease in the specimen is strongly adverse.
  • Distant disease (non-regional nodes, liver-only oligometastases, limited peritoneal or lung): only after durable response on serial imaging, negative whole-body restaging, staging laparoscopy with nodal sampling, and complete resectability of every site.
  • Counsel: recurrence about 70% after conversion; major complications about 61% and mortality about 9% in conversion series; survival benefit not proven.
  • Conversion does not open transplantation (iCCA-B4).

Where this applies

  • iCCA roadmap · 4 · Treat and reassess for resection If regional nodal disease has responded and resection is considered
  • iCCA roadmap · 6 · Distant metastatic disease If distant disease has responded durably and surgery is raised
  • Related question iCCA-B4

Why

The conversion evidence comes mostly from locally advanced disease. For metastatic iCCA it rests on very small, selected series, and unselected metastatic resection gives poor survival.

pCCA-B1 · Previously malignant nodes, peritoneal or distant disease

Should regional nodal disease that has responded, or any treated peritoneal or distant disease, ever allow transplantation for perihilar cholangiocarcinoma?

Proposed position

Keep closed. Nodal, peritoneal or distant disease found at any time, including at operative staging and after response to chemoradiation or chemotherapy, ends the transplant route; there is no re-entry after nodal response. The data on transplantation after nodal disease are absent rather than negative, and that is recorded.

Proposed rules

  • Before neoadjuvant chemoradiation: EUS-FNA of any suspicious regional node (not through the primary). A positive node ends the route.
  • Operative staging before transplantation with biopsy of hepatic artery and pericholedochal nodes and of any suspicious lesion.
  • Previously proven malignant nodes stay excluded even after radiographic or pathological clearance; distant or peritoneal disease at any time ends the route.

Where this applies

  • pCCA transplant pathway · high-risk review pCCA-B1
  • Related question pCCA-R1

Why

Nodal disease is the commonest finding that removes patients at staging, and survival after removal is short. One US centre accepted regional N1 disease and four patients were transplanted with metastases, without separable outcomes, so there are no usable data either way.

CRLM-B5 · Previously treated extrahepatic disease

Should a lung-only study stratum be opened for completely treated pulmonary metastases, and if so with which interval and biology limits? All other previously treated extrahepatic sites stay closed.

Proposed position

Keep previously treated extrahepatic disease closed in the CRLM transplant pathway. IHPBA Statement 9 excludes patients who initially present with or later develop extrahepatic metastases, and TransMet excluded previous or concomitant extrahepatic disease. Propose one narrow study stratum (not active) for limited, completely treated lung-only metastases; every other site stays closed.

Proposed rules

  • Default: extrahepatic metastasis after primary resection (including nodes outside the primary field), metastasis removed with the primary, and local relapse are outside the pathway, even after resection, ablation, radiotherapy or complete response. Response to systemic therapy alone never counts as clearance.
  • Proposed lung-only study stratum (needs its own protocol, IRB and a living-donor or individually reviewed allocation route): at most three lung metastases, each completely treated with curative intent, no thoracic nodal involvement, and no recurrence for ≥12 months after the last lung treatment (SECA-III uses 1 year since lung resection; the Japanese protocol uses >6 months recurrence-free). The meeting must choose the interval.
  • The stratum adds no other relaxation: every CRLM-B3 limit applies, and favorable biology is required (low Oslo or Fong score; low liver metabolic tumor volume). Chest CT and PET-CT within one month.
  • Hilar nodes: frozen section of hilar nodes before implantation, and before donor hepatectomy in living donation. A positive node ends the transplant.
  • Closed with no stratum: nodal disease outside the primary field; peritoneal, ovarian, adrenal, bone, brain or other non-lung sites; local relapse; more than three lung lesions; thoracic nodes; recurrence after lung treatment.

When colorectal cancer has extrahepatic disease that is treated or completely treatable, when should liver resection be offered?

Proposed position

Treated or completely treatable extrahepatic disease is not an absolute contraindication to curative-intent liver resection, but complete clearance of every site is mandatory and benefit depends on the site. Transplant pathway · HEHEOpen proposal · Defined, not activated

HEHE-B2 · Extrahepatic disease (EHD component)

Should a lung-only extrahepatic stratum be activated for hepatic EHE, given the conflict between the 2021 consensus (no transplantation with extrahepatic disease) and the registry data?

Proposed position

Start with liver-only disease and no macrovascular invasion. Keep the lung-only stratum defined but not activated: limited lung disease that is stable through an observation period or completely resected. Nodal (beyond hilar), pleural or other serosal, bone, peritoneal, progressive or symptomatic disease stays closed. The explant-based HEHE-LT score is for post-transplant surveillance, not selection.

Proposed rules

  • Lung-only stratum (not active): asymptomatic, no pleural involvement, stable on serial imaging for ≥6 months or completely resected or ablated with no recurrence for ≥6 months; independent expert review and a written comparison with observation or systemic therapy.
  • A waiting time longer than 120 days is a usable pre-transplant test of time; shorter waits were associated with recurrence.
  • Hilar nodal involvement is a recurrence risk factor, not an extrahepatic exclusion; plan hilar lymphadenectomy. Nodes beyond the hilum are extrahepatic disease and are closed.
  • Record the guideline–registry disagreement in every case.

Where this applies

  • HEHE transplant pathway · high-risk review HEHE-B2

Why

The registry finding that extrahepatic disease was not adverse is an absence of evidence: it was mostly untreated lung disease and the hazard ratio was 1.5 with a wide interval. The consensus exclusion is expert opinion.

Cancer roadmap · PDACOpen proposal · Trial or registry first

PDAC-R1 · Surgery after oligometastatic disease responds

When synchronous oligometastatic PDAC responds to chemotherapy, or isolated lung metastases appear late, should surgery be offered outside a trial?

Proposed position

Distant metastasis stays systemic-therapy-led. Response-selected conversion surgery for limited liver metastases is offered preferably within a trial (METAPANC, CSPAC-1) or a registry after MDT review. Metachronous isolated lung metastases after a long disease-free interval may be resected. There is no PDAC transplant pathway.

Proposed rules

  • Synchronous liver oligometastases: ≤3 liver metastases (OligoPanc working definition, relative-majority only; METAPANC ≤3 liver-only), no peritoneal or other extrahepatic disease, induction chemotherapy without progression (METAPANC: 8 cycles of mFOLFIRINOX; Heidelberg: usually ≥6 cycles with RECIST response of metastases and a marker fall), CA19-9 fall >50% to <500 U/mL (CSPAC-1), resectable primary and metastases.
  • Counsel: in Heidelberg, 46% were unresectable at exploration and only about 1 in 4 explored was ypM0; median survival 25.5 months (ypM0) vs 10.7 (ypM1) vs 8.1 (exploration only). Surgery ≥9 months after diagnosis did worse.
  • Metachronous isolated lung metastases: ≤3 (ideally solitary), unilateral, disease-free interval of about 2 years (17–28 months cited), after systemic therapy and MDT review.
  • Para-aortic (16b1) nodes known before surgery are systemic disease and outside the OligoPanc definition; when found at operation, do not abort resection on 16b1 alone.

Where this applies

  • PDAC roadmap · 8 · Metastatic disease: systemic treatment If limited metastases respond to chemotherapy, or isolated lung metastases appear late

Why

Survival after resection is concentrated in complete responders, and unselected resection of metastases does not help. Trial rules give the clearest selection thresholds.

AMP-R1 · Local treatment after metastatic disease responds

When metastatic ampullary adenocarcinoma responds to systemic therapy, should limited liver or lung metastases be treated locally?

Proposed position

Systemic therapy is the default for metastatic ampullary adenocarcinoma. Local therapy or surgery for limited liver or lung metastases that respond durably is a case-by-case MDT decision, extrapolated from PDAC and biliary data; there is no ampullary-specific evidence after response.

Proposed rules

  • Apply the PDAC-R1 selection rules by analogy (limited sites, no progression, marker fall, complete resectability).
  • Isolated late lung recurrence has a better prognosis than synchronous or liver metastases.
  • Station 16b1 involvement found at upfront surgery is prognostically poor but is not by itself a reason to abort resection.
