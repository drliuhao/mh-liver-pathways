Treatment options and evidence

OS = overall survival; PFS = progression-free survival; ORR = objective response rate; HR = hazard ratio; SSTR = somatostatin receptor; ICI = immune checkpoint inhibitor.

HCCPreferred first-line option

Atezolizumab + bevacizumab

When to consider. Unresectable HCC requiring systemic treatment; strongest evidence in Child-Pugh A, ECOG 0–1. Assess and treat high-risk varices before bevacizumab.

Clinical evidence. IMbrave150: updated median overall survival 19.2 versus 13.4 months with sorafenib; HR 0.66.

Limits of the evidence. Bleeding, portal hypertension, blood pressure, proteinuria and immune toxicity affect selection. Do not compare its survival median directly with a different trial.

Transplant implications. Checkpoint therapy can affect subsequent transplant timing and rejection risk. Discuss transplant intent before treatment.

HCCPreferred first-line option

Durvalumab + tremelimumab (STRIDE)

When to consider. Unresectable HCC, preserved liver function and fitness for dual checkpoint therapy. An option when bevacizumab is unsuitable and immunotherapy is appropriate.

Clinical evidence. HIMALAYA: median overall survival 16.43 versus 13.77 months with sorafenib; HR 0.78. Progression-free survival was not significantly improved.

Limits of the evidence. Main portal-vein invasion was excluded from the pivotal trial. Immune toxicity remains important; avoiding bevacizumab does not remove treatment risk.

Transplant implications. Requires a transplant-specific plan if transplantation may later be considered.

HCCPreferred first-line option

Nivolumab + ipilimumab

When to consider. Unresectable HCC; select patients able to tolerate dual checkpoint therapy and close early monitoring.

Clinical evidence. CheckMate 9DW: median overall survival 23.7 versus 20.6 months with lenvatinib or sorafenib; HR 0.79.

Limits of the evidence. Survival curves crossed: mortality was higher during the first six months (HR 1.65). Grade 3–4 treatment-related events occurred in 41% versus 42%; treatment-related deaths were 12 versus 3.

Transplant implications. Review future transplant intent and immune adverse effects before choosing this regimen.

HCCSelected alternative

Single-agent checkpoint therapy

When to consider. Tislelizumab or durvalumab when a single-agent approach is appropriate. NCCN HCC v2.2026 also lists pembrolizumab first line, category 2B.

Clinical evidence. RATIONALE-301 established overall-survival noninferiority of tislelizumab to sorafenib. HIMALAYA established noninferiority of durvalumab. KEYNOTE-394 supported later-line pembrolizumab in an Asian population; KEYNOTE-240 did not meet its prespecified statistical thresholds.

Limits of the evidence. These agents do not have identical evidence or regulatory indications. Check the current agent-specific label; a later-line study does not establish first-line superiority.

Transplant implications. Single-agent checkpoint therapy still carries transplant rejection concerns.

HCCAlternative when immunotherapy is unsuitable

Lenvatinib or sorafenib

When to consider. Unresectable HCC with adequate liver reserve; consider when checkpoint therapy is contraindicated or inconsistent with the transplant plan.

Clinical evidence. REFLECT: lenvatinib was noninferior to sorafenib for overall survival (13.6 versus 12.3 months). SHARP established sorafenib benefit over placebo (10.7 versus 7.9 months).

Limits of the evidence. REFLECT excluded main portal-vein invasion, ≥50% liver occupation and bile-duct invasion. Dose tolerance and hepatic function matter; trial medians are not individual predictions.

Transplant implications. Coordinate perioperative treatment interruption and liver reserve with the transplant team.

HCCSubsequent treatment

Cabozantinib, regorafenib or AFP-selected ramucirumab

When to consider. After progression or intolerance, choose an unused agent according to prior exposure and hepatic reserve. Ramucirumab requires AFP ≥400 ng/mL; RESORCE required prior sorafenib tolerance.

Clinical evidence. Placebo-controlled overall-survival benefit: CELESTIAL HR 0.76; RESORCE HR 0.63; REACH-2 HR 0.71.

Limits of the evidence. These pivotal trials largely followed sorafenib. The best sequence after modern first-line immunotherapy has not been established by these trials.

Transplant implications. A treatment response does not itself restore transplant or MELD-exception eligibility.

HCCSurveillance; avoid routine adjuvant atezolizumab–bevacizumab

After curative resection or ablation

When to consider. HCC with no residual disease after resection or ablation.

Clinical evidence. IMbrave050 initially reported a recurrence-free survival advantage. The 2026 update did not sustain that result: RFS HR 0.90 (95% CI 0.72–1.12); overall survival remained immature.

Limits of the evidence. The updated evidence does not support routine adjuvant atezolizumab–bevacizumab. Trial participation remains an option.

Transplant implications. Continue surveillance and reassess recurrent disease through the standard pathway.

HCCSelected multidisciplinary or trial consideration

Systemic therapy combined with TACE

When to consider. Embolization-eligible unresectable HCC with suitable liver reserve; review the specific regimen, current label and guideline position.

Clinical evidence. EMERALD-1, LEAP-012 and EMERALD-3 reported progression-free survival benefit for their respective combinations. In EMERALD-3, STRIDE + lenvatinib + TACE yielded PFS 13.0 versus 9.8 months; overall-survival superiority was not established at the reported analysis.

Limits of the evidence. Regimens, comparators and follow-up differ. PFS benefit does not establish cure, transplant benefit or interchangeability. Combined treatment can increase serious toxicity.

Transplant implications. These trials do not validate routine checkpoint therapy as a transplant bridge. Review the transplant plan before treatment.

pCCA / iCCAPreferred first-line options

Gemcitabine + cisplatin + durvalumab or pembrolizumab

When to consider. Unresectable or metastatic biliary cancer after addressing biliary obstruction, infection, renal function and performance status.

Clinical evidence. TOPAZ-1: overall-survival HR 0.80 for durvalumab + chemotherapy versus chemotherapy. KEYNOTE-966: median overall survival 12.7 versus 10.9 months, HR 0.83, with pembrolizumab added.

Limits of the evidence. Mixed biliary-tract populations; neither trial establishes a separate pCCA-specific effect or superiority between these two checkpoint regimens.

Transplant implications. For potentially transplant-eligible pCCA or iCCA, obtain transplant-program review first. Palliative chemoimmunotherapy is not an interchangeable transplant neoadjuvant protocol.

pCCA / iCCAWhen checkpoint therapy is unsuitable

Gemcitabine + cisplatin; adapt intensity when necessary

When to consider. Use chemotherapy alone when immunotherapy is unsuitable. Renal impairment, neuropathy, hearing loss or frailty may require a different platinum, regimen or single agent after oncology review.

Clinical evidence. ABC-02 established cisplatin–gemcitabine over gemcitabine: median overall survival 11.7 versus 8.1 months; HR 0.64.

Limits of the evidence. Alternative platinum substitutions and dose reductions do not reproduce the exact ABC-02 evidence. Treat infection and improve drainage before reassessing fitness.

Transplant implications. An approved transplant regimen may use chemotherapy differently; confirm the protocol before starting treatment.

pCCA / iCCAEvidence-based subsequent option

FOLFOX after gemcitabine–cisplatin

When to consider. Progression after gemcitabine–cisplatin, with adequate function and performance status; obtain molecular results before choosing the next treatment.

Clinical evidence. ABC-06: FOLFOX + active symptom control versus active symptom control alone; median overall survival 6.2 versus 5.3 months, HR 0.69.

Limits of the evidence. Benefit was modest and toxicity substantial, including three treatment-related deaths. The trial preceded routine first-line chemoimmunotherapy.

Transplant implications. Persistent progression calls for reassessment of transplant candidacy and treatment goals.

pCCA / iCCAUsual adjuvant discussion

Capecitabine after resection

When to consider. Resected biliary cancer after recovery; discuss approximately six months of adjuvant treatment and margin-specific radiation considerations.

Clinical evidence. BILCAP: primary intention-to-treat overall-survival comparison was not significant (HR 0.81; p=0.097); prespecified sensitivity and per-protocol analyses favored capecitabine. ASCOT supported S-1 in a Japanese population.

Limits of the evidence. Do not describe BILCAP as an unequivocally positive primary-endpoint trial. PRODIGE-12 and BCAT did not establish adjuvant GEMOX or gemcitabine benefit. S-1 applicability and access differ by region.

Transplant implications. Adjuvant treatment after resection and neoadjuvant treatment before transplant are separate settings.

iCCA / pCCAPreviously treated, biomarker-selected CCA

FGFR2 fusion/rearrangement-directed therapy

When to consider. Confirm an actionable FGFR2 fusion or rearrangement. Pemigatinib and lirafugratinib have CCA indications; futibatinib evidence and indication are specific to iCCA. Most evidence comes from iCCA.

Clinical evidence. FIGHT-202 final: pemigatinib ORR 37.0%. FOENIX-CCA2: futibatinib ORR 42%. FDA approval of lirafugratinib in September 2026 was based on REFOCUS: ORR 46%, median response duration 11.8 months.

Limits of the evidence. Single-arm response studies do not establish a survival advantage or the preferred FGFR inhibitor. REFOCUS efficacy cohort was FGFR-inhibitor-naïve; sequence after an FGFR inhibitor needs separate evidence.

Transplant implications. Response may prompt reassessment of resectability or a study, but does not establish transplant eligibility.

iCCA / pCCAPreviously treated, biomarker-selected disease

Ivosidenib for IDH1-mutant CCA

When to consider. Confirmed susceptible IDH1 mutation after previous treatment; most enrolled tumors were intrahepatic.

Clinical evidence. ClarIDHy: median PFS 2.7 versus 1.4 months, HR 0.37. Final unadjusted overall survival was 10.3 versus 7.5 months, HR 0.79, with extensive crossover.

Limits of the evidence. Primarily a disease-control strategy. Crossover-adjusted survival analyses require modeling assumptions; do not present them as the unadjusted randomized result.

Transplant implications. Continue multidisciplinary reassessment; a molecular response is not a transplant selection rule.

pCCA / iCCASelected molecular options after prior treatment

HER2- or BRAF-directed treatment

When to consider. For HER2-positive disease, use the exact assay/label: zanidatamab requires IHC 3+ for its US indication. For BRAF V600E, consider dabrafenib + trametinib. Other HER2 options require agent-specific review.

Clinical evidence. HERIZON-BTC-01 supports zanidatamab activity in previously treated HER2-positive biliary cancer. ROAR: investigator-assessed ORR 51% with dabrafenib + trametinib in 43 BRAF V600E-mutant biliary cancers.

Limits of the evidence. Single-arm cohorts; do not infer superiority over chemotherapy or assume all HER2 assay categories have the same activity. Confirm the current indication, cardiac status and relevant toxicities.

Transplant implications. Treat the confirmed molecular alteration while reviewing the separate surgical and transplant options.

pCCATransplant-team decision before treatment

Protocol-directed chemoradiation before transplant

When to consider. Patients meeting the center’s pCCA transplant protocol criteria. Protect diagnostic routes and confirm staging before beginning a palliative regimen.

Clinical evidence. The established transplant strategy combines protocol-directed neoadjuvant treatment, operative staging and transplantation. The V7 review keeps alternative chemotherapy or SBRT regimens as separate proposals.

Limits of the evidence. Gemcitabine–cisplatin ± checkpoint therapy is not automatically a replacement for the established transplant regimen. Expanded size, biopsy-history and prior-node strata remain closed in the V7 proposal.

Transplant implications. Open the standard pCCA pathway; unresolved regimen questions go to the high-risk review.

      FOLFOX / CAPEOX or FOLFIRI + selected biologic

      When to consider. MSS/pMMR disease: use primary sidedness, RAS/BRAF status, fitness and the chance of conversion to resection. Left-sided RAS/BRAF wild-type tumors may favor an anti-EGFR doublet; bevacizumab-based therapy is another established approach.

      CRLMFor fit patients with initially unresectable liver disease

      Selected triplet chemotherapy for conversion

      When to consider. A more intensive regimen may be appropriate when substantial shrinkage could permit complete local treatment. Review by an experienced liver multidisciplinary team is essential.

      Clinical evidence. CAIRO5: FOLFOXIRI–bevacizumab improved PFS over doublet–bevacizumab in its right-sided or RAS/BRAF-mutant group (10.6 versus 9.0 months; HR 0.76), with greater toxicity. TRICE studied cetuximab with triplet versus doublet chemotherapy in RAS/BRAF-wild-type CRLM.

      Limits of the evidence. Do not prescribe triplet therapy for every patient or extrapolate one molecular/sidedness group to another. Response, conversion and survival are different endpoints.

      Transplant implications. Confirm persistent unresectability after reviewing resection, ablation and selected HAI conversion options before transplant review.

      CRLMMSI-H / dMMR metastatic colorectal cancer

      Pembrolizumab or nivolumab + ipilimumab

      When to consider. Confirm mismatch-repair deficiency or microsatellite instability before treatment.

      Clinical evidence. KEYNOTE-177: pembrolizumab PFS 16.5 versus 8.2 months with chemotherapy. CheckMate 8HW established first-line PFS benefit of nivolumab–ipilimumab versus chemotherapy; its separate comparison versus nivolumab alone also favored the combination (HR 0.62).

      Transplant implications. Effective immunotherapy is a major competing option. MSI-H/dMMR expansion is closed in the initial V7 CRLM transplant proposal.

      CRLMBRAF V600E-mutant metastatic disease

      Encorafenib + cetuximab + fluorouracil-based chemotherapy

      When to consider. Confirm BRAF V600E. BREAKWATER supports first-line encorafenib–cetuximab with chemotherapy; the February 2026 FDA indication includes mFOLFOX6 or FOLFIRI.

      Clinical evidence. BREAKWATER: encorafenib–cetuximab + mFOLFOX6 improved PFS (12.8 versus 7.1 months) and OS (30.3 versus 15.1 months) over standard care. BEACON established encorafenib–cetuximab-based treatment after prior therapy.

      Limits of the evidence. A first-line chemotherapy-containing regimen and later-line targeted doublet are different settings. Do not switch between them without considering prior treatment.

        Perioperative chemotherapy around liver resection
