Initial advanced-disease treatment

Atezolizumab + bevacizumab

HCC
Preferred first-line option

When to consider. Unresectable HCC requiring systemic treatment; strongest evidence in Child-Pugh A, ECOG 0–1. Assess and treat high-risk varices before bevacizumab.

Clinical evidence.
  • IMbrave150: updated median overall survival 19.2 versus 13.4 months with sorafenib; HR 0.66.
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. Bleeding, portal hypertension, blood pressure, proteinuria and immune toxicity affect selection. Do not compare its survival median directly with a different trial.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. Checkpoint therapy can affect subsequent transplant timing and rejection risk. Discuss transplant intent before treatment.

Durvalumab + tremelimumab (STRIDE)

HCC
Preferred first-line option

When to consider. Unresectable HCC, preserved liver function and fitness for dual checkpoint therapy. An option when bevacizumab is unsuitable and immunotherapy is appropriate.

Clinical evidence.
  • HIMALAYA: median overall survival 16.43 versus 13.77 months with sorafenib; HR 0.78. Progression-free survival was not significantly improved.
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. Main portal-vein invasion was excluded from the pivotal trial. Immune toxicity remains important; avoiding bevacizumab does not remove treatment risk.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. Requires a transplant-specific plan if transplantation may later be considered.

Nivolumab + ipilimumab

HCC
Preferred first-line option

When to consider. Unresectable HCC; select patients able to tolerate dual checkpoint therapy and close early monitoring.

Clinical evidence.
  • CheckMate 9DW: median overall survival 23.7 versus 20.6 months with lenvatinib or sorafenib; HR 0.79.
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. Survival curves crossed: mortality was higher during the first six months (HR 1.65). Grade 3–4 treatment-related events occurred in 41% versus 42%; treatment-related deaths were 12 versus 3.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. Review future transplant intent and immune adverse effects before choosing this regimen.

Single-agent checkpoint therapy

HCC
Selected alternative

When to consider. Tislelizumab or durvalumab when a single-agent approach is appropriate. NCCN HCC v2.2026 also lists pembrolizumab first line, category 2B.

Clinical evidence.
  • RATIONALE-301 established overall-survival noninferiority of tislelizumab to sorafenib.
  • HIMALAYA established noninferiority of durvalumab.
  • KEYNOTE-394 supported later-line pembrolizumab in an Asian population;
  • KEYNOTE-240 did not meet its prespecified statistical thresholds.
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. These agents do not have identical evidence or regulatory indications. Check the current agent-specific label; a later-line study does not establish first-line superiority.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. Single-agent checkpoint therapy still carries transplant rejection concerns.

Lenvatinib or sorafenib

HCC
Alternative when immunotherapy is unsuitable

When to consider. Unresectable HCC with adequate liver reserve; consider when checkpoint therapy is contraindicated or inconsistent with the transplant plan.

Clinical evidence.
  • REFLECT: lenvatinib was noninferior to sorafenib for overall survival (13.6 versus 12.3 months).
  • SHARP established sorafenib benefit over placebo (10.7 versus 7.9 months). Median OS 10.7 versus 7.9 months; HR 0.69 (0.55–0.87). Radiologic time to progression 5.5 versus 2.8 months, HR 0.58; this is not PFS. ORR 2% versus 1%, no complete responses.
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. REFLECT excluded main portal-vein invasion, ≥50% liver occupation and bile-duct invasion. Dose tolerance and hepatic function matter; trial medians are not individual predictions. Historical placebo comparison, not a comparison with current ICI combinations. Grade 3 diarrhea and hand–foot reaction each 8%; dose interruptions/reductions frequent. Little evidence for decompensated cirrhosis.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. Coordinate perioperative treatment interruption and liver reserve with the transplant team.

Gemcitabine + cisplatin + durvalumab or pembrolizumab

pCCA / iCCA
Preferred first-line options

When to consider. Unresectable or metastatic biliary cancer after addressing biliary obstruction, infection, renal function and performance status.

Clinical evidence.
  • TOPAZ-1: overall-survival HR 0.80 for durvalumab + chemotherapy versus chemotherapy.
  • KEYNOTE-966: median overall survival 12.7 versus 10.9 months, HR 0.83, with pembrolizumab added.
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. Mixed biliary-tract populations; neither trial establishes a separate pCCA-specific effect or superiority between these two checkpoint regimens. ASCO 2026.1.0 is an advanced-BTC systemic guideline, not a transplant-neoadjuvant or eligibility guideline.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. For potentially transplant-eligible pCCA or iCCA, obtain transplant-program review first. Palliative chemoimmunotherapy is not an interchangeable transplant neoadjuvant protocol.

Gemcitabine + cisplatin; adapt intensity when necessary

pCCA / iCCA
When checkpoint therapy is unsuitable

When to consider. Use chemotherapy alone when immunotherapy is unsuitable. Renal impairment, neuropathy, hearing loss or frailty may require a different platinum, regimen or single agent after oncology review.

Clinical evidence.
  • ABC-02 established cisplatin–gemcitabine over gemcitabine: median overall survival 11.7 versus 8.1 months; HR 0.64.
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. Alternative platinum substitutions and dose reductions do not reproduce the exact ABC-02 evidence. Treat infection and improve drainage before reassessing fitness. ASCO 2026.1.0 is an advanced-BTC systemic guideline, not a transplant-neoadjuvant or eligibility guideline.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. An approved transplant regimen may use chemotherapy differently; confirm the protocol before starting treatment.

FOLFOX / CAPEOX or FOLFIRI + selected biologic

CRLM
First-line metastatic treatment

When to consider. MSS/pMMR disease: use primary sidedness, RAS/BRAF status, fitness and the chance of conversion to resection. Left-sided RAS/BRAF wild-type tumors may favor an anti-EGFR doublet; bevacizumab-based therapy is another established approach.

Clinical evidence.
  • PARADIGM: left-sided RAS-wild-type disease had median OS 37.9 versus 34.3 months with panitumumab versus bevacizumab, both with mFOLFOX6.
  • FIRE-3 and CALGB/SWOG 80405 provide complementary, differently designed comparisons.
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. Anti-EGFR selection depends on molecular status and sidedness. Do not generalize a response-rate advantage to all primary sites or automatically add cetuximab around resection.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. Map lesions before treatment and reassess resectability repeatedly. Systemic response alone does not establish a transplant indication.

Octreotide LAR or lanreotide

NELM / pNET
Indolent, well-differentiated, usually SSTR-positive NET

When to consider. Choose according to primary site, somatostatin-receptor status, growth and hormone symptoms. Selected asymptomatic, low-burden stable disease may be observed.

Clinical evidence.
  • PROMID: time to progression 14.3 versus 6 months with octreotide versus placebo in midgut NET.
  • CLARINET established PFS benefit with lanreotide in nonfunctional enteropancreatic NET, Ki-67 <10%.
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. These trials chiefly demonstrate tumor control, not cure or a proven overall-survival advantage. Functional tumors may need ongoing hormonal treatment even when another antitumor treatment is added.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. Stable low-burden disease may have greater benefit from continued nontransplant care than immediate transplantation.

First-line PRRT for selected higher-grade NET

NELM / pNET
Well-differentiated G2/G3, SSTR-positive GEP-NET

When to consider. NETTER-2 population: newly diagnosed advanced, well-differentiated GEP-NET, Ki-67 10–55%, with suitable somatostatin-receptor expression.

Clinical evidence.
  • NETTER-2: median PFS 22.8 versus 8.5 months with 177Lu-DOTATATE + octreotide versus high-dose octreotide; HR 0.276.
  • The 2026 subgroup report supports activity across pancreatic/GI origin and G2/G3 subsets.
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. Comparator was high-dose octreotide, not CAPTEM or another chemotherapy regimen. This does not apply to poorly differentiated NEC.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. G3 treatment activity does not make G3 disease eligible for the initial V7 NET transplant cohort.

Capecitabine + temozolomide (CAPTEM)

NELM / pNET
Particularly for pancreatic NET when shrinkage matters

When to consider. Advanced well-differentiated pancreatic NET, especially progressive, bulky or symptomatic disease. Decide sequence alongside PRRT and targeted agents.

Clinical evidence.
  • E2211 randomized phase II: median PFS 22.7 versus 14.4 months with CAPTEM versus temozolomide at the scheduled interim analysis; final OS was not significantly different.
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. Do not generalize pancreatic NET data to all small-bowel NET or poorly differentiated NEC. Review marrow reserve, infection risk and fluoropyrimidine safety.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. Tumor control can inform a transplant assessment, but selection still requires favorable grade, distribution and expected benefit.

Platinum + etoposide or irinotecan for poorly differentiated NEC

NELM / pNET
A separate disease pathway

When to consider. Digestive-system poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinoma requires pathology confirmation and a NEC treatment plan. It is different from well-differentiated G3 NET.

Clinical evidence.
  • TOPIC-NEC: cisplatin–etoposide versus cisplatin–irinotecan did not differ significantly in OS (12.5 versus 10.9 months; HR 1.04).
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. Toxicity differed, including more myelosuppression with etoposide. Do not apply SSA/PRRT evidence from well-differentiated NET automatically to NEC.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. Poorly differentiated NEC is outside the NET transplant pathway.

PDAC
Setting-specific MDT option

Limits of the evidence. Evidence and label requirements vary by regimen and setting; confirm the source and pharmacy plan.

Combined HCC CCA — Advanced disease

cHCC–CCA
Setting-specific MDT option

When to consider. Expert pathology and molecular review first. Gemcitabine-platinum is commonly considered; adding an ICI, or choosing an HCC-directed regimen, needs an explicit extrapolation rationale and transplant-intent safety review. Prefer a suitable trial. Supports cautious platinum-based treatment discussion and expert pathology/molecular review, not a definitive superiority claim or transplant-selection benefit. Adding ICI is extrapolation and requires LT-intent safety review.

Clinical evidence.
  • Overall OS 8.9 months, PFS 2.8 months, ORR 5.6%. Pooled platinum regimens had longer observed OS than sorafenib; adjusted sorafenib-versus-platinum mortality HR 15.83 (2.25–111.43). All seven sorafenib exposures including later line had progression.
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. Tiny nonrandomized groups, major confounding and very wide CI. Abstract and body reverse individual GemCis/5-FU-cisplatin median ordering; do not present those medians as settled. Old pathology classification; no modern ICI comparison.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. Combined HCC-CCA is not interchangeable with pure HCC or pure BTC. Regimen borrowing and any transplant proposal require explicit multidisciplinary rationale; these data do not validate a new LT cutoff.

Molecularly selected treatment

FGFR2 fusion/rearrangement-directed therapy

iCCA / pCCA
Previously treated, biomarker-selected CCA

When to consider. Confirm an actionable FGFR2 fusion or rearrangement. Pemigatinib and lirafugratinib have CCA indications; futibatinib evidence and indication are specific to iCCA. Most evidence comes from iCCA.

Clinical evidence.
  • FIGHT-202 final: pemigatinib ORR 37.0%.
  • FOENIX-CCA2: futibatinib ORR 42%.
  • FDA approval of lirafugratinib in September 2026 was based on REFOCUS: ORR 46%, median response duration 11.8 months.
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. Single-arm response studies do not establish a survival advantage or the preferred FGFR inhibitor. REFOCUS efficacy cohort was FGFR-inhibitor-naïve; sequence after an FGFR inhibitor needs separate evidence. ASCO 2026.1.0 is an advanced-BTC systemic guideline, not a transplant-neoadjuvant or eligibility guideline.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. Response may prompt reassessment of resectability or a study, but does not establish transplant eligibility.

HER2- or BRAF-directed treatment

pCCA / iCCA
Selected molecular options after prior treatment

When to consider. For HER2-positive disease, use the exact assay/label: zanidatamab requires IHC 3+ for its US indication. For BRAF V600E, consider dabrafenib + trametinib. Other HER2 options require agent-specific review.

Clinical evidence.
  • HERIZON-BTC-01 supports zanidatamab activity in previously treated HER2-positive biliary cancer.

Limits of the evidence. Single-arm cohorts; do not infer superiority over chemotherapy or assume all HER2 assay categories have the same activity. Transplant implications. Treat the confirmed molecular alteration while reviewing the separate surgical and transplant options.

Encorafenib + cetuximab + fluorouracil-based chemotherapy

CRLM
BRAF V600E-mutant metastatic disease

When to consider. Confirm BRAF V600E. BREAKWATER supports first-line encorafenib–cetuximab with chemotherapy; the February 2026 FDA indication includes mFOLFOX6 or FOLFIRI.

Clinical evidence.
  • BREAKWATER: encorafenib–cetuximab + mFOLFOX6 improved PFS (12.8 versus 7.1 months) and OS (30.3 versus 15.1 months) over standard care.
  • BEACON established encorafenib–cetuximab-based treatment after prior therapy.
Limits of the evidence

Limits of the evidence. A first-line chemotherapy-containing regimen and later-line targeted doublet are different settings. Do not switch between them without considering prior treatment.

Transplant implications

Transplant implications. BRAF-mutant expansion remains closed in the initial V7 CRLM transplant proposal; response does not remove that boundary.

    • CAIRO5: FOLFOXIRI–bevacizumab improved PFS over doublet–bevacizumab in its right-sided or RAS/BRAF-mutant group (10.6 versus 9.0 months; HR 0.76), with greater toxicity.
    • TRICE studied cetuximab with triplet versus doublet chemotherapy in RAS/BRAF-wild-type CRLM.
    Limits of the evidence

    Limits of the evidence. Do not prescribe triplet therapy for every patient or extrapolate one molecular/sidedness group to another. Response, conversion and survival are different endpoints.

    Transplant implications

    Transplant implications. Confirm persistent unresectability after reviewing resection, ablation and selected HAI conversion options before transplant review.

        Safety and transplant context

        Choose a drug class to review reasons to avoid, delay or seek specialist input. Regimen-specific transplant implications remain inside each treatment option.

        Drug-class precautions

        Shared safety considerations across tumor types. Confirm the current product label, laboratory thresholds, interactions and perioperative timing for the selected regimen. An evidence gap in severe liver dysfunction is not equivalent to a formal label contraindication.

        Checkpoint inhibitors

        Reasons to avoid, delay or obtain specialist review

        Review solid-organ transplant history, unresolved immune toxicity, significant autoimmune disease, immunosuppression and infection. These are different clinical situations; obtain oncology/transplant input rather than treating them as one blanket label contraindication.

        Practical implications and sources

        After liver transplantation, rejection and graft loss are major concerns. Before transplantation, no washout interval guarantees safety.

        HCC immunotherapy / transplant review

        VEGF antibodies and VEGFR inhibitors

        Reasons to avoid, delay or obtain specialist review

        Avoid or delay when uncontrolled serious bleeding, unhealed major wounds, uncontrolled hypertension or relevant vascular complications make treatment unsafe. Check proteinuria and agent-specific hepatic/cardiac risks.

        Practical implications and sources

        Coordinate surgery and agent-specific interruption intervals. HCC with portal hypertension needs particular attention to varices.

        Fluorouracil / capecitabine

        Reasons to avoid, delay or obtain specialist review

        FDA labeling recommends DPYD testing before treatment unless immediate treatment is necessary. Avoid in known complete DPD deficiency; individualize treatment in partial deficiency. Review renal function for capecitabine, marrow reserve, severe diarrhea/mucositis and prior cardiac toxicity.

        Practical implications and sources

        Relevant to FOLFOX, FOLFIRI, CAPEOX, CAPTEM and fluoropyrimidine chemoradiation.

        FDA · DPYD safety labeling, 2026

        Platinum agents / irinotecan / cytotoxic combinations

        Reasons to avoid, delay or obtain specialist review

        Correct active infection and major organ dysfunction before treatment. Cisplatin: renal function, hearing and neuropathy. Oxaliplatin: neuropathy and hepatic sinusoidal injury. Irinotecan: bilirubin, severe diarrhea and UGT1A1-related toxicity risk.

        Practical implications and sources

        Review cumulative chemotherapy-associated liver injury and functional remnant before liver surgery.

        Biomarker-directed agents

        Reasons to avoid, delay or obtain specialist review

        Confirm the exact alteration and assay required for the agent. FGFR inhibitors: eye examination and phosphate monitoring. HER2 agents: cardiac assessment; trastuzumab deruxtecan also requires pneumonitis vigilance. Ivosidenib: QT interval and interactions.

        Practical implications and sources

        A negative or nonspecific molecular result is not an indication for empirical targeted treatment.

        177Lu-DOTATATE PRRT

        Reasons to avoid, delay or obtain specialist review

        Requires suitable somatostatin-receptor expression, renal function and marrow reserve. Review pregnancy, prior marrow-toxic treatment, blood counts and the ability to receive renal-protective amino acids.

        Practical implications and sources

        Assess cumulative radiation and marrow toxicity when sequencing with alkylating chemotherapy.

        Everolimus / somatostatin analogues / temozolomide

        Reasons to avoid, delay or obtain specialist review

        Everolimus: infection, pneumonitis, glucose control and interactions. Somatostatin analogues: gallbladder, glucose and bradycardia concerns. Temozolomide/CAPTEM: marrow suppression, infection risk and cumulative exposure.

        Practical implications and sources

        Choose according to tumor grade, primary site, hormonal symptoms and growth rate.

        Complete the molecular review

        For advanced biliary and colorectal cancers, obtain an adequate molecular profile early enough to affect treatment. Review MSI/MMR, relevant fusions and other actionable alterations using the disease-specific guideline and current drug indication. A rare alteration may support a tumor-agnostic therapy even when it is not represented by a separate card.

        When further tumor-directed treatment is unsuitable

        Agree best supportive care with the patient and treating team. Treat pain, nausea, pruritus, ascites, obstruction, infection, malnutrition and other burdens; involve palliative care, and discuss hospice when appropriate. Palliative care can also accompany active anticancer treatment.

        Reassess reversible causes of poor performance or organ function before concluding that further treatment is unsuitable. Record the agreed plan and a point of reassessment. NCI · Palliative care.
